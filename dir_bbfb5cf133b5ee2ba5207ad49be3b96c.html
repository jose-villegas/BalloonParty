Detailed Description

The runtime half of the shot solver (see the authoritative rule-mirroring/approximations doc in Assets/Source/Editor/ShotSolver/README.md): the pure event-to-event ShotSimulator (with ShotFlightState/ShotBoardSnapshot as its state/board data types) and its dynamic-board companions (ShotBoardDynamics, ShotSimBoardActor, ShotMotionMath), its item-carrier companions (ShotItemLayer, ShotSimEffectBoard), and ShotBoardGather, which snapshots the live board/thrower/config into a ShotSolveContext.

The sweep's sample grid (ResolveSweepSampleCount/ResolveSweepAngle) and its sibling ClampToReachableAngle live in Shared/AimAngleGrid rather than here — that grid is a domain rule about which angles the thrower can reach, consumed by four independent places (the live ThrowerController, FireBestShotCheat, ShotSolverWindow, and the Scene-view aim fan overlay), not solver-specific logic, so it lives in Shared/ next to WallLimits for the same reason. ShotBoardGather now consumes AimAngleGrid for its sweep rather than owning the grid math itself: with IProjectileFlightConfig.AimAngleStepDegrees at 0 (continuous aim), a sweep lerps a fixed count of samples across the arc, same as before quantization existed; with a non-zero step, it instead samples exactly the step's multiples that fall inside the arc — the same absolute grid ThrowerController.ClampAndQuantizeAimDirection snaps player input to — so a sweep can never recommend an angle the player could not actually aim at. Both sweeping callers (FireBestShotCheat, ShotSolverWindow) go through AimAngleGrid's two statics rather than each lerping the arc themselves, and both source the arc bounds from IProjectileFlightConfig.AimAngleMinDegrees/AimAngleMaxDegrees — the same range the thrower clamps aim to — rather than a separate constant, so a sweep can never cover a different set of angles than the player can actually reach.

AimAngleGrid.ClampToReachableAngle is the sibling the thrower's own clamp calls: given a raw aim angle, it resolves the same grid-anchored "reachable" angle ResolveSweepAngle would sample at that position — clamping the rounded grid index, not the angle itself, so a clamp-then-snap or snap-then-clamp can't disagree with what the sweep considers reachable (see ThrowerController.ClampAndQuantizeAimDirection's own comment for why the naive orderings both fail).

Lives in the Runtime assembly so both the editor Shot Solver window AND runtime tooling (the Fire Best Shot cheat, development builds on device) can run the same simulation — the editor window keeps the sweeping/bisection UI, this folder owns the physics truth.

The mushrooms

On a meadow board the simulated ball bounces off the mushrooms, and a simulated lance cuts their caps and flies on (Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md). ShotBumperLayer.Gather copies the board's bumper circles (IBumperField, each whole cap with its id and its wall) with the game's bounce rule, its chain rules and the Snipe's speed buff, and every caller of ShotBoardGather.Gather hands it over (the parameter has no default: a caller that left it out would aim through the mushrooms). On a board without mushrooms it is null, and Simulate runs the loop it had before: no line of the mushroom code runs, and ShotMushroomFreeBoardTests holds that mushrooms no shot can reach change no flight to the bit and that a sweep over mushrooms allocates nothing.

The simulator owns no mushroom geometry and no chain rule. It calls BumperSweep, MushroomChainRule and the IBumperBounceRule the live flight calls, in the live flight's order, and pays a completed chain as the game's Snipe handler does (ShotSimulator.PayMushroomReward: Arm arms, and nothing else pays). So the Fire Best Shot cheat, the autoplay and the playtest bot that aim with it see a chain for what it does: a flight that completes one is a lance from the third mushroom on, and is ranked by what that lance then pops. The ranking has no term for a lance, a chain or a cut cap by itself (ShotRanking: golden, hearts that count, nuke, disco, then score).

A lance cuts caps (ShotSimulator.TryCutCap, the mirror of ProjectileMotionResolver.CutCaps). A simulated ball that is piercing, however it was armed, bounces off no mushroom: the first whole cap its path enters before its next wall is cut, there and then, and the ball flies on unturned, so a cut adds no point to the path. The chain is not asked, and the cap that armed the ball is left alone while it still flies the direction it left along. A cut cap leaves the layer's circles for the rest of that flight (ShotBumperLayer.Cut), so the sweep, the kick and the lookahead no longer find it, and the next flight starts from the board as gathered (ResetForNewFlight, which allocates nothing). ShotMushroomTally.CapsCut counts them and ShotBumperLayer.CutLog, when a test sets it, names them. Each cut banks the simulated flight one plain charge, as the game's does (decision K; ShotSimulator.BankCutCharge): BankedPierceCharges, with the Snipe's speed to re-arm at (the layer's LanceSpeedBuffMultiplier, the item configuration's own number, which a test holds against what ships), and ShotMushroomTally.ChargesBanked for the tally. The ranking gives a banked charge no value. A charge is spent where the simulator spends a pierce (SpendPierceAtDischarge), which is a cruise-earned pierce's discharge: a Snipe's lance, and so a chain's, rides to the end of the simulated flight and never needs one.

A bomb blows caps off (ShotSimulator.BlowCapsOff, the mirror of Item/Bomb/MushroomBlast.Bomb). A bomb the simulated flight sets off, the host's or one its blast chained into, takes the caps the game's takes: the ones the bomb's own rule breaks outright (BombBlast.Pierces: the six slots round the bomb's; for a rainbow bomb, a centre within its kill radius). ShotItemLayer notes each bomb it resolves and the simulator hands them to ShotBumperLayer.BlowOff before it looks for the next event, and once more after the last. The caps leave the layer for the rest of that flight, as a cut cap does, and the next flight starts from the board as gathered. For this the layer keeps each cap's slot, taken at the gather from IBumperField.SlotAt; a layer built by hand without slots loses no cap to a bomb. ShotMushroomTally.CapsBlown counts them and ShotBumperLayer.BlownLog, when a test sets it, names them. Not modelled: the sway, which moves no ball, and a nuke's blast, which comes after a fuse and kills every shot in flight. And the game's bomb goes off a frame after the pop that released it, where the simulator's goes off at once: a ball that reaches a cap next to the bomb inside that frame bounces in the game and flies through here.

Which caps are whole is the field's to say: IBumperField.Bumpers lists whole caps only, so a cap a lance cut before the gather is not in the layer, as it is not there for the game in the same frame. What the simulator does not model is anything that changes the board after the gather. It has no beats: a cap the game regrows on the board's beat, seconds into a long flight, stays cut for the whole of a simulated one, and a cap cut before the gather stays cut however long the flight is. And it flies one ball: a cap another ball in flight cuts after the gather is still whole in the layer, so a shot solved a moment before another lance's cut bounces off a cap the game's ball will find gone. Both are accepted: a flight is seconds long, and the next solve gathers the board as it then stands. And the simulator's lance is not spent when it plows a tough balloon (the approximation in Editor/ShotSolver/README.md), so where the game's lance is an ordinary ball again past its next wall and bounces off the caps left, the simulated one goes on cutting them.

The loaded ball

A ball can already carry a grant before it fires: a Snipe pickup that armed the ball still in the thrower, or the Sharpshooter card arming it as it loaded (Game/Upgrades/README.md). Gather takes the loaded ball (IProjectileRegistry.Loaded) and keeps what it carries as the context's LoadedSeed (ShotBoardGather.SeedFor): a piercing ball seeds as ShotFlightSeed.Armed, with its speed buff and a rainbow host's grant riding the pierce, and a rainbow Shield grant seeds as ending at the next wall. SimulateAt launches every angle from that seed, so the Fire Best Shot cheat, the autoplay that aims with it, and the Shot Solver window all plan an armed ball as the lance it is. Banked Snipe charges on a held ball aren't seeded; they matter only once its first pierce discharges.

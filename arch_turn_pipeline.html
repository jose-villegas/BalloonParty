Hit, Spawn and Balance Pipeline

What this diagram shows

Two independent flows that share the grid: balls hitting balloons, and a timer bringing new balloon lines in. There is no "turn" any more: a line never waits for a shot to end, and several balls can be in flight at once.

Hit Any ball in flight bounces off walls. On each balloon contact ProjectileView hands the collision to the plain-C# ProjectileHitResolver, which calls EvaluateHit (state-mutating), embeds the outcome in ActorHitMessage, and routes it through IHitDispatcher (Game/HitPipeline). The pipeline runs the order-dependent stages synchronously and explicitly: ScoreController records streak/score first, then the owning BalloonController (resolved via BalloonControllerRegistry) pops/deflects, and only then broadcasts the message for order-independent observers (NudgeService, ItemActivator, GridActorHitController, BalloonSpawner, SpeckField, VFX). A direct (DamageFlags.DirectHit) pop also gives BalloonSpawner a chance to roll an extra "pop-spawn" balloon into the board, independent of the clock's lines. A ball's death (ProjectileDestroyedMessage) spawns nothing.

Spawn clock SpawnClock counts down scaled game time (2 s at level 1, shrinking per level to a floor, after a grace at each level start) and publishes SpawnBalloonLineMessage(1). It holds while paused, off the Game screen, during a level-up, and while a lone doomed ball glides its last breath. One line per tick, however long the frame. BalloonSpawner handles each message with the synchronous SpawnLine():

  1. Pre-line balance — Balance() consolidates existing balloons upward. On a wave's first line it is Balance(atWaveStart: true), which also relocates roamers and refreshes the heavy-step budget. Transit slots for in-progress balance animations are reserved in BalancePathHolder.
  2. Spawn — one line is placed. Spawn animations use ComputePath waypoints; the BalancePathHolder is consulted to detect conflicts with still-animating balance moves.
  3. Post-line balance — BalloonSpawner publishes BalanceBalloonsMessage; BalloonBalancer runs again on the next frame to settle gaps the fresh line created.

A wave is LinesPerWave (3) consecutive lines. The wave's last line publishes ItemCheckMessage, which is what the item cadence counts.

Rebalance pulse Independently of lines, BalloonBalancer.Tick pulses a Balance() every FlightRebalanceInterval all through play, so hits that open gaps get settled without waiting for the next line.

Guidance

Where to add behavior triggered by a balloon pop: Subscribe to ActorHitMessage filtered by Outcome == Pop. Your subscriber runs synchronously, before the projectile dies, and after the pipeline's ordered stages (score recording and the owning balloon's reaction) have completed. If your behavior must run before the pop or interleave with scoring, it belongs in HitPipeline as an explicit stage, not on the bus. Never publish ActorHitMessage directly — route hits through IHitDispatcher.

Where to add behavior triggered by a ball's death: Subscribe to ProjectileDestroyedMessage. It says nothing about the board: lines come from the clock, so do not hang spawning or board-settling work on it.

Where to add behavior triggered by a new line: Subscribe to BalanceBalloonsMessage for "the grid just got a line and needs settling", or to ItemCheckMessage for once-per-wave work over the new balloons.

Rebalancing: Balance isn't tied to a line. BalloonBalancer.Tick pulses a full Balance() pass at IBalloonsConfiguration.FlightRebalanceInterval all through play — not paused, not while a lone doomed ball glides, and only when HasPossibleMove() finds an actual gap to close — on top of the pre-line and post-line passes above. SecondsUntilNextPulse exposes the pulse timer to the shot solver.

Ordering note: BalanceBalloonsMessage is published by both ProjectileView (fallback — in case BalloonSpawner never gets to publish it) and BalloonSpawner (authoritative — after each line is placed). BalloonBalancer debounces redundant publishes via a one-frame UniTask.Yield delay.

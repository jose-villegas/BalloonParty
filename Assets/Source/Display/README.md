| `SceneCaptureService` | MonoBehaviour on the persistent camera — a **shared low-res capture of the scene's visuals**, bound globally as `_SceneCaptureTex` (resolution divisor from `IGameDisplayConfiguration`). Any effect that wants "what the screen roughly looks like" acquires it instead of using a `GrabPass` (a full-screen mid-frame resolve on tile GPUs); the first consumer is the Unbreakable's chrome reflection (`UnbreakableBalloonVariant` ref-counts via `Acquire`/`Release` on enable/disable). The serialized layer mask picks what gets captured — consumers sampling it should exclude their own layer to avoid feedback. The capture camera clears with **alpha 0**, so the RT's alpha channel doubles as a sprite-coverage mask (`ScreenSpaceLightService`'s occlusion source). The capture RT is allocated with a **24-bit depth buffer** even though nothing samples it — URP's RenderGraph rejects a depthless camera output texture ("Fake or uninitialized surface"). The runtime-created capture camera gets a `UniversalAdditionalCameraData` with post-processing off, and its volume stack frozen before its first capture (`CameraVolumes`): it reads none, and the scenario grade animates only the main camera's. One shared mask/resolution on purpose; a second consumer with different needs is the moment to generalize. Its inspector live-previews the capture RT in play mode (`SceneCaptureServiceEditor`). Registered via `RegisterComponentInHierarchy<SceneCaptureService>()` in `AppLifetimeScope` (scope build fails if absent from the persistent camera). Exposes `ContentVersion`, bumped once per unique capture (stamped at the start of `LateUpdate`, since a capture camera left enabled last frame rendered during last frame's render phase) — consumers gate rebuild work on it instead of re-deriving the cadence themselves |
| `EffectsClearingMaskView` | MonoBehaviour on the persistent camera: the **clearing mask** of `Plans/PLAN-EffectsLayerClearing.md`, a two-channel target (`RGHalf`, else `ARGBHalf`, else `ARGB32`; screen over `IEffectsClearingSettings.MaskDownscale`; red how cleared, green the radius of the dome there) bound globally as `_EffectsClearMask`, with `_EffectsClearParams` (edge low, edge high, strength, view aspect) and `_EffectsClearLens` (refraction as a share of the radius, one mask texel in view heights, dispersion, highlight). Once a frame, from `LateUpdate`, one ping-pong blit (`EffectsClearingMask` shader) decays last frame's mask by Δt / linger time and stamps each ball in flight on it: a capsule from where the ball was to where it is, valued 1 − (d/r)², its radius r growing evenly with the ball's shields (`MinClearRadius` at one, `MaxClearRadius` at `ShieldsForMaxRadius`), so the cleared area shrinks inward as it decays. Last frame's mask is read through the camera's move and zoom since (`ClearingView.ReprojectionFrom`), its radii scaled with the zoom, so the stamps stay on the world while the level-up rig pans and zooms in on the ball. That trail is off by default (`Trail` on the settings): then the mask is wiped each frame and every ball stamps a disc, so it holds only a dome where each ball is; a ball gone from flight leaves its disc where it was last seen, fading (`EffectsClearingStamps.Fades`, taken off the dome in the shader) so its lens shrinks inward to nothing over the linger time, and the mask goes idle once none is left. The fade runs on game time while another ball flies and on real time otherwise, the same clock as the decay. Pulls the stamps from whatever `IEffectsClearingSource` is bound (the run's `EffectsClearingController`). `IsClearing` holds from the first stamp until a full linger time after the last; past it the mask is blitted black and the blit stops, so aiming and menus cost nothing. Decays on game time while a ball flies (slow-mo stretches a clearing, a pause holds it) and on real time once none does, so the level-up's freeze can't hold a clearing open. | `EffectsMapCompositor` | MonoBehaviour, one on each camera: its **effects map**. While the mask is clearing, and for `MapHoldSeconds` (1.5, game time) after, it claims its effect layers away from the camera (`CameraCullingMask`), renders them with a runtime effects camera into a map (transparent clear, so the map is the effects premultiplied, alpha their coverage) and lays the map back with a camera-fitted quad (`EffectsMapComposite` shader, `Blend One OneMinusSrcAlpha`). The **main camera's** takes the `Effects` layer, the chaos a ball flies through (balloon pops and armour breaks, the bomb's smoke, the laser, the lightning chain, the paint splashes and blobs, the nuke cloud, and two things that are always there: the dust specks and the unbreakable formations' glow rings; the lightning's glow, a light on the ground under the balloons, stays on the board), into an `ARGBHalf` map composited at Sky −1, over the board and the ball and before post, so bloom and the tonemapper see the lightning and the laser as they're made, over 1. The **HUD camera's** takes `Trail` (the score, heart and highlight trails and the score shapes' pens) into an 8-bit map at UI 3300, where the score trails sort. Where the mask is open the map is seen through a dome of thick glass over each ball: a stamp's 1 − (d/r)² profile is the dome's height, so the shader refracts the map by the mask's slope (magnified, so effects spread away from the ball), splits it a little by colour at the rim, fades it by the strength, and adds a glint and a rim of light (the highlight is the scene light's: on the side of the dome the light comes from, in its colour and strength; the rim is not dimmed). With the mask empty that is the same blend the effects make drawing straight in, which is why it only runs while clearing. The hold keeps it on until the pops a shot set off are done: off, the main camera's effects sort in their own places, under the ball and some under the balloons, so a shard mid-air would slip under them. Within the clearing and the hold, the **effects camera renders only while something is alive on its layers** (`Shared/Rendering/LayerPresence`: every pooled effect counts from `Get` to `Return`, and the nuke cloud, the dust specks and the formation rings, which aren't pooled, by their renderers). On any other frame the map would lay back nothing, and those two extra camera renders were most of the lens's cost: the rest of the hold once the last effect is done renders none. A ball flying before its first pop no longer renders none: the specks are alive the whole run, so the effects camera renders on every frame of a flight and of the hold after it (its cost has not been measured on a device). While a ball flies the specks and rings are laid at the map's place, over the board and the ball, and not under the balloons where they sort; they drop back when the map is released. Releasing the layers then changes nothing either, as nothing is on them; the moment something spawns the layers are claimed and the map renders that same frame (looked at again just before render, on `Canvas.preWillRenderCanvases`, for anything a late tick launched). The hold is now a ceiling: the map goes off once its layers empty, and never stays past `MapHoldSeconds`, so an effect still alive then drops into its own place as before. The **glint belongs to the glass, not the map**: the quad stays on whenever a clearing is open, with a transparent texture in place of a map not rendered that frame, so the glint never flickers as effects come and go. Each composite adds it, the main one before bloom and the tonemapper and the HUD's after, as since the HDR map came in. In checked builds the compositor counts the frames it was held (`HeldFrames`, every one of which it rendered before) against the frames its camera rendered (`RenderedFrames`), and once a second while held with its layers said empty it looks for a renderer drawing on them anyway and warns once for each: a source neither pooled nor watched, which would show outside the glass. The effects camera's volume stack is frozen (`CameraVolumes`), as it post-processes nothing. Runs right after the mask view, so it turns on in the same frame as the first stamp. The effects camera renders just before the stack (`_stackBase`'s depth, else its own camera's, − 1) and mirrors its orthographic size on `Canvas.preWillRenderCanvases`, as `HudCamera` does. Injected by `AppLifetimeScope`'s build callback, each one in its hierarchy (nothing resolves them) |

| `ScreenSpaceLightService` | MonoBehaviour on the persistent camera, next to `SceneCaptureService` (architecture: @ref arch_screen_space_light). ## How it works

`GameDisplayConfiguration` (in `Configuration/`) holds a reference world width and height. `GetOrthogonalSize()` computes the orthographic size that fits both against the current screen aspect (`max` of fit-width and fit-height), so the play field is fully visible on every device without letterboxing or clipping. Its custom editor previews the resulting size for common device ratios, in the inspector and as a Scene-view overlay.

There is a single persistent camera for the whole session (the `Main Camera` nested in the app-root
prefab, `DontDestroyOnLoad`, with its `HUD Camera` overlay as a child) — Launch and Game never had, and now never get, their own camera. The
whole camera pipeline above (`OrthogonalSizeCameraController`, `SceneCaptureService`,
`ScreenSpaceLightService`, `CameraBackgroundTint`, `CinematicCameraView`, `CameraShakeView`,
`NavigationCameraReveal`) is registered via `RegisterComponentInHierarchy<T>()` in `AppLifetimeScope`
(`Game/`) against that one prefab; `GameLifetimeScope` and `LaunchLifetimeScope` resolve them from the
parent rather than owning their own copies.

## URP RenderGraph constraints

The project runs URP's 2D Renderer. Two constraints apply to anyone touching `SceneCaptureService` or `ScreenSpaceLightService`:

- **No mid-render-loop `Graphics.Blit`.** `RenderPipelineManager.beginCameraRendering` looked like the natural port target for the old `OnPreRender`-driven blit chain, but URP's RenderGraph rejects a `Graphics.Blit` issued from inside that callback ("EndRenderPass: Not inside a Renderpass"). `ScreenSpaceLightService` instead drives its two blits from `LateUpdate`, the same pattern the disturbance field already used — outside the render loop entirely, at the cost of reading the previous frame's capture (see above; it's invisible here).
- **Camera output render textures need a depth buffer.** A camera's `targetTexture` must carry a depth-stencil attachment even if nothing reads depth, or RenderGraph rejects it as a "Fake or uninitialized surface". `SceneCaptureService`'s capture RT is allocated with 24-bit depth for this reason alone — the 2D content underneath never samples it.

Both were discovered porting `ScreenSpaceLightService`; treat them as defaults for any new camera-to-texture or per-frame blit code in this pipeline, not special cases.

Detailed Description

All game data is split across focused ScriptableObjects. Most are registered as singletons in GameLifetimeScope and injected wherever needed. A handful that the single persistent camera and its always-on backdrop fields need — GameDisplayConfiguration, DisturbanceFieldSettings, BackgroundFieldSettings, SkyScatterSettings, and the direction/GI-facing interfaces of SceneLightFieldSettings (ISceneLightSettings/ITimeOfDaySettings/IScreenSpaceLightSettings) — are instead owned by AppLifetimeScope, the persistent app root that parents both the Launcher and Game scopes; see Assets/Source/README.md.

Source is organized into per-context subfolders: Balloons/, Buffs/, Cinematics/, Effects/, GridActors/, Items/, Level/, Palette/, Ranges/, and Editor/ (drawers). The tables below note the subfolder where it isn't obvious.

LevelPacingConfiguration ILevelPacingConfiguration How levels pace (Configuration/Level/). Every positive level's mix is generated by LevelGenerator from the _generation block (LevelGenerationSettings, see Data types); ParametersForLevel(level) returns it as a RangedLevelParameters, and SeedForLevel(level) is the level's seed. The _ranges array now holds only negative-keyed debug presets (-100…, reached by the start-level cheat with a negative level), exposed as DebugPresets. Also holds the ThresholdModifier/scoring curve and the _spawnClock block (SpawnClockSettings / ISpawnClockSettings, exposed as ILevelPacingConfiguration.SpawnClock) — when lines arrive, independent of the per-level what: first-level interval (1.7 s), per-level decay (×0.95) down to MinInterval (0.9 s), then a late decay (LateIntervalDecayPerLevel, ×0.98) down to LateMinInterval (0.55 s), so lines keep speeding up on deep levels where the generator has nothing left to add, start-of-level GraceSeconds (2.5 s), LinesPerWave (3), OverlapShotPull (0.35: the share of the current wait a ball fired while another is flying takes off the next line), SparseBoardSpeedup (8: how much faster the clock runs on an empty board), TargetOccupancy (0.5: the share of open slots the clock pushes balloons towards) and OccupancyWeight (0.5: how the board's fill is read, 0 = against the level's starting fill only, 1 = as occupancy against the target only). The clock eases to normal pace as that blended fill reaches 1. On top of that board pace, FlightSpeedup (3) and FlightPressureSeconds (6) add flight pressure: the summed flight time of every ball in the air, full at 6 s, adds up to 2 more times the pace. A big clear earns a breather: losing BreatherClear (0.15) of the open slots within BreatherClearWindow (2.5 s) drops the clock to BreatherPace (0.2 of its full pace), easing back to full over BreatherSeconds (11 s), and BreatherCooldownSeconds (14 s) of play must pass after it before the next; 0 clear turns it off. Score shapes covering BreatherShapeCover (0.5) of the play area start the same breather; 0 turns that off. So does danger at BreatherDanger (1) or more, a full board on the last heart (0.5 adds the second to last); 0 turns that off. Pressure (PressureSpeedup 2×, PressureBuildSeconds 120 s, PressureHoldOccupancy 0.85, PressureKeptOnHeartLoss 0.5) pushes back on a comfortable player: it builds while the board stays under the hold and the player is not in need, and runs the clock up to the speedup. IntervalForLevel(level) is the only read the spawn clock needs; levels below 1 run at level 1's pace. Mushrooms exposes the _generation block's mushroom settings (IMushroomSettings, see Data types), which GameLifetimeScope registers under that interface. Resolved into the live LevelParameters by LevelDifficultyResolver (Game/Level/); consumers read IActiveLevelParameters.Current, never this SO directly

HeadsUpSettings IHeadsUpSettings (HeadsUp/) The handwritten heads-ups: how many show at once (MaxConcurrent, 6, held ones aside) and one HeadsUpStyleSettings per HeadsUpStyleId: the format, the look (HeadsUpStyle: ink, size, growth, weight), the priority, cooldown, hold time, held-until-released, the offset from its anchor, the range of numbers worth writing, the count its size starts growing from, and the count it's underlined from (the streak from 6). BalloonPrefabEntry Serializable entry in BalloonsConfiguration — holds a BalloonView prefab reference, spawn weight, optional max-count cap, HitsToPop (how many hits before popping; -1 = unbreakable), ScoreValue (points awarded on pop), per-type NudgeOverride[], per-outcome HitVfxOverride[], and a PopPulse. BalloonType drives which model class is created (Simple → BalloonModel, Tough → ToughBalloonModel). Item eligibility is determined by whether the model implements IHasWriteableItemSlot — not a flag. Pool key is derived from the prefab's GameObject name.
GridActorPrefabEntry Serializable entry in GridActorConfiguration — holds a GridActorView prefab reference, GridActorType, SlotPlacementMode (Random or Cluster), and HitsToPop (relevant only for Gatekeeper). Per-level spawn count and cluster cap come from the level pacing gate (IActiveLevelParameters.TryGetGridActorGate → ResolvedGridActorGate), not the entry. Pool key is derived from the prefab's GameObject name.
MushroomSettings / IMushroomSettings Serializable tuning of the meadow's mushrooms (GridActors/, Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md). Rules (MushroomChainRules, in Shared/): ChainTarget (3 hits in one flight arm the lance; nothing is paid past it, since the ball is a lance from there and cuts caps instead of bouncing off them), RepeatHitCounts (off: the wall of caps hit last counts again only after another) and MaxBouncesPerStretch (12: the most bounces off mushrooms between two walls, after which they let the ball through; a chain is paid by its third counted hit, and 0 or less reads as 12, never as no cap). The bounce: ForgivingKick (on) and Kick (BumperKickTuning, in Shared/): ConeDegrees (20: how far from the mirror reflection a cap of another wall may lie and still pull the bounce toward it; it is the dial for how much skill a rally asks, narrowed from 30 by the owner), MinOffset and MaxOffset (0.15 and 0.9: how near a cap's centre, and how near a graze, a kicked ball may land, as a share of the radius it bounces at; they are also the turns a kicked ball can make, 163 down to 52 degrees). Radius (0.3 world units, without the ball's). Placement (MushroomPlacementLimits, in Slots/Actor/): 2 to 4 walls of caps (a wall is 1 to 3 caps in neighbouring slots), 4 to 7 caps in all, MinSpacing (1.9 world units, two and a half slots: the least two walls stand apart, nearest cap to nearest cap, like the bumpers of a pinball table; never read as less than the room a ball needs between two, and 0 or less reads as 1.9), the columns and rows kept clear, and Band (MushroomLayoutBand: what a layout must measure, flown from every aim angle on an empty board, to be in the baked catalogue the levels are dealt from: 7 to 16 in 100 angles arm the lance, the widest unbroken run of them spans 2 degrees or more, and at least 20 angles arm) and Cannon (MushroomCannonClearance: the room a layout must leave the cannon to be in the catalogue: MinDistance (3.25: no cap's centre nearer the point the cannon turns about at rest, which keeps the lowest row a cap may stand in empty), and MaxShadowShare of the aim angles (0.2) at the most whose first straight leg from the muzzle meets a cap within NearRange (4), so no wall stands side-on across the cannon's view). The layouts themselves are baked to these numbers (Slots/README.md, Mushroom placement): after changing the cone, the offsets, the radius, the rules or the placement, bake the catalogue again (Tools > BalloonParty > Bake Mushroom Wall Catalogue), or MushroomAimGridTests fails. PermanentSlotLimit (9): the slots the mushrooms' caps and the bushes may take together. Lean (MushroomLeanTuning, in Slots/Actor/), how a cap answers a hit: LeanPerSpeed (0.014 world units of lean at its furthest for each unit of the ball's speed, so 0.14 for a shot off the cannon, nearly the most: seen from above, the stem under the cap shows only once the cap has been knocked well off its foot), MaxLean (0.15, the furthest it ever leans), Frequency (3.6 wobbles a second) and Damping (0.16: low, so the cap swings back past its rest a few times), RewardPulse (1: how far the cap swells when a bounce pays the lance, as a share of the material's full swell and glow) and RestLean (0.0015: under it a cap that has also stopped is still, and its view switches off), and SwayLean (0.15, never more than MaxLean: how far a bomb's blast leans a cap it sways at full strength, before the cap swings back). RegrowBeats (5: how many of the board's beats a cap a lance cut stays gone; it grows back on the beat that brings its count to this, so between 6.8 and 8.5 seconds with the beat at 1.7; never read as less than 1). Blast (MushroomBlastTuning, in Shared/), what a bomb does to the mushrooms it does not take the caps off: SwayShare (2.4: how far the bomb sways them, as a share of its kill radius; at 1 a mushroom sways when its cap overlaps the kill radius, which on the shipped board is the ring of slots past the ones next to the bomb, faintly and under the bomb's own cloud, so it ships wider: at 2.4 the reach is 3.3 and the third ring, outside the cloud, sways at about a half; 0 turns it off, and it is never read as under 0). Which caps a bomb takes off is no setting: it is the bomb's own rule for what it breaks outright (Item/Bomb/BombBlast.Pierces). Cut (MushroomCutTuning, in Slots/Actor/), how a cap a lance has cut falls and pops back, the look only (when a cap is cut is the rule's to say, and when it grows back is RegrowBeats): FallSeconds (0.6, from the cut until the two pieces have faded out), PartDistance (0.16 world units each piece ends up from the cut line, to its own side) and CarryDistance (0.08 along the lance's way), TurnRadians (authored in degrees: 14, one piece each way), EndSize (0.55: a piece's size when it reaches the ground, as a share of its size on the stem; it shrinks toward its far rim, and at this size its cut edge has drawn back off the stem by the end), RegrowSeconds (0.35) and RegrowOvershoot (0.1: how far past its size the cap swells as it pops back); Spores (MushroomSporeTuning, in Slots/Actor/), what a mushroom lets go (Slots/README.md): Refractory (0.4 seconds a mushroom keeps quiet after a hit's puff), Budget and BudgetPerSecond (18 and 3: what the whole board may let go at once, counted in hits, and how fast it comes back), a hit's puff (MinSpeed 2 and FullSpeed 9.8, the ball's speeds for nothing and for all of it; Length 1 and EndWidth 0.6, the smoke's cone, longer than a cap is wide; Smoke 0.9, its share of the smoke profile's opacity (the field shows nothing of a stamp under about a third); Specks 10; PaidScale 1.5 for the hit that pays a chain), CutScale (2) and BlastScale (6), a cut's and a blast's as multiples of a full hit, BlastDelay (0.45 seconds, for the bomb's own cloud to thin) and BlastFanDegrees (170), and the specks (SpeckSpeed 3.4, SpeckSeconds 1.1, SpeckSize 0.09, which most are under and a few well over, SpeckGreen and SpeckBlue, the two colours each is between). The numbers were about half these until the owner asked for more (PLAN-MeadowMushrooms, Bigger spores). The smoke's colour and how long it lingers are the smoke field's (SmokeFieldSettings, the MushroomSpores profile: about a second for a hit's puff, two and a half for a blast's cloud). Blown (MushroomThrowTuning), how a cap a blast blows off flies, whole: Seconds (1.1 in the air), Distance (0.9 world units thrown, away from the blast), Lift (0.25: how much larger it is drawn at the top of its throw; its shadow lies up to ShadowLift times as far off it, 2.25) and TurnRadians (authored in degrees: 40). RoomBeyond(radius) is the room what came off needs past the cap's own radius by the bound the shader boxes it with (the radius times the lift and the turn, plus how far it is moved; the larger of a cut's and a throw's), which the builder sizes the quad for. Validate adds a message for each number that cannot work with the others (a cap on bounces under what a chain takes, crossed offsets, a cone too narrow for two walls to send the ball back, a spacing under two caps across, fewest walls over most, fewest caps over most, most caps under the most walls, caps that leave the bushes fewer than 2 slots, a band with its ends crossed, a ceiling that lets no aim angle meet a cap within the cannon's near range, a count of walls the baked catalogue has no layout for, a furthest lean over half the radius, which would carry the cap off the quad it is drawn on); LevelGenerationSettings.Validate calls it, so they show with the Level Pacing asset's other issues. The kept-clear rows and the shared limit are first guesses, to be tuned in play; the spacing, the band and the cannon's room were measured, and the cone is the owner's (the plan's Spacing as built, Walls with a kick as built and Walls with a kick, after its review). It is a plain serializable class, the _mushrooms block of LevelGenerationSettings on the Level Pacing asset. Its field initialisers are the shipped values, so the asset plays them until someone edits the block in the Inspector. LevelGenerator reads the count range and PermanentSlotLimit; the bounce, the chain, the solver, the aim line and MushroomView inject IMushroomSettings
IWeightedEntry Shared interface for weighted random selection — Weight, MaxCount, PoolKey, and NeedBoost (the weight × (1 + NeedBoost × the player's need) when a pick passes one). Implemented by the resolver's resolved pick entries (ResolvedBalloonEntry, ResolvedItemEntry), which carry the active range's weight/cap; the raw catalog entries no longer implement it.
LevelRangeEntry Serializable debug preset in LevelPacingConfiguration (Level/) — a RangedLevelParameters keyed by a negative FromLevel; IsPreset is true for those. A positive entry is ignored (the validator warns), since positive levels are generated. A preset always plays on the sky, so the validator also warns when one lists a ground-only actor (one the catalogue doesn't allow on the sky, IContentPiece.AllowedOn: the bushes) with a count above 0: it would float. Mushrooms are reported the same way whatever the catalogue says: they are no catalogue piece, so it has no entry to ask, and they belong to the meadow
LevelGenerationSettings The _generation block of LevelPacingConfiguration (Level/) — everything a generated level is built from: a seed salt; the scenario schedule (_scenario, see ScenarioScheduleSettings below); two BudgetFormulas (threat and aid: base + step per level, capped); FeaturedLevels; level-stepped colour count and board lines (IntLevelStep[]) and item-count curves (ItemCountStep[], each step holds until the next); the extra-colour level (ExtraColorLevelSettings, below); the meadow's mushrooms (_mushrooms, see MushroomSettings above); ItemCadence; and three catalogues (BalloonContentEntry[], ItemContentEntry[], GridActorContentEntry[]). SeedForLevel hashes the level with the salt, so a retry replays the same mix. It validates its own catalogue (no staple balloon, bad unlock level, duplicates, invalid weights, a Mushroom entry, which is never used)
LevelGenerator Pure, seeded builder (Level/): Generate(settings, level, seed) → RangedLevelParameters, so every consumer is unchanged. Staples (cost 0) and pieces still in their featured levels are always in; then each role's budget buys a cost-weighted pick of the other unlocked pieces. A level leaves out the pieces its scenario doesn't allow (IContentPiece.AllowedOn) before anything is bought, so their share of the budget goes to the rest: bushes off the sky; clouds, bushes and everything that bounces shots but the unbreakable off the water; Tough and Tougher balloons off the meadow. The threat budget grows with depth (more threats); the aid budget stays small (aids rotate). A meadow level also gets its mushrooms (Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md): a GridActorType.Mushroom gate with a fixed count, which no budget pays for and no catalogue entry asks for. MushroomWallsFor gives the fewest of IMushroomSettings.Placement (2) on the stretch's first level, growing evenly to the most (4) on its last, a half rounding up (2, 3, 3, 4 over four levels), and the fewest on a stretch of one level. The gate counts walls of caps; which layout a level is dealt, and so how many caps, is not known until it is. Bushes and caps share PermanentSlotLimit (9) slots, and the bushes give way: the bush gate's count is capped at the limit less the most caps the level's walls may hold (MushroomPlacementLimits.MaxCapsFor: 6 beside two walls, 7 beside three or four, which leaves the bushes 3 or 2 slots), and the gate is dropped when no slot is left. An extra-colour level has no grid actors, so no mushrooms. The level also says which formation shapes its unbreakables may form (AllowedFormationShapes): the snake alone on the meadow, every shape elsewhere. Nothing places a mushroom on the board yet: the level carries the count for the plan's next phase
ScenarioScheduleSettings / ScenarioSchedule / ScenarioKind Which levels are set in the sky, on the ground or on water (Level/). The _scenario block of LevelGenerationSettings: a window for the first ground level (GroundFromLevel 6 to GroundByLevel 6, so level 6; ScenarioSchedule.FirstGroundLevel rolls one level in it, and every level before it is sky), a first ground stretch of a fixed length (4), then alternating sky and ground stretches, each kind with its own min and max (sky 2–4, ground 3–5), rolled from the stretch's first level and the block's own salt mixed with the run's seed (IRunSeed, Game/Run/). So each run lays out its own ground and sky, retuning content never reshuffles scenarios, and a retry, which keeps the run's seed, keeps its scenario. ParametersForLevel and ScenarioForLevel take the run seed; 0 is the schedule with no run mixed in, which the generation preview window shows. ScenarioSchedule.StretchAt is pure and returns a ScenarioStretch (kind, the level's position in its stretch, the stretch's length); ForLevel is the kind alone, read through ILevelPacingConfiguration.ScenarioForLevel (debug presets are sky). A meadow trades its puff clouds for bushes: ScenarioStretch.PuffCloudShare is how much of its puff-cloud count a level keeps (a meadow of n levels has clouds on its first ⌊n/2⌋, at least one, the last of those at half, then clears) and BushShare how much of its bush count (half under full clouds, growing to full as they go). LevelGenerator scales the Puff and Bush gates by them after picking and drops the Puff gate at 0; once a meadow starts clearing (IsClearingMeadow), unlocked bushes are always in, bought with the budget the clouds free. The surface stretch right after the first meadow is water (WaterAfterFirstMeadow), and every one after that is a meadow or water (ScenarioKind.Water, rolled with WaterShare, 50%, after the stretch's length, so water never moves where stretches fall); two surfaces are always separated by sky. Water has no puff clouds or bushes at all (both shares are 0), see IContentPiece.AllowedOn

ExtraColorLevelSettings The _extraColor block of LevelGenerationSettings (Level/): a rare level that plays one colour more than its step gives (with the shipped steps holding at three, the fourth, purple), drops every threat but the staple balloons (no tough, tougher or unbreakable balloons, no grid actors) brings its lines faster (SpawnIntervalScale, 0.7: about 40% more often) and more items

From FromLevel (8) a level rolls on the run's seed, so runs differ and a retry replays it. MinLevelsBetween (2) ordinary levels always come between two, so they never bunch up; after that the first level rolls Chance (0.15), and each one that misses raises the next by ChanceGrowthPerLevel (0.1), a soft pity, so a run never waits long for one: on average every 5 levels, never closer than 3 apart, and in a simulation of 4,000 runs never more than 12 (the flat 0.2 with only "never two in a row" before 2026-11-16 averaged every 6 but left one gap in ten at 12 levels or more). The editor's level preview marks one with a + after its colour count (for run seed 0). The level's points are split over its colours, the extra one included (ThresholdForLevel(level, extraColor: true)), so it asks no more in all than an ordinary level: each colour's goal is a quarter smaller with three steps' colours. It asked the full per-colour goal of the extra colour on top until 2026-11-15, a third more in all, which made late ones drag. Design rules

  • Never hardcode values that exist in a configuration asset.
  • Never duplicate configuration data via [SerializeField] on individual systems. If a system needs a value, it injects the relevant configuration object.
  • Always inject the read-only interface, not the concrete SO type. Every configuration SO has a corresponding I-prefixed interface (IBalloonsConfiguration, IGamePalette, etc.) that exposes only read-only properties. GameLifetimeScope registers each SO via RegisterInstance<IInterface>(so). This prevents accidental mutation of SO fields at runtime and decouples consumers from the SO class.
  • New configuration fields are added to the appropriate asset type. When a domain of settings grows large enough to stand alone, extract it into its own ScriptableObject rather than bloating an existing one.
  • Cross-assembly configuration interfaces live in Shared/ (not Configuration/) because they are consumed by assemblies that do not reference the Configuration namespace. Examples: IProjectileFlightConfig, ISlotGridConfig, IPredictionTraceConfig, IRunConfig, IScoreTrailConfig. All other configuration interfaces live alongside their SO in Configuration/. These five focused contracts replaced the former single GameConfiguration / IGameConfiguration umbrella, which was decomposed into ProjectileFlightConfig, SlotGridConfig, PredictionTraceConfig, RunConfig, and ScoreTrailBehaviourConfiguration (score-trail timing) plus ProjectileVisualConfig (projectile-death presentation) and removed entirely.
  • New common fields added to ItemSettings or BalloonPrefabEntry appear in the Inspector automatically — no drawer changes required. Only fields that need custom layout (variable height, conditional visibility, custom controls) must be added to the drawer's ExcludedFields set.
  • Collection properties on interfaces use IReadOnlyList<T> to prevent element assignment and mutation of the collection structure. The backing T[] or List<T> in the SO satisfies this implicitly.
  • Editor config lookups must use ConfigAssetCache<T> (in Shared/) instead of inline AssetDatabase.FindAssets + LoadAssetAtPath. This caches the result after the first lookup and avoids repeated asset-database queries. The class is guarded by #if UNITY_EDITOR so it compiles away in builds, but is available to both runtime OnValidate blocks and editor code. The config-asset-cache audit rule enforces this in editor files and #if UNITY_EDITOR blocks everywhere.

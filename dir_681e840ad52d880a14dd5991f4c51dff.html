Detailed Description

A ribbon of cloth with writing on it, hanging from a spring line: the launch screen's Continue balloon carries one (Game/LaunchContinueView). The line and ribbon are simulated together as points joined by springs, so they swing, stretch a little and flutter as whatever holds the line moves.

How it moves

RibbonCloth is the simulation, plain C#. Points carry position and last position (position-based dynamics, at fixed steps of Steps Per Second, at most eight a frame); each step adds the forces, then a few solver passes pull every spring back toward its rest length and push points out of the obstacles.

  • The line hangs from an anchor (the balloon's knot), a chain of springs. Its last point is the ribbon's pivot: the middle of the ribbon's head, in the swallowtail notch.
  • The ribbon is three rows of points (top edge, middle, bottom edge) by Columns, joined along, across, diagonally (shear) and two columns apart (bend). Its points are light (Ribbon Weight): the breeze moves the ribbon without hauling the line off hanging down.
  • The breeze streams the ribbon out behind the anchor's sideways travel, so a still or drifting balloon keeps it straight. Each column is held upright, top over bottom: the ribbon can droop and turn about the vertical, but can't roll over in the screen's plane.
  • Turning round. When the anchor's travel reverses (faster than Heading Speed), the breeze turns round over Turn Rate, and while it turns the tail is pushed out of the screen (Swing Kick). So the ribbon swings round through depth to trail the other way, rather than flipping over; on the way, and once it trails the other way, its back faces the screen.
  • Room. Given the sideways room it has (SetRoom), the breeze streams the ribbon toward the side with room for it, even against the travel, rather than off the edge; turning back takes a margin over the ribbon's length, so it doesn't flutter at the edge of room.
  • The wave runs down the ribbon toward the tail, growing from nothing at the pivot: mostly a fold in depth, a little lift. With the springs it reads as cloth fluttering.
  • Obstacles (KeepOut: a circle, or a capsule) push points out sideways, never in depth.

Space is the caller's: x right, y up, z toward the viewer. The ribbon's front faces the viewer while it streams to +x. Drawing drops z.

How it draws

Class What it draws
SpringLineGraphic The line, a thin strip through its points
ClothRibbonGraphic The ribbon's three rows as a notched strip, through BalloonParty/Sprite/CelRibbon (the card reveal's ribbon shader): each fold shaded by how its normal leans, as seen from the side facing the screen, plus the shader's drop-shadow copy
ClothRibbonWriting The line of writing, on both faces: two TMP_Texts laid out flat, then every glyph corner carried onto the cloth where it sits. The back's copy is laid along the ribbon the other way (mirrored), so seen from behind, once the ribbon has swung round, it reads the right way. Each glyph shows on the face turned toward the screen where it sits and fades as that part turns edge on

All three are simulated in their own local space, so they sit together at the same origin. ClothRibbonWriting.Layout lays the writing out and returns the length of ribbon it needs, which the simulation is built with.

Settings

RibbonClothSettings (serialized on the view that owns the ribbon) holds the line's and ribbon's shape, the forces and the solver. Tooltips say what each does.

Detailed Description

Upgrade cards: the run's source of power. Every few level-ups a hand of cards is dealt and one card is taken, which stays for the rest of the run. For now the level-up takes the hand's first card in play and names it in the tooltip; the pick screen that lets the player choose is switched off because it stopped play (see UI/LevelUp/README.md). Pressure grows with level (the spawn clock, the level budget); power grows only with the player's picks. The ball cap in particular never rises with level, only through a card.

Components

File Responsibility
IWeaponStats The run's weapon after its cards: MaxBallsInFlight, ReloadSeconds (the reload while another ball flies), IdleReloadSeconds (the near-instant one with nothing flying, which no card changes), StartingShields, ExtraItemChance. Each value is the configured base (IProjectileFlightConfig) plus every card held. Read these, not the flight config, wherever a card can change the value
IUpgradeDraft The cards side: Offer (the hand of the level-up being presented, empty when none is pending or it deals none), BonusCard (the current attempt's bonus card, or null), StacksOf(card), and Pick(slot), which stages a card of the hand as the level-up's pick
RunUpgrades Implements both, plus Game/Run's IBonusCardSource, IRunResettable and IStartable (it follows ILevelProgress.Phase). Holds the stack count per card and the bonus card, and recomputes the weapon when either changes

The deck and its rules are IUpgradeSettings (authored in RunConfig as Upgrades, and registered on its own so RunUpgrades depends on the deck alone): CardEveryLevels (2), CardsOffered (3) and Cards, each with a kind, title, description, a per-stack Amount, MaxStacks and a deal Weight.

The cards

Kind What one stack does Read by
BallCap +Amount balls in flight at once (rounded) ThrowerReload.HasFreeSlot, and the projectile pool, which ThrowerController tops up on the next load

All four are read live, so a held card applies from the next ball loaded and the next item roll.

A level-up's hand

The hand follows the level-up phase (Game/Level/README.md):

stateDiagram-v2
[*] --> Closed
Closed --> Open: Pending on a card level-up
Open --> Staged: Pick a slot
Staged --> Closed: Transitioning, the pick is held
Open --> Closed: Playing, the level-up was aborted
Staged --> Closed: Playing, the pick is dropped
Open --> Closed: ResetRun
Staged --> Closed: ResetRun
Phase change What happens to the hand
→ Pending Opens: deals the hand for entering Level + 1, or nothing on a level-up that deals no cards
Pick(slot) Stages that card. Rejected when no hand is open, a pick is already staged, or the slot is outside the hand
→ Transitioning The level has advanced: the staged card is held, and the hand closes. A held card is announced with UpgradeCardHeldMessage (the card and its stacks now), which the card reveal queues (Game/CardReveal/README.md)
→ Playing Reached from Pending only when the level-up is aborted (a loss became certain mid-beat): the hand and any pick are void
ResetRun The hand and any pick are void

So a card is held only once the level that dealt it is completed. That is what lets a retry keep "the cards held when the level started" without a snapshot: the cards held at a loss are always the ones held when the lost level began.

A level-up deals cards when (newLevel − 1) is a multiple of CardEveryLevels: with 2, entering levels 3, 5, 7 and so on. For now every level-up passes in play, and a card level-up takes the hand's first card, already the first draw by weight (see UI/LevelUp/README.md). 0 turns cards off.

The hand is drawn by weight (WeightedPickExtensions.PickWeightedIndex), without repeats, from the cards not yet maxed and not zero-weight; fewer are dealt when fewer are left, and none once every card is maxed. The draw is seeded from the level's seed (ILevelPacingConfiguration.SeedForLevel, salted), so reaching the same level-up with the same cards held deals the same hand, including the re-detected level-up after an abort.

Run scope and retries

ResetRun drops every card on a fresh run and keeps them on a retry (IRetryState.RetryLevel > 0). It runs at RunResetOrder.Counters, before RetryTracker consumes the staged retry level.

A fresh run that a start-level cheat begins past level 1 (CheatState.StartLevel, in checked builds) then takes the cards a run that played up to it would hold (TakeCardsOnTheWayTo): every card level-up on the way deals its seeded hand and takes the first card, as the level-up does in play. Start does the same for a session that opens at a start level. Without this, a jumped run plays a deep level with the base weapon, which makes it both too slow to clear and unlike the real level.

The bonus card

The game-over screen can offer a retry with a bonus card, bought with a rewarded ad (IFailureOffers.TryBonusRetryAsync, see Game/Run/README.md). When that retry's reset runs (IRetryState.RetryWithBonusCard), ResetRun deals one card by weight and publishes BonusCardDealtMessage with the stacks the attempt plays with (held plus the bonus); the on-screen tooltip names it once play resumes (see UI/Tooltip/README.md), or the card reveal shows it when its view is attached (Game/CardReveal/README.md).

Rule Why
Never an Extra Pen An ad never buys the ball cap (Plans/PLAN-FastPaced.md §8)
Never a maxed card So a bonus never takes a card past its max
Applied on top of the held cards, not counted in StacksOf It isn't a pick: it doesn't fill a card's stacks or change the next hand
Dropped when the attempt ends: on Transitioning (the level is won) and on any reset The advantage is per attempt, so repeated ads never stack power. An aborted level-up doesn't end the attempt, so it keeps the bonus
Seeded by the level and the retries used Each bought retry of a level can deal a different card

CanDealBonus (its IBonusCardSource side) is false once only Extra Pen and maxed cards are left, and the screen then hides the bonus button.

Not built yet

  • A pick for the bonus card. It's dealt at random and named in the tooltip; a quick pick screen (three cards, as at a level-up) is banked for later.
  • Stable card ids. Held stacks are keyed by the card's settings object, which is enough while nothing persists or reports cards. Saving a run, per-card telemetry or staging a card across a reset will need an id on IUpgradeCardSettings.

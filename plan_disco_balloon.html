Disco Balloon

A rare third reward balloon: a disco ball. It is plated with mirror tiles that catch the scene light and reflect the board, and rainbow god rays turn behind it. Like the golden balloon, only a piercing shot or a laser beam breaks it. Broken, it sends a disc out across the board, and every balloon the disc's edge reaches is nudged, gives a small burst, and is painted rainbow. Balloons that resist paint keep their colour.

No changes until this plan is actioned.

Goals

  1. A third reward balloon, placed by RewardBalloonService and never by a level's mix, like the golden and heart balloons (Plans/PLAN-RewardBalloons.md). It is rare, and there is at most one on the board.
  2. It breaks like the golden balloon. An ordinary hit from the shot bounces off it. A piercing shot plows it, and it shatters at the shot's next wall. A laser beam breaks it at once, and so does a bomb's blast beside it, as it breaks an unbreakable. Lightning and paint have no effect. It has no colour and no score of its own, resists paint, and carries no item.
  3. Its pop paints the board rainbow, as a wave. A disc grows out from where the ball was. Each balloon the edge reaches is nudged away from the ball, gives a small pop burst, plays the paint drip, and is painted rainbow. Paint-immune balloons are nudged and play the drip, but the paint runs off and their colour stays, exactly as under the Paint item.
  4. The wave never feeds its own shot. The shot that broke the ball is often still flying when the board turns rainbow. It must not climb its own streak on balloons it just painted (see Self-feeding check).
  5. The look: a mirror ball that reflects the board, its tiles flashing as they turn through the light, lit by the scene light (bright at noon, dim and tinted at night), with rainbow god rays behind it.

When it comes

It comes reward-style, through RewardBalloonService. It does not come from a level-mix weight.

A weight in the level's mix would bring the ball back again and again on any level that allows it, and the mix's caps count balloons, not jackpots. The service already does what this balloon needs: at most one on the board, per-level rolls, a completion threshold, and a guaranteed first meeting. A level that rolls one sees it at most once.

It follows the golden balloon's rules with its own numbers (decided 2026-11-15): a rare roll on an ordinary level, sure on a bonus (purple) level, a little more often than the golden balloon and less than the heart. The two plated balloons share one schedule (Balloon/Reward/PlatedRewardSchedule), so the rules can't drift apart. All the tuning lives in IRewardBalloonSettings:

Setting Disco Golden Why
SureDiscoLevel 8 5 Every player meets one with no roll, early in the level (SureDiscoAtCompletion 0.15). After the golden balloon's level, so the player already knows only a pierce breaks a plated balloon
OrdinaryDiscoEveryLevels 1 1 Off a purple level, every level from the sure level on rolls once, on its first line. A level before the sure one never rolls, so the sure level is the first sight. Was every 4th (disco) and 5th (golden) at 0.45 and 0.4, about one level in nine and one in twelve: too rare off bonus levels (the owner, 2026-11-16)
OrdinaryDiscoChance 0.25 0.2 The roll's chance at the sure level, halved OrdinaryChanceHalvedAfterLevels (20) levels past it, a third at 40 past
DiscoPityLevels 4 4 Hard pity: after four levels in a row without one placed, the next ordinary level brings one with no roll. Late in the run, where the eased chance rarely wins, they come on this beat
DiscoAtCompletion 0.35 0.5 Earlier than the golden balloon: the rainbow board pays out goals, and needs goals left to pay (Open questions, 1)
PurpleDiscoAtCompletion 0.25 0.15 On a purple level, rolled every PurpleRollEveryLines (6) lines from a quarter done
PurpleDiscoChances 1, 0.25, 0.15 0.35, 0.2, 0.1 The first is sure, so every purple level gets one. The next two are rolls, rarer than the heart's (0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.12, 0.06)

When more than one reward is due on the same line, the golden balloon goes first (it already beats the heart), then the disco balloon, then the heart. A reward that is due but finds no room on its line stays owed to the next line, as the other two do.

Breaking it

These are the golden balloon's rules, through the shared PlatedBalloonModel:

  • The shot's ordinary hit deflects: BalloonDeflectedMessage, the deflect nudge, and the deflect stamp.
  • A piercing shot (a pierce earned in cruise, or a Snipe lance) plows it. It pops at the shot's next wall discharge, as the golden balloon does, and the wave starts from the ball's slot at that moment.
  • A laser beam pops it at once (DamageFlags.Beam).
  • A bomb's blast carries Piercing, so it pops the ball, as it pops an unbreakable beside it (changed 2026-11-15 at the owner's ask; it first did nothing). Lightning, paint and snipe blooms do nothing. A board clear or a level change takes the ball away with no wave: only a pop pays, as with the other rewards.

Its pop scores nothing and leaves the streak alone, because the ball has no colour. Everything it gives comes from the wave.

The wave

The lines after it. The party outlasts the wave: the pop owes the next clock lines rainbow (DiscoWaveSettings.RainbowLines, 3 by default; asked for on 2026-11-15). RainbowLines holds the count, and BalloonSpawner hands it each clock line's new balloons once they are placed, before the item roll. Each line that brings a balloon pays one, by the wave's rules (PaintTargets): a paintable balloon arrives rainbow and a paint-immune one keeps its look. A line that brings none, because the board is full, doesn't count, so every line owed arrives. A second pop tops the count up to the setting instead of adding to it. A level change or a run reset forgets what is still owed. The painted balloons are booked to the breaking shot while it flies, so the self-feeding check holds for them too.

Shape and timing. The disc is centred on the ball's position at the moment of the pop. It does not follow the board afterwards, so a balloon line arriving mid-wave doesn't drag the disc with it.

  • R_max is the distance from that centre to the farthest corner of the board's slot bounds, plus one balloon radius. So a ball in a corner still covers the whole board, and the wave lasts the same time wherever the ball was.
  • R(t) = R_max × EaseOutQuad(t / T), with T = DiscoWaveSeconds (0.9 s by default). It starts fast, so the balloons beside the ball turn almost together, then slows, so the far edge of the board reads as a ripple travelling outward. A fixed duration keeps the wave short on a big board and makes it easy to tune.
  • Each frame, the wave visits every occupied slot inside this frame's radius that it hasn't visited yet. It keeps a set of the models it has already visited, so a balloon that moves between frames is never painted twice, and one that slides into ground the disc has already covered is reached on the next frame.
  • When t reaches T, a final sweep reaches every balloon still on the board and not yet visited. The spec is "every balloon on screen", and the sweep is what makes that true.

What each reached balloon gets. The checks are the Paint item's, through PaintTargets:

The balloon Nudge Burst Drip Painted
Paintable, any concrete colour yes yes, its old colour rainbow rainbow
Already rainbow yes no no stays rainbow
IResistsPaint (tough, tougher, unbreakable, golden, heart) yes no yes, the paint runs off no
Not a balloon (gatekeeper, bush and other grid actors) only if it is IHasNudge no no no
Leaving the board (mid-pop, pooled) no no no no
  • Nudge: NudgeMessage(actor, centre, NudgeType.Wave). The direction comes out from the centre, and the distance and duration come from a Wave override on DiscoWaveSettings, which a prefab's own overrides can beat. It uses no falloff: every balloon gets the same push as the edge passes, so the ripple reads the same right across the board.
  • Burst: a small cosmetic burst in the balloon's old colour, as if that colour pops off and leaves the rainbow underneath. The balloon is not popped: nothing is scored, it doesn't leave the grid, and no ActorHitMessage is sent. It is IPaintReactive.PlayRecolourBurst(Color, scale), which BalloonView plays as the pop's particle at DiscoWaveSettings.BurstScale, plus a DisturbanceField stamp (StampSource.Paint) tagged with the old colour, like the Paint item's blob landing. Using the real pop path is ruled out: BalloonPopPresenter.Present drives the outcome VFX of an actual pop.
  • Drip: IPaintReactive.PlayPaintDrip, resolved from the grid as PaintItemHandler.ApplyPaint does. The colour is committed first, so the drip reads as paint settling. Today a rainbow drip is white (GamePalette.GetColor returns white for the wildcard). Phase 4 gives the PaintDrip shader the rainbow bands (Include/RainbowBand.cginc, the level's own colours), so it matches the body underneath.
  • Paint: IPaintable.Color.Value = GamePalette.RainbowColorId. Then BalloonView.ApplyColorMode swaps the body to the rainbow material, as for any balloon painted rainbow. The wave records each one in ShotPaintLedger against the shot that broke the ball.

A level ending mid-wave. DiscoWave is IRunResettable and cancels the wave on a run reset, a level change or a game over. It despawns the disc and paints nothing more. A balloon the wave already painted stays rainbow.

Time scale. The wave runs on scaled time, so a slow-motion beat (the pierce discharge's slow-mo, if it overlaps) slows the ripple too and keeps it in step with the rest of the board.

Self-feeding check (the rainbow multiplier trap)

The known trap: a shot's own effect paints balloons rainbow, and then the same shot pops them. Each pop is a wildcard pop (CarryStreak, or DeferredStreak for a colourless shot), and ColorStreak.RecordWildcard raises the streak with no cap. This has been fixed twice already: once for the side neighbours in ProjectileHitResolver, and once for the forward exclusion in SnipeDischargeBloom. The disco ball is the worst case yet, because it paints the whole board.

Does the same shot hit what it painted? Yes, on every way it can break:

  1. Pierce. The ball pops at the shot's next wall, and the shot then bounces straight back into a board that is turning rainbow in front of it. If a Snipe charge was banked, the shot even re-arms its pierce at that moment and plows through it.
  2. Laser. The beam's own pops are item hits, with no DirectHit, so the beam can't climb the streak. But the shot whose hit fired the laser keeps flying, and it is the one that would harvest. The laser's DamageContext.Projectile names that shot.
  3. Several shots can be in flight at once (rapid fire). The shot that broke the ball is the one to guard. Any other shot, and every later throw, pops the rainbow board as it is meant to. That is the reward.

Why the earlier fixes don't carry over. Both of them worked by geometry: they skipped balloons ahead of the shot. A whole-board wave has no "behind": the shot is inside the disc, and the bounce can send it anywhere.

The guard: ShotPaintLedger. For each conversion the wave records the pair (the shot that broke the ball, the balloon painted). The shot comes from the pop's DamageContext.Projectile, which may be null for a cheat, in which case nothing needs guarding. In ProjectileHitResolver, when isWildcardPop is true and the ledger holds (this projectile, this balloon):

  • it adds neither CarryStreak nor DeferredStreak, and arms no carry;
  • the pop scores at whatever multiplier is already live, and the streak neither climbs nor breaks. It needs a new DamageFlags.OwnPaint, read by ScoreController.RecordStreakMultiplier as "return the current multiplier, change nothing". The Log.Assert that guards against items authoring streak flags covers it;
  • the pop refunds no streak shield. A continued streak pays a shield on every pop from x2, so a painter at x2 or more would otherwise refill its shields off its own paint and fly on through the rainbow board;
  • the balloon still pays its rainbow attribution to every allowed colour. Goal progress is not the trap: progress is still earned one pop at a time. The guard is only about the uncapped multiplier and the shields.

A shot carrying the rainbow buff (isRainbowBuff) climbs on any pop anyway. That comes from the buff, not from the disco ball, so the ledger doesn't change it.

The ledger lives in one place for any shot that paints, and it is keyed by model reference (models are built fresh for each spawn, never pooled). It drops a shot's entries when the shot despawns. Retrofitting the two existing guards onto it is a possible follow-up, but not part of this plan: they work, and they keep the board's colour for the shot's next hit.

The solver has to mirror the rule (ShotSimulator), or it will rate a disco shot by a streak it can never cash in. It paints the board the moment the simulated ball pops, marking each balloon as its own shot's paint (ShotBalloonState.OwnPaint). Live, the disc takes 0.9 s to cross the board and a pierced ball only pops at the next wall, so the solver lets the rest of the flight score painted balloons a little early.

The look

The body: Balloon/DiscoBall.shader. It starts from GoldenBalloon.shader's structure (the sphere projection, the scene light helpers in Include/SceneLight.cginc, and the glint-gated glitter), on a prefab built from the golden balloon's, so it keeps the knot and reads as a balloon.

  • Tiles. A latitude–longitude grid of small square mirrors over the sphere, with thin aluminium grout between them. The tiles get narrower toward the poles, as on a real disco ball. The grid turns about once every five seconds around the vertical axis (_SpinSpeed, 1.2 rad/s), so the ball reads as spinning: tiles slide across the face and over the limb. Each tile is flat: its normal is the sphere normal at the tile's centre, quantized. That one choice is what makes it read as a mosaic and not as chrome.
  • Reflection. Each tile samples _SceneCaptureTex, the board as SceneCaptureService renders it, which the unbreakable balloon already uses. The sample is offset by the tile's flat normal, so each mirror shows its own flat piece of the board, and the pieces jump from tile to tile. The variant calls Acquire() and Release() on the capture when it is enabled and disabled, as UnbreakableBalloonVariant does, so the capture camera only runs while a ball is on screen. A cheaper fallback, if the capture costs too much on device: reflect the golden balloon's horizon gradient instead (Open questions, 4).
  • Lit by the scene light. Each tile's colour is multiplied by the light field sampled at the ball (*At(worldPos)), so the ball is bright at noon, dim at night, and takes on the colour of the local light from a nearby pop or the unbreakable's glow. Each tile also gets a sharp specular toward SceneLightDirection(). As the grid turns, single tiles pass through the light angle and flash. Those flashes are the disco-ball sparkle, and they follow the time of day. The glitter is gated on the same flash, so it only sparkles where the light catches, as on the golden balloon.
  • DiscoBallLook (Balloon/Type/, an IBalloonViewBinding) pushes a random spin phase through a MaterialPropertyBlock and handles the capture ref-count. It is a binding, not a variant: BalloonView takes one variant per balloon, and the prefab keeps the tough balloon's, as the golden one does. The shader reads the ball's centre from its own transform, so nothing else is pushed. GPU instancing is off on the material, as on the unbreakable, because the phase is set per instance.

The god rays: rainbow. A GodRays child on the root, as on the golden balloon, driven by GodRaysLook, sorted at -1 on the Balloons layer (below every balloon), on TransparentFX. GodRays.shader gets a _RainbowRays toggle. When it is on, each ray's colour is RainbowBandColor(rayIndex), the level's own allowed colours, the same bands as the rainbow balloon's body, so the rays show exactly what the ball will turn the board into. The DiscoRays material holds the shape: more rays than the golden balloon's, narrower, and visibly spinning: _SpinSpeed 0.9 rad/s, above the golden balloon's 0.6 and the heart's 0.45, with the second fan counter-rotating at 0.7x as the shader already does. A new DiscoRays palette entry holds the strength in its alpha.

The disc: Sprite/DiscoWave.shader on DiscoWaveView. A quad centred on the pop and sized to the disc each frame, so the shader only runs over the ground the disc covers, with the radius and the progress pushed each frame. It draws a soft wash inside the disc that fades as the disc grows, and a bright edge band whose colour runs through RainbowBandColor by angle, turning slowly. It sorts with the god rays, below the balloons, so it sweeps through the gaps, and the balloons themselves show the edge as they flip to rainbow. A short SceneLightFieldService.Flash at the pop (the rainbow balloon's pop's, in BalloonPopPresenter) lights the board for a beat. The drip on each painted balloon is a rainbow one: the PaintDrip shader's per-instance _Rainbow draws the level's colours in diagonal bands, and the Paint item's rainbow holder now drips the same way.

Its pop. The ball shatters into mirror tiles: PSVFX_DiscoPop, which is the golden balloon's shatter using a disco_parts sheet cut from a capture of the ball by Tools/art/shatter, lit through Particle/SceneLit.

Sound. BalloonDeflectDisco (a glassy tick), and BalloonPopDisco (the golden balloon's chime layered with a shimmer). The wave gets a rising glissando through the board's key, and each balloon it paints gets a quiet tick pitched by its distance from the centre, capped so a full board doesn't stack fifty clips. All of these are layered from clips already in the sound bank, as the golden balloon's cues are.

As built: the glissando is harp notes (harp005) walking two octaves of the key, a degree for each tenth of the wave's time, from an octave under the root to an octave over it. A slow frame that crosses several notes plays only the last, and a wave shorter than about 0.6 s would crowd its notes into the burst window below. The last sweep's balloons all arrive in one frame and sound one tick. The ticks are the same harp, shorter and quieter, on the root's octave, at the degree the balloon's distance reaches. The cap is the tick's cooldown in the bank (0.07 s): stronger than one a frame, and it keeps back-to-back ticks out of SfxThrottleGate's burst window, which would detune them off the key. Both entries walk with a four-octave window, because ScaleWalkUp folds its top notes back near the edge of its window, and ShippedDiscoSoundsTests pins every degree climbing. The deflect is a ping-pong bump under Chime 0011 an octave over the board's key; the pop is the golden balloon's pop with the item-sighted wind chime over it. Picked without listening, so they want a pass by ear.

Open questions

  1. How big is the jackpot? A rainbow pop pays its full score to every allowed colour (BalloonModel.ResolveRainbowAttribution). A whole board turned rainbow lets the next two or three shots fill most of a level's goals. The ledger stops the streak trap but deliberately leaves this alone. Is a near-level-skip what you want from it? The levers, if not: place it earlier in the level (so there's more left to fill), make it rarer, or have the wave skip a share of the board. Skipping goes against the spec, so the plan doesn't propose it. The bot measurement in Phase 6 is the evidence: where the ball was popped, the level took about half the time and half the shots (Measured below). That's a big jackpot, but not a skip.
  2. The next throw's streak. Every later throw climbs one step per rainbow pop, with no cap. The streak resets on each load, so it is bounded by a single shot, but a pierce through a rainbow board will set a record multiplier. The streak cap was tried and reverted (score is not difficulty), so the plan accepts this as the reward unless you say otherwise. Measured: where the ball popped, the level's peak streak about doubled (22 and 23 against 10 and 11), and it went no higher than the control's best on another level (23).
  3. Items on painted balloons. An item hosted on a balloon the wave paints becomes the rainbow version (a rainbow bomb converts its survivors, a rainbow laser, a rainbow Snipe lance blooms). The board's items all go rainbow together and can cascade. Do you want that, or should a hosted item keep its concrete version?
  4. Reflection cost. SceneCaptureService renders a second camera while a ball is on screen. The unbreakable balloon already pays this cost, and the two share one capture. If a device build shows the cost, fall back to the golden balloon's horizon reflection.
  5. The silhouette. The plan keeps the balloon outline and knot, as the reward plan decided for the golden balloon, so it reads as a balloon. The alternative is a true sphere, like the unbreakable, which looks more like a disco ball but less like a balloon.
  6. Dancing lights on the board. A real disco ball throws moving specks of light around the room. Two or three tagged scene lights orbiting the ball while it's on the board would do that. They are left out of the phases as a stretch goal, because the light field is shared and busy.
  7. Purple levels: settled (2026-11-15). Each purple level gets a sure one; the look on paper is checked in Phase 4.

Measured

On 2026-11-15, with the rainbow lines in, the playtest bot played three seeds (--purple --seed N, average skill, 300 s each). It played each once as configured and once with no disco ball (--no-disco). A purple level brings a sure disco ball. No session hit a game over.

  • The bot is not a person, and two popped levels are thin evidence. Rerun with more seeds, or after a tuning change, using the same two flags.

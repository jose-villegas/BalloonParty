Effects Layer Clearing

Working plan for keeping every ball in flight readable: the effects drawn above the board render into their own layer (a render target, the "effects map"), and that layer is laid over the frame through a clearing mask that opens a hole around each ball. The hole lingers and shrinks after the ball has gone by.

Orientation

A shot is hard to follow once the board gets busy. Score shapes tumble over the balloons, trails stream to the score bar, heads-ups flash up, and item effects bloom. The ball, and the balloons it is hitting, vanish under all of it.

Earlier attempts changed the objects themselves. They pushed score shapes away from the ball, bent trails around it, faded effects inside a small circle and moved the ball's sorting layer up and down. None of it read on a phone, and all of it was removed. This plan doesn't touch any object. It changes how the frame is put together:

  • The board (the backdrop and ground, balloons, obstacles, balls and the thrower) renders as it does today.
  • Everything above the board is effects. Effects stop drawing straight into the frame. They draw into an effects map, a render target of their own.
  • The effects map is laid over the board through a clearing mask: a small screen-space texture holding a clearing value at every point. Where the mask is clear, the effects are see-through.
  • Every frame, each ball in flight stamps the mask with a soft disc along the path it just covered. Stamps decay over time. Because a stamp is strongest at its centre, the cleared region shrinks inward as it decays: a clearing lingers behind the ball and closes up after it.

"Ball" here is the projectile (Projectile/), the thing the player fires. The score formations' glowing trail heads are also called pens in Game/Score/Behaviours/. They are effects, and get cleared like any other.

The layer split

The board is the set of physics layers that are the board today: Scenario, Balloons, Grid, Projectile, Thrower, Items (the badges on balloons) and Limits. Effects are what draws above it. They sit on two cameras, and that decides how they split:

  • The main camera draws the board and some effects, and then post-processes: bloom, then the tonemapper.
  • The HUD camera (Display/HudCamera) draws on top of the finished, tonemapped frame. It draws the canvases, heads-ups and the score, heart and highlight trails.

An effect has to be composited where it draws today. If a HUD effect moved into the main camera's map, the tonemapper would recolour it, which is what the HUD camera exists to stop. So there are two maps, one per camera, both read through the same mask.

The main camera's effects live on several physics layers that also hold board pieces (Items holds the badges as well as item VFX, Projectile the ball as well as the shield VFX). So this phase needs a new physics layer, Effects (17), added in the Editor (TagManager.asset is never edited by hand). The chaos effects above are on it; the rest of the table would move to it. The composite collapses these effects to one sorting position: Sky −1, below the danger walls and the light quad. One visible change comes with that: item VFX on PowerUps draw under bushes and puffs (GridActors) today, and would draw over them.

The clearing mask

One low-resolution, two-channel target: RGHalf (ARGBHalf, then ARGB32, where a device lacks it), screen size divided by MaskDownscale (default 4, so 270 × 600 on a 1080 × 2400 phone; the glass below reads its slope, which shows steps at 8 bits or past 4). Red is how cleared a point is; green is the radius of the stamp that set it, so the glass knows how big the dome under each point is. Two of them ping-pong. Once a frame, from LateUpdate (blits stay outside URP's RenderGraph, as Display/README.md requires), one blit:

  1. Decay: mask = max(0, previous − Δt / LingerSeconds).
  2. Stamp: for each of up to MaxStamps capsules (8, the shader's fixed array), mask = max(mask, profile(d / Radius)), where d is the distance from the fragment to the capsule's segment, Radius that stamp's own, and profile(x) = 1 − x² inside the radius, 0 outside. Green takes the radius of whichever stamp, or last frame's value, won.

The composite reads the mask bilinearly and fades the map by fade = 1 − Strength × smoothstep(EdgeLow, EdgeHigh, mask). Because the stamp peaks at the centre and the decay is uniform, the visible edge (where mask crosses EdgeLow) moves inward over time. That is the decaying radius. The clearing's visible radius at age t is about \(R\sqrt{1 - E - t/L}\), with \(E\) the edge and \(L\) the linger time, so it closes up at \(t = L(1 - E)\).

The glass

The cut alone read too strong to the owner, so the clearing is drawn as a dome of thick glass over each ball. A stamp's profile, 1 − (d/R)², is a dome's height, so the composite takes the mask's slope (four more mask taps, a few texels apart) as the glass's surface:

  • Refraction. The map is sampled toward the top of the dome, by LensRefraction of the dome's radius at a fresh rim, so what lies under the glass reads magnified and spreads away from the ball, and a small dome is the same glass as a big one. The bend eases in over the glass's outer edge, so the rim meets what lies outside it without a tear.
  • Dispersion. Red and blue sample a little further and nearer than green (LensDispersion, a fraction of the bend): a colour fringe where the glass is steep.
  • Fade. Effects under the glass fade from the rim in: EdgeLow 0 and EdgeHigh 0.9 spread the fade over the whole dome, so they thin slowly from the rim and are near gone only over the ball, which stays readable.
  • Glint. A mirror highlight off the dome's normal and a thin bright rim (LensHighlight) are added light, so the dome shows over the board too, not only over effects. The light is the scene's (Include/SceneLight.cginc): the highlight sits on the side of the dome the light comes from and takes the light's colour and strength, so it dims and tints with the time of day; the rim keeps its authored brightness. It is the glass's, not a map's: each composite quad stays on while a clearing is open, with a transparent texture standing in for a map not rendered that frame. Both composites add it, the main one before bloom and the tonemapper and the HUD's after; that second glint came with the main map (tuned at 0.6 when the HUD's was the only one), and is kept so the look is unchanged.

Decay subtracts evenly, so an older part of the dome keeps its slope and shrinks inward: the corridor behind a ball is a glass tube that narrows and closes. Outside the glass the composite branches to one plain map tap. The glass bends only the effects map. The ball and the board under it stay sharp, which is the point.

How the stamps are fed:

  • EffectsClearingController loops IProjectileRegistry.InFlightCount / InFlightPositionAt(i) every frame. The registry already exists, is run-scoped and is cheap to walk.
  • EffectsClearingStamper remembers each ball's last position, keyed by its registry model, so a stamp is a capsule from last frame's position to this frame's. A fast ball leaves an unbroken corridor instead of a row of dots. A ball seen for the first time stamps a disc. A ball that retired drops out.
  • Positions go to viewport space through the main camera. The radius is in world units and converted with the camera's orthographic size, so a cinematic zoom scales the clearing with the board.
  • The radius follows the ball's shields, as the owner asked: MinClearRadius (0.6) at one shield, growing evenly with each shield to MaxClearRadius (1.2) at ShieldsForMaxRadius (10), and no wider past it. A ball with no shields left, on its last leg, clears as one. The rejected attempt used a circle a little wider than the ball, and it read as nothing, so one shield is still about a balloon wide.
  • Δt is game time (Time.deltaTime) while a ball flies: slow-mo stretches a clearing along with the ball, and a pause holds it. Once no ball is in flight it's real time, so a freeze that starts after the shot can't hold a clearing open. UseUnscaledTime puts the flight on real time too.

MaxStamps caps the uniform array. It's a constant, not a setting: a shader array's size is fixed when it compiles. More balls than that in flight (a split shot, say) keep the nearest MaxStamps to the screen's centre. Nothing in today's shots comes close.

Compositing

The effects camera is a runtime child of the camera it serves, like SceneCaptureService's capture camera. It has the same projection, mirrored every frame, culls only the effect layers, clears to transparent black and has post-processing off. Its target is the map: ARGB32 for the HUD and ARGBHalf for the main camera, whose colour buffer is HDR and so keeps values above 1 for bloom.

Effects render into the map premultiplied, with correct coverage in alpha. Most shaders already do that: URP's sprite shaders blend alpha with One OneMinusSrcAlpha, the built-in Sprites/Default and TMP are premultiplied, and Particle/Glow and Particle/SceneLit set alpha blending of their own. Shaders that write Blend [_SrcBlend] [_DstBlend] with no alpha term (Particle/LightResponsive, Particle/SpecularDrop, Display/TraceGlitterLine) would leave the wrong coverage. Each gets an explicit alpha blend: One OneMinusSrcAlpha when alpha-blended, Zero One when additive, so additive light adds no coverage. Phase 2 audits the materials actually in the HUD map. Phase 3 does the same for the main map.

The composite is one camera-fitted quad (built with QuadRendererBuilder, like the light overlay) with Blend One OneMinusSrcAlpha, returning map × fade. With the mask empty, fade is 1 and the result is the effects exactly as they drew before, the same blend into the same frame.

Idle

While no ball is in flight and the mask has fully decayed, a map would only reproduce the frame. So the compositor turns it off: the effect layers go back into the camera's own mask, the effects camera stops and the quad hides. The first ball fired turns it back on. The mask is empty on that frame, so the switch can't be seen. Aiming, the level-up and menus cost nothing extra.

Mobile cost

Measured against a 1080 × 2400 panel at 60 fps (2.6 M pixels). Real numbers come from the device, not from this table.

Item Per frame while live Notes
Mask blit 160 k fragments, 8 capsule tests each negligible
Mask sample in the composite 1 bilinear tap per pixel from a 650 kB texture stays in cache
The glass 4 more mask taps and 3 map taps (not 1) per pixel under a dome a few balls' worth of the screen
HUD map, ARGB32 about 10 MB stored + 10 MB read back ≈ 1.2 GB/s MapDownscale 2 quarters it, at the cost of softer lines. Default 1
Main map, ARGBHalf about 2.4 GB/s at full size the expensive one. Runs while a ball flies and something is alive on its layer, at most MapHoldSeconds after the last clearing; MapDownscale 2 quarters it
Extra camera per map one cull and one RenderGraph setup on the CPU, about a fifth of the main thread's frame on a device while both ran only on frames its layer has something alive (LayerPresence); a ball before its first pop, or a hold whose effects are done, renders none
Effects no longer drawn by their own camera a saving they draw once, into the map

The idle rule means none of this is paid while aiming. The HUD stack already pays one full-screen copy (see HudCamera). The HUD map is a second one of about that size, so the expected cost is "one more HUD camera" for the time a ball is in flight. Phase 2 measures it on the phone with the profiler before phase 3 is started.

Rejected on cost:

  • Re-rendering the board without effects and blending it back in around the ball. That's exact, but it doubles the board's draw cost (the sky scatter, the grass, every lit balloon) for every frame a ball flies.
  • Masking inside every effect shader with no map: the cheapest on the GPU, but every effect shader, including the stock sprite, particle and TMP ones, would need a project copy that samples the mask. Every future effect would have to remember to opt in. It also isn't the layered composition asked for.

Phases

Each phase follows the per-phase review protocol: implement, review, curate, owner signs off.

Phase 1: the clearing mask

  • IEffectsClearingSettings + EffectsClearingSettings SO (under Configuration/Effects/), registered in AppLifetimeScope: Enabled, MinClearRadius, MaxClearRadius, ShieldsForMaxRadius, LingerSeconds, EdgeLow, EdgeHigh, Strength, LensRefraction, LensDispersion, LensHighlight, MaskDownscale, MapDownscale, UseUnscaledTime, mask and composite shaders.
  • EffectsClearingStamper (pure) + EditMode tests: a first sighting stamps a disc, a moving ball a capsule from its last position, retired balls drop out, the radius scales with orthographic size, the cap keeps the most central balls.
  • EffectsClearingController (run-scoped) feeding EffectsClearingMaskView (persistent camera) and the EffectsClearingMask shader. The mask shows in Editor/Maps/GameRenderMapsWindow.
  • Nothing on screen changes yet. The mask can be watched in the maps window during a run.

Phase 2: the HUD effects map

  • EffectsMapCompositor + EffectsMapComposite shader, on the HUD camera, reading the Trail layer.
  • An alpha audit of the HUD map's materials, then an in-editor A/B with the mask forced empty: the frame must match the one without a map.
  • A stage.py command to hold a ball mid-flight (ball x,y --from x,y --seconds s), so a still can show a ball inside a burst of score shapes, with the clearing on and off.
  • A phone check: does the shot read now? And a profile of the cost.

As built (phases 1 and 2)

  • Alpha audit. The HUD map draws Sprites/Default (the trail heads; premultiplied, correct coverage), URP Unlit set to alpha blending with One OneMinusSrcAlpha on alpha (the highlight trail) and the project's PencilLine (premultiplied). The score trail's ribbon material (TrailMaterial_ScoreTrail) was on the built-in Mobile/Particles/Alpha Blended, which writes no correct coverage. It moved to Sprites/Default, which draws the same colour (texture × vertex colour, the material's tint white) and writes its coverage, so it looks the same drawn straight in.
  • Heads-ups stayed on the UI layer. Moving them to Trail would also put them in the GI scene capture, which renders Trail: heads-up text would start to shade the board under it. Open question 4.
  • The composite sorts at UI 3300, where the score trails are set, not at 100 as first planned.
  • The decay clock. Game time while a ball flies (slow-mo stretches the clearing, a pause holds it), real time once none does. Otherwise a freeze that starts just after a shot, the level-up's, would hold the hole open through the cinematic. UseUnscaledTime puts the flight on real time too.
  • No trail. Compared side by side on the phone, the owner chose a dome that stays on the ball over a corridor that lingers behind it. Trail on the settings asset is off: the stamper stamps a disc where each ball is (no capsule back to last frame's spot) and the mask is wiped each frame, so it holds only the domes where the balls are. A ball gone from flight (retired, or its last glide over) keeps a disc where it was last seen, faded a little more each frame (_Fades takes that much off its dome, as the decay would have), so its lens shrinks inward to nothing over LingerSeconds, the owner having liked that from the trail. The mask closes once none is left. On, the trail and its decay clock below come back.
  • Camera moves. The mask is in screen space, so when the level-up rig pans and zooms in on the ball, last frame's stamps would stay where they were on screen, a stale dome off the ball, and the cinematic's slow-mo barely decays them. Each blit reads last frame's mask through the camera's change since (ClearingView.ReprojectionFrom) and scales its radii by the zoom, so the stamps stay on the world.
  • Update order. The mask view runs after every LateUpdate that moves the camera ([DefaultExecutionOrder] 1000), and the compositor right after it, so the stamps frame the view this frame renders and the map turns on in the same frame as the first stamp.

Phase 3: the main effects map (in part)

Started for the chaos effects, which open question 1 had put in the HUD map: there the lightning and the laser, made to pass the bloom threshold (_HdrBoost 2–3), were clipped to 1 with no bloom. The Effects physics layer is added, the pops, item effects and nuke cloud are on it, and the main camera has its own EffectsMapCompositor (ARGBHalf, composite at Sky −1, before post). The composite shader reads the map in half, since fixed is low precision on some mobile GPUs. The GI capture takes Effects, as it took them on Trail. The rest of the table above (range previews, shield VFX, the dust specks) waits on open question 2.

Off, the main camera draws these effects in their own sorting places: under the ball, and the pops on Default under the balloons. So the map stays on for MapHoldSeconds (1.5, game time) after the mask empties, past the pops a shot set off, and no shard mid-air slips under the balloons when it turns off.

The main camera's culling mask has two writers: NavigationCameraReveal replaces the whole mask on each navigation change, and the compositor takes Effects out while it runs. So the mask has one owner, CameraCullingMask, the way TimeScaleService owns the time scale: navigation sets the base mask (the game mask includes Effects), compositors claim the layers they suppress, and the camera draws the base mask minus the claims.

Out of scope

  • Anything that moves, bends or fades individual objects. That approach was tried and removed.
  • Clearing the HUD itself: the canvases, the score bar and the hearts counter.
  • Clearing board pieces: clouds, bushes, puffs, danger walls, the wall net.
  • Sorting-layer reshuffles.
  • Other sources of clearings (impacts, items going off). The stamp list is the seam for them later.
  • Per-device resolution tiers for the maps. MapDownscale is a single setting for now.

Open questions for the owner

  1. Pops. Answered: pops go to the upper layer and clear like the other effects. All nine PSVFX_*Pop prefabs (the default pop, which also plays for recolours and bubble merges, and the heart, golden, disco, nuke, soap, tough, tougher and unbreakable pops and armour breaks) are on Effects, so the main camera's map takes them and they draw over the board, balls included, except under the glass. They went to the HUD map on Trail first, which cost them the main camera's bloom and grading, so phase 3 started for them. Their legacy Mobile/Particles/Alpha Blended materials moved to Sprites/Default, which draws the same colour and writes coverage. The nuke blast's flood and the disco wash are discs on the ground beneath the balloons, and the blast lights the board itself, so they stay on the board: the glass can't lift a light.

    The owner widened it to the chaos a ball flies through: lightning, bombs, paint, the laser and the nuke. The item effects (the bomb's smoke burst, the laser, the lightning chain, the paint splash, drips and flying blobs) and the nuke cloud moved to Effects too, the bomb smoke and the paint splash off the legacy particle shader, and PaintBlob writes coverage. They draw over the board and over the ball now, as the pops do, and the glass opens a hole around the ball. The ball itself already drew above them all on the board, so what they hid was what it was about to hit. The shield item's gain burst stays: it's news about the ball. So does the lightning's glow (LVFX_LightningRange/Glow, on Items): a light on the ground under the balloons, which the map, laying everything over them, would lift over them.

  2. The rest of phase 3. The score shapes and trails are the HUD map, the chaos effects the main map. Should range previews, shield VFX and the dust specks clear as well? The main map already runs while a ball flies, so they would add only their own draws into it.
  3. How clear is clear. Answered: a hard cut near the rim read too strong. The clearing is now the glass dome above, its effects thinning slowly from the rim to near nothing over the ball, with the lens the owner picked from stills (refraction 0.5, dispersion 0.15). It still needs a look in play.
  4. Heads-ups. They are on the UI layer with the canvases, so the HUD map can't take them alone. One way is an Effects-style physics layer for them, which you'd add in the Editor (the same one phase 3 needs). The other is the Trail layer, which needs no new layer but puts their text into the GI capture, so it shades the board a little under each line. Which one, or should they stay as they are?

Nuke Balloon

A fourth reward balloon: a nuke. It is plated in dull lead and bolted together, with a riveted danger placard on its face, a small LED counter and a status lamp that flares green. Shots bounce off it and dent it. The third dent, or any piercing damage, arms it. The lamp turns red, the counter runs down from 3 with a beep each second, and the board leans in towards it. At zero it goes off. A blinding light floods the board behind a growing shock disc that pops every balloon and every shot in flight. A mushroom cloud rises where it stood. Then the spawn clock gives the breather a big clear earns, on a gust of wind. It is rare, and it comes when the player is in trouble: a full board, a fast clock, few hearts, or hearts lost one after another.

Goals

  1. A rescue, placed by RewardBalloonService and never by a level's mix, like the golden balloon, the heart balloon and the disco ball (Plans/PLAN-RewardBalloons.md, Plans/PLAN-DiscoBalloon.md). At most one on the board.
  2. Its odds follow trouble. A tiny chance on any line, raised by four signals: the board's fill, the spawn clock's pressure, low hearts, and hearts lost in quick succession.
  3. It is heavy. The shot's ordinary hit bounces off and dents it. The third dent arms it, and so does any Piercing damage at once (a pierce, a bomb's blast beside it, a laser beam).
  4. Armed, it counts down. It stays where it is for a 3 s fuse. Its lamp turns red and its LED counts 3, 2, 1 with a beep on each, while the balloons around it lean slowly in.
  5. At zero it clears the board. Every balloon but the other rewards pops, scoring nothing, and every shot in flight dies. Then the spawn clock starts the breather a big clear earns.
  6. The spectacle. The disco wave's growing disc, with light that blinds the board as it spreads. The disturbance field's strongest push. A nudge outward running just ahead of the disc. A mushroom cloud rising where the nuke stood. The owner flagged the cloud as the hard part.
  7. The bar the other rewards set. Depth (the plated sphere's relief, a baked ground shadow), the scene light (lit by the time of day, like the golden balloon), and its own sounds.

Decisions

These were open in the brief. Each is decided here, and each can be changed.

Question Decision Why
Does it take hits like the golden balloon? Like the golden balloon, plus dents. Piercing damage or a beam arms it at once. The shot's ordinary hit bounces off it and costs one of its hits (the catalogue entry's hit count, 3). The last one arms it The nuke is a rescue. The player who needs it is losing, and that player rarely has a pierce. The measured disco ball shows this: the average bot left half of its balls unpopped because only a pierce breaks one. Dents keep the lead feel (shots bounce off) and give every player a way in. The tough balloon's crack overlay shows the dents for free
What does an armed nuke do when hit? It deflects every ball, piercing or not, and takes no more damage. Item damage does nothing It is a lead casing with a fuse running: nothing more can happen to it. A deflect bounces a piercing ball and keeps its pierce, so a pierce isn't wasted on it. It never absorbs, which would kill the ball
Does it move while armed? No. It is anchored (IBalanceAnchor): the balancer, the wave-start roam and pressure shoves leave it in its slot The counter has to be read where it is, and the blast's centre should not drift
What does "the whole board" clear? Every balloon except the other reward balloons. Tough, tougher and unbreakable balloons, formations and bubble clusters all pop. A golden balloon, heart balloon or disco ball stays where it is. Grid actors (bushes, gatekeepers, deflectors, absorbers) stay A reward the player hasn't collected yet shouldn't be destroyed by another reward. Grid actors are the level's terrain, not its threat
Do the cleared balloons score? No. No score, no goal progress, no streak. Items on cleared balloons don't fire The nuke buys time. It doesn't advance the level. Scoring a whole board would complete most of a level's goals, which is the disco ball's job. Firing every item on the board at once would be a second, uncontrolled blast
Which shots die? Every shot in flight, through its normal death (pierce discharge, death sound). The ball waiting in the cannon is kept "It will also clear any projectiles". The loaded ball is the player's next turn, not something on the board. The existing ForceDestroyProjectileMessage kills the loaded ball when none is in flight, so the nuke gets its own message that spares it
Order on a line The nuke first, then the golden balloon, the disco ball and the heart It is the rarest, and the most urgent
Party Favors (IWeaponStats.RewardBalloonOdds) Raises the nuke's chance like the other reward balloons' The nuke is one of the card's family. The card multiplies the trouble-driven chance, so a calm board's nuke stays near 0 and the boost lands only when the player is in trouble
What stops a fuse? A level change, a game over or a run reset, with no blast and no breather. A pause holds it: it runs on scaled time The same rule as the blast. A level the shot completes during the fuse ends as any level does, and the level-up takes the board

When it comes

Through RewardBalloonService, rolled on each clock line while the level is played, and only for a type the catalogue has an entry for. The numbers are INukeOddsSettings.

The gates.

  • From FromLevel (4) on. Early levels rarely put a player in trouble, and the first sight of a nuke should come after the player has learned the board.
  • Not while a nuke is on the board.
  • At most PerLevel (1) a level.
  • After one is placed, CooldownLines (150) lines pass before the next roll. Lines come every 2 s on the first level and every 0.55 s late in a run, so that is about two to five minutes.
  • A won roll stays owed until placed, as for the other rewards. A new level drops it.
  • Level SureLevel (10) always brings one, SureAfterLines (10) lines in so the board has filled: past the odds and the cooldown, so every run that gets that far sees a nuke. Not if one is already on the board, or the level has already had its PerLevel. 0 turns it off.

The chance. Each line rolls

chance = BaseChance + (MaxChance - BaseChance) × urgency

where urgency is the weighted sum of four signals, each 0 to 1, capped at 1:

Signal Reads 0 at 1 at Weight
Fill IBoardFill.Occupancy FillFrom (0.7) FillFull (0.95) FillWeight (0.5)
Spawn rate ISpawnClock.Pressure no pressure the clock at PressureSpeedup PressureWeight (0.25)
Hearts PlayerNeed.HeartsNeed full hearts the last heart HeartsWeight (0.5)
Hearts lost in succession the recent losses (below) none LossesForFull (2) LossWeight (0.5)
  • Recent losses. Each WaveDamageMessage adds its HeartsLost. Each clock line keeps LossKeptPerLine (0.75) of the sum. One heart lost reads 0.5, fading over the next lines; two lost on lines close together read near 1. A run reset clears it.
  • The numbers. BaseChance 0.0005 and MaxChance 0.04 per line. Out of trouble, a nuke comes about once in 2,000 lines, about an hour of play. A board 90% full on the last heart (urgency 1) rolls 4% a line, about one in 25 lines. Any two strong signals saturate it. These are starting values for the bot to measure.
  • The pressure signal is the odd one: pressure builds only while the player is comfortable and the board is low. It is there because the owner asked for "high spawning rates", and its weight keeps it a nudge.

Where it lands. The reward goes to the front of its line (BalloonSpawner.MoveRewardToFront), so it lands as long as any column takes a balloon. An almost-full board still takes it. A full board passes it on, owed, to the next line with room.

Arming it

  • The shot's ordinary hit deflects and costs one of its hits: BalloonDeflectedMessage, the deflect nudge and stamp, and the tough balloon's cracks (the prefab keeps ToughBalloonVariant, which draws them for any balloon with durability, and the body shader dents a plate for each). The last hit arms it, and that shot bounces off as from any dent.
  • A piercing shot plows it while it has more than one hit left and arms it at the shot's next wall, as the golden balloon pops there. With one hit left the pierce arms it on contact and bounces off, keeping its pierce.
  • A laser beam (DamageFlags.Beam) and a bomb's blast beside it (Piercing) arm it at once.
  • Lightning and paint do nothing (HitOutcome.None), as on the golden balloon.
  • A board clear or a level change takes it away unarmed. Only arming starts the fuse.

Armed, it deflects every ball for the rest of the fuse. A ball can ricochet off it, so the deflect chain counts those bounces (DeflectChainController). That is three seconds at most, and the shot dies at zero, so it stays as it is.

The fuse

NukeFuseSettings.Seconds (3). It starts on the hit that arms the nuke and runs on scaled time.

  • The counter. FuseSecondsLeft reads 3 at arming, then 2, then 1. Each change publishes NukeCountdownMessage, and each one beeps (Sound). The LED on the body shows the digit (The look).
  • The lamp. Unarmed, it pulses green, and the rays behind the nuke flare green with it. Armed, both turn red, and the lamp blinks once a second with the beep.
  • The pull. Twice a second, every balloon within PullRadius (4 world units) of the nuke gets a single-actor nudge towards it (NudgeType.Wave, a negative distance). The nudge grows from PullDistanceStart (0.04) to PullDistanceEnd (0.16) over the fuse, so the board seems to lean in harder as the count runs down. A disturbance-field implosion stamp at the nuke (negative strength, as the bomb's first stamp) pulls the clouds, grass and specks in with it.
  • The end. At zero, NukeBlast.Detonate(nuke).

The blast

NukeBlast.Detonate at the nuke's position:

  1. The nuke pops through IBalloonPopper (its pop VFX, its slot freed, its leave callback run), and NukeDetonatedMessage goes out for the boom.
  2. The shots in flight die. ClearShotsInFlightMessage makes ThrowerController kill every shot in flight through its normal death path. The loaded ball is kept.
  3. The push. The disturbance field's strongest stamp at the nuke: a radius as wide as the board, PushStrength above the bomb's, ramped over the disc's time, so clouds and grass blow out across the whole scene.
  4. The disc. NukeBlast grows a disc from there, as the disco wave does, in Seconds (1.0 s), fast first and slowing at the far edge. A balloon within NudgeLead (1.2) of the edge is nudged outward once (NudgeType.Wave), so the board flinches before the edge arrives. Each balloon the edge reaches:
    • pops through IBalloonPopper: its pop VFX play, its slot is freed, its leave callback runs, and no ActorHitMessage goes out. So nothing scores, no streak moves, and no item fires;
    • unless it is a reward balloon. Those were nudged ahead of the edge with the rest, and stay.
  5. The light. A scene light registered at the nuke, its radius following the disc and its intensity rising to LightIntensity (the field's ceiling) and falling over LightSeconds. The disc's own inside burns white past the HDR bloom threshold, so the board goes blinding white as the disc spreads, then clears.
  6. The cloud. NukeCloudView starts the mushroom cloud at the nuke's position (The look).
  7. The end. When the time is up, a last sweep pops every balloon still on the board, so a line that landed during the blast is cleared too. Then IBreatherGrant.Grant(), and NukeBreatherMessage for the wind.

A level change, a run reset or a game over stops the blast where it is, without the breather. A pause holds it: it runs on scaled time. A second nuke can't be on the board while one blast runs, but if a cheat places one, the first blast finishes at once.

The breather. The same breather a big clear earns: the clock drops to BreatherPace (0.2) and eases back over BreatherSeconds (11 s), then the cooldown (14 s) holds before another. A big clear would often start it anyway, from the fall in occupancy, but not inside the cooldown of an earlier one. The grant starts it whatever the cooldown. The sparse board's speedup still applies, so an empty board refills sooner than a full one even in the breather. That is the existing rule for any big clear.

The look

The body: Balloon/NukeBalloon.shader, a fork of GoldenBalloon.shader on a prefab built from the golden balloon's. It keeps the golden balloon's depth and light: the sphere projection, the relief of bevelled, tilted plates, the sky and floor reflection, and the scene light (Include/SceneLight.cginc). So it is dull at noon and near black at night, with its rim still catching the light. Its ground shadow is the prefab's baked shadow child, as the golden balloon's is.

  • Lead plating. The golden balloon's hexagonal plates, bolted at the corners over a dark rubber skin, in dull lead: a broad, weak sheen and no glitter.
  • Dents. Each dent sinks a growing share of the plates (_DamageProgress, which the prefab's ToughBalloonVariant pushes): a dented plate tips the other way and darkens.
  • The danger placard. A round steel plate riveted to the face: a black radiation trefoil on yellow, inside a black ring, held by three rivets between the blades. It takes the sphere's light, a soft sheen and some wear.
  • The LED counter. A small dark display below the placard with one red seven-segment digit, glowing past white so it blooms. Unarmed it shows a green heart. Armed it shows the seconds left, bright red.
  • The lamp. A small domed lamp above the placard. Unarmed it pulses green. Armed it is red and blinks with each beep. Both are drawn by the body shader from _FuseArmed and _FuseDigit, which NukeFuseView pushes, and both shine _FuseGlow (3) times past white.
  • The seam light. A thin line of light down the gaps between the plates, with a soft glow either side: HDR green while it waits, dim red once armed, flaring bright red on each count (_FuseBeat).
  • The LED, the lamp and the seam light are the only parts of a balloon that bloom: every other balloon colour is capped at white (Plans/PLAN-HDR), so the shader adds them after the cap.
  • The flare. God rays behind it (GodRaysLook, NukeRays), spinning like the other reward balloons'. One fan is green (palette NukeRays) and the other yellow (the material's second fan colour) while it waits; once armed the green fan turns red (palette NukeArmedRays).

The blast: Sprite/NukeBlast.shader on NukeBlastView: a quad sized to the disc each frame. A white inside that floods the board and fades as the disc slows. A white-hot core at the nuke. A ragged orange shock ring at the edge, and a scorch behind it, fading out at the end.

The mushroom cloud: Sprite/NukeCloud.shader on NukeCloudView, one square quad centred on the nuke (CloudReach), over CloudSeconds (5 s). The game is seen from above, so the cloud is too: no stem, the cap seen from over it. It is made of shapes, not a texture:

  • the cap, a dome swelling over the blast, its edge broken into billows by the tileable noise, which flows outward from the middle as the top of the cloud rolls over its rim;
  • its shadow, cast on the board away from the scene light;
  • a ring of dust thrown along the ground, racing out past the cap and thinning.

The dome is lit from the scene light's side, and so is each lump of smoke on it. At first the cap is fire, white-hot in the middle and orange at the edge (HDR, so it blooms), cooling to smoke. The smoke takes the scene light, so night leaves it dark. It is smoke, not a solid: only the thickest heaps hide the board, the thin edges and the folds let it show through, and light glows through the thin parts. Over its last stretch it thins, spreads a little and erodes away through the noise, edges first, until nothing is left.

Its pop. The nuke shatters into lead plates: PSVFX_NukePop, the golden balloon's shatter on a nuke_parts sheet cut by Tools/art/shatter from a capture of the nuke armed (its red LED on 1, the lamp and the seams red), so the shards carry the red seams, bits of the trefoil and the LED. They are lit through Particle/SceneLit with the balloon's own light knobs.

Sound. All layered from clips already in the sound bank, as the other rewards' cues are.

Open questions

  1. Dents or plated? Decided as dents (3 hits). If the owner wants the golden balloon's rule exactly, set the catalogue entry's hit count very high, or make NukeBalloonModel deflect forever. The bot measures how often the nuke is armed either way.
  2. Should the cleared balloons count toward the goals? Decided no. With yes, the nuke would end most levels on the spot.
  3. Items on cleared balloons. Decided they vanish with their balloon. The alternative fires them, which is spectacular and chaotic.
  4. The wind on every breather? Decided only on the nuke's, the moment the owner described. Any breather could have it.

Phases

Each phase follows the per-phase review: implement, then review, curate and quick fixes, then the owner's sign-off.

  1. Model and hit rule. Done. BalloonType.Nuke, IsReward, the factory, NukeBalloonModel, the heavy impact colour, the solver snapshot.
  2. Spawning. Done. The settings, NukeOdds, NukeSchedule and the service's order, the catalogue entry, the cheat, the bot's counts and --no-nuke.
  3. The blast and the breather. Done, as an instant blast on the pop: NukeBlast, IBreatherGrant, IBalloonPopper, BoardDisc.
  4. The look. Done for the body, the placard, the dents, the rays and the blast disc. Both shaders compile for Vulkan, GLES3, Metal and D3D.
  5. Sound. Done for the clank and the boom.
  6. The fuse. Done. Arming in the model (Armed, the deflect-only rule, IBalanceAnchor), NukeFuse, the messages, NukeFuseView, the LED and the lamp in the body shader, the green-to-red rays, the pull, the armed alarm and the beeps. The solver: arming deflects and counts, with no instant clear. Tests: each arming path, an armed nuke deflects every ball and takes no damage, it stays anchored, the count and its messages, a stop on level change, game over and reset.
  7. The detonation. Done. Detonate, ClearShotsInFlightMessage, the push, the nudge ahead of the edge, the blinding light, the white disc, the breather's wind. Tests: the shots in flight die and the loaded one stays, the nudge comes before the pop, the light follows the disc and is released.
  8. The mushroom cloud. Done, as seen from above: NukeCloud.shader, NukeCloudView and the NukeCloud material, first drawn side-on and redrawn top-down after the owner saw it. Recorded on a full board in the Editor at noon, with the nuke in the middle and in a corner; still to judge at night.
  9. Docs and measuring. The READMEs. The playtest bot on several seeds with and without nukes (--no-nuke): how often one is placed, how often armed, hearts lost per level and game overs, and the level times.

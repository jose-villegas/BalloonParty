Detailed Description

Unbreakable balloons normally roam: once per wave each one jumps toward the far side of the board. When three or more are on the board, they gather into a formation instead. They line up, stay in place for a few waves, then switch to a different line. While a formation stands, its members float in midair: the balloons below rise until they reach the formation, and spawn lines stop under it.

Several formations can stand at once. Unbreakables left over after one formation is full form a second one, so two lines can stack into a blockade. A formation the player leaves alone calls in more unbreakables, past the level's usual cap, and those form formations of their own.

Tough balloons (toughs and toughers) gather too, but only into one snake of their own. It starts from the toughest, every other tough falls in behind its tail, and it walks through the balloons in its way. See "Tough snakes" below.

How a formation runs

  1. Wave start. BalloonBalancer.Balance(atWaveStart: true) calls ArrangeAtWaveStart before the usual roam pre-pass. Roamers therefore see the formation's slots as already taken.
  2. Forming. If at least MinMembers unbreakables are free (in no formation), the service picks a shape for them. The pick is weighted at random from the shape weights. The snake's weight grows by SnakeWeightPerExtraUnbreakable of itself for each unbreakable on the board beyond MinMembers, so a crowded board snakes more often. It leaves out the shape just held, and ItemCover finds no spot unless a bomb, a snipe or a lightning is on the board. If the picked shape fits nowhere, the service tries the next one. If nothing fits, nothing forms and the unbreakables roam as usual. A formation takes at most MaxMembers (a horizontal line, HorizontalMaxMembers); an item cover takes three to six. While MinMembers or more are still free after it, they form another one.
  3. Moving in. The service hands each member's move to the balancer's relocate routine. That is the same routine roam jumps use, so the members glide in at their normal speed. Each member is marked in formation (IWriteableFormationMember.SetInFormation).
  4. Holding. The formation lasts HoldWaves waves, counting the one it formed in. During that time the next wave starts only count down: nothing moves.
  5. Changing. At the first wave start after the hold, a new shape is planned for that formation's members and any free unbreakables. Members that stay in the formation are never marked out, even for a moment. Members the new shape doesn't need turn red and roam again. If no shape fits, the formation breaks up. Each formation keeps its own count, so they change at different waves.
  6. Breaking up. The service watches SlotGrid.OnChanged. When a member leaves the grid (it was pierced, or the board was cleared) it drops out of its formation. If fewer than MinMembers are left in that formation, the rest are released at once. The other formations stand. The next line's balance, or the timed pulse, floats the released ones up. ResetRun releases everything.

Snakes

A snake forms as a row, like a horizontal line, of at most SnakeMaxMembers. It sets off along the row, east or west at random, led by the member at that end. From then on it walks on the board's own beat, one FlightRebalanceInterval (4 s) of play apart, so the player can see each step coming. Every SnakeStepPulses beats the balancer asks the service to StepSnakes, right before that beat's race. The beat keeps its own clock beside the balancer's pulse: a pause or a doomed shot's last glide holds it where the pulse starts over, since nearly every shot ends in one and the snakes would never step.

  • The head steps one slot along the snake's heading (HexCoordinates.Step, six directions numbered from east, clockwise). When that slot is off the board, below the row clearance, or taken, SnakeWalk tries the gentlest turn instead: 60° right, 60° left, 120° right, 120° left. It never turns back.
  • Each member behind the head steps into the slot the member ahead of it just left. When a member was popped, the one behind it closes the gap, so the snake stays one line.
  • Boxed in, the snake turns round: its tail leads the way back.

Members stay anchored and blue as they walk (IFormationMember.Walking), and the board settles around each step in the same pulse. The snake keeps the row's limits: never in the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows. Like any formation it changes shape after HoldWaves, breaks below MinMembers, and calls reinforcements while untouched.

The shot solver predicts the pulse's rise but not a snake's step, so a shot that meets a step mid-flight can play out differently from its prediction.

Tough snakes

The board's toughs and toughers (ToughBalloonModel, an IArmouredFormationMember) are one body: a single snake of their own, walking on the same beat and with the same SnakeWalk as an unbreakable snake. Toughs never join an unbreakable formation, and unbreakables never join theirs.

  • Starting as one body. With no tough snake on the board, once MinMembers toughs are on it, the toughest that has landed (IsStable: not still gliding in from a spawn line) starts it, where it stands. Ties go to the higher row. The head is never one in the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows, which a snake never walks. It heads toward the nearer edge, so its body has room behind it, and every other tough that has landed lines up behind it at once (SnakeBody): a line of slots on from the head, away from its heading, bending as little as it can. A balloon on that line moves out to a slot one of the toughs left. The balancer asks every frame (ToughsDueToGather), so the snake forms the moment it can, not at a wave start or on a beat, and takes its first step on the next beat.
  • Falling in at the tail. A tough that lands later, or one still gliding in when the snake formed, falls in behind the tail the moment it lands, the same way. Where nothing on from the tail can take a tough (toughs, unbreakables, anchored actors and anything static block the line), it waits until something on the board changes and tries again.
  • Walking through the board. A board in play is nearly full, so a snake that only steps into empty slots would hardly move. The tough snake walks through balloons instead: its head may step into a slot held by a balloon that moves and is held by nothing (SnakeWalk.CanPassThrough), and that balloon goes round the snake to the slot the tail leaves. When a tough joins in the same step, the tail's slot is the joiner's, so the balloon takes the joiner's slot instead. Toughs and unbreakables (free or in a formation), anchored actors and anything static still stand in its way, and then it turns as any snake does. The balancer carries out each step as one batch (SlotMove): every mover leaves its slot before any lands, so the head and the balloon it passes can trade slots.
  • Most armour leads. The head is the hardest balloon to stop. The worn ones trail behind it.
  • Re-ordering one place per step. Before every step the snake moves toward armour order by one place: from the head down, a member with more armour than the one ahead of it passes it, and the one passed holds its slot for that beat (ArmourOrder.StepTowardOrder). So a tough hit at the head drops back a place a step, and a tougher that joined at the tail works its way forward. Boxed in, the snake turns round like any snake, and its toughest works back to the new front the same way.
  • Walking until the last pops. The tough snake has no other shape to change to, so it never holds or re-plans, and it doesn't break below MinMembers: it walks on with whoever is left. When its last member pops it ends, and the toughs on the board start another at once. It calls no reinforcements.
  • No wall from toughs. A tough gives way to enough hits, so it never counts toward the wall across the board (ShotWall.Walls), and the tough snake's step skips that check.

Members are anchored (IBalanceAnchor) while in their snake, exactly as unbreakables are. A tough snake has no look of its own: its members look like any tough.

Reinforcements

A formation the player doesn't break calls for help. At each wave start, every formation that has had no member pierced since it formed rolls ReinforcementChance, eased while the player is in need (Game/Assist/ThreatEase: half as likely on the last heart). Each success calls in one more unbreakable. The wave a formation forms in doesn't roll, so the player always gets a wave to answer it. A formation keeps its record through shape changes: once pierced, it never calls again.

The service only counts the calls (IFormationReinforcements). BalloonSpawner takes them right after the formations arrange, since both run at the wave's first line, and adds that many unbreakables to the line. They enter like any other balloon. They don't count against the level's own unbreakable cap or wave quota. Instead, at most ReinforcementCeiling of them are on the board at once (ReinforcementPlan). Calls past the ceiling, or past the line's open places, are dropped. On the board they are ordinary unbreakables: they roam, and once enough are free they form a formation of their own.

Rules a formation keeps

  • No pushing. A formation slot must be empty or already hold one of the unbreakables it is planning with. A formation never pushes another balloon aside, and never plans over another formation's members.
  • Spawn room. It never uses the bottom BottomClearance rows, so spawn lines always have room to enter.
  • No wall across the board. Every formation keeps its own row short, but two of them, or one and a roamer, could still meet: a row of three beside a snake of four fills a seven-wide row, and two lines one row apart can touch end to end. So nothing that moves an unbreakable may link the board's unbreakables (and deflectors) from one side wall to the other (Slots/Grid/ShotWall). The planner drops every spot that would, a snake's head turns away from a step that would, and BalloonBalancer's roam pre-pass drops the landing slots that would (DropWallClosingSlots). A board some other way walled already (the balance race floating unbreakables up, say) is no reason to freeze them, so then the check stands aside.
  • Rows stay up and short. A horizontal line never uses the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows, and takes at most HorizontalMaxMembers: close to the cannon, or across the board, a wall shuts the player out. Diagonals leave lanes open, so they keep BottomClearance and MaxMembers. The unbreakables a row leaves out are free to form their own.
  • A cover is a shield against the cannon. ItemCover stands around a balloon carrying a bomb, a snipe or a lightning (the items that hit hardest; the others are left open). It takes three to six of the free unbreakables, whatever MaxMembers says, and fills the item's neighbours in order: the two below it, toward the cannon, then its two sides, and the two above only once those are taken. Six close the ring. With an odd count it tries the left and the right side both and keeps the cheaper. A neighbour off the board is shielded by the edge, so the cover skips it; a corner with fewer than three neighbours on the board gets none. A slot it takes in BottomClearance rules the spot out. Its place is the item's, so it never looks for a spot of its own.
  • Members keep their slot. A member already standing on a formation slot keeps it: assignment takes the nearest pairs first, so its distance-0 pair always wins. As a result, no member ever has to move into a slot another member still holds.
  • A full ring holds the item. Six leave the item's balloon nowhere to move. A smaller cover leaves the side above it open, so the item can float up out of it. If the item moves or pops, the cover stays where it is until the next change.
  • A cover's slots must be free. A neighbour holding an ordinary balloon rules the spot out, as for every shape: the cover never pushes a balloon aside, and a balloon is no shield, since a shot pops it.

Anchoring

A member in formation reports IBalanceAnchor.IsAnchored (from Slots/Actor). Everything that moves balloons leaves an anchored actor in its slot:

  • the balance race (BalancePlanner) and BalloonBalancer.HasPossibleMove,
  • the roam pre-pass, and the model's own TryPickRelocation,
  • PressurePropagation, where an anchored actor blocks the shove the way a static actor does,
  • the shot solver. ShotBoardGather copies the flag into BalanceProfile.Anchored, and ShotSimDynamicActor reports it, so the solver's predicted pulses leave anchored balloons in place too.

Look

Balloon/Type/UnbreakableFormationLook turns a member blue once it has arrived in its slot (in formation and IsStable). The core glow, the inner rim and the exhaust trail all change, and a GlitterSwirl halo fades in behind the sphere. The halo doesn't sway or spin with its balloon: the service gives every member its formation's heading (IFormationMember.FormationHeading, a world direction), and the look turns the halo so its swirl drifts that way. A line heads along its slots, and an item cover heads up at the item. The unbreakable's periodic burst is tagged blue too, so its specks match. All the blue comes from the palette's UnbreakableFormation entry. See Balloon/Type/README.md.

Settings

The settings are IUnbreakableFormationSettings, nested in BalloonsConfiguration under "Unbreakable Formations":

  • Enabled (all formations, tough snakes too)
  • ToughSnakes (on: the toughs walk as one snake. MinMembers toughs on the board start it, it steps every SnakeStepPulses pulses, and it keeps out of the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows)
  • MinMembers (3) and MaxMembers (5)
  • HoldWaves (3)
  • BottomClearance (2) and HorizontalBottomClearance (4, for horizontal lines)
  • HorizontalMaxMembers (3, for horizontal lines; an item cover takes three to six)
  • SnakeMaxMembers (4) and SnakeStepPulses (1: a step on every pulse)
  • SpotChoices (3)
  • ReinforcementChance (0.35, eased in need by Game/Assist/ThreatEase) and ReinforcementCeiling (4)
  • the shape weights: horizontal 1, each diagonal 1, item cover 2, snake 1
  • SnakeWeightPerExtraUnbreakable (0.5: the snake's weight 1.5x with one unbreakable past MinMembers, 2x with two)

The design and its open risks are in Plans/PLAN-UnbreakableFormations.md.

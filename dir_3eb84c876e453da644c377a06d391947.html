Detailed Description

Unbreakable balloons keep one clock, the beat (BeatSeconds, 1.7 s of play), and every BeatsPerCycle (2) beats make a cycle (3.4 s). An unbreakable snake steps SnakeStepsPerBeat (2) times a beat, on the beat and the off-beat (every 0.85 s), so it walks quicker than the tough snake. When two or more are on the board, they gather into a formation. They form up on any beat, stay in place for a few cycles, then switch to a different line on the cycle. While a formation stands, its members float in midair: the balloons below rise until they reach the formation, and spawn lines stop under it. An unbreakable left alone, in no formation, roams: it jumps toward the far side of the board, but only on a cycle, only after it has stood LoneHoldCycles (3) cycles, and only one at a time, so the board moves to one rhythm rather than a lone unbreakable crossing it at every wave.

Several formations can stand at once. Unbreakables left over after one formation is full form a second one, so two lines can stack into a blockade. A formation the player leaves alone calls in more unbreakables, past the level's usual cap, and those form formations of their own.

Tough balloons (toughs and toughers) gather too, but only into one snake of their own. It starts from the biggest group, every other tough falls in behind its tail, and it walks through the balloons in its way. See "Tough snakes" below.

The clock

BalloonBalancer keeps the beat (TickStepClock): it counts seconds of play, and a pause holds it. The rhythm comes before anything else, so a doomed shot's last glide, which freezes the rest of the board, doesn't hold the beat: an unbreakable snake steps on its tick right through it, a ring turns on its beat right through it, and a cycle or form-up that falls due in the glide runs in the balance after it. A ring turns once a beat (TurnRings), on the beat and not on a snake's off-beat step; a ring formed or re-planned in the balance that turns it waits for the next beat. An unbreakable snake steps SnakeStepsPerBeat times a beat, evenly spaced, and on a beat between cycles the free unbreakables form up (FormOnBeat) once MinMembers of them are free, still gliding in or not, so one that arrives or is set free is red a beat at most, not a cycle. On every cycle the balancer asks the service to ArrangeOnCycle, then moves one lone unbreakable (RelocateOneRoamer): the one that has rested longest of those that have stood LoneHoldCycles, and none while the last one to move is still on its way (RoamRests keeps the count). Formations go first, so the lone one sees their slots as taken. A cycle with no unbreakable on the board does nothing, and no balance runs for it. The beat runs with no snake too, and with no unbreakable on the board at all, so the snakes' steps and the cycles keep one rhythm. The tough snake keeps its own clock (ToughSnakeStepSeconds, 1.5 s): it has no shape to change and nothing to hold, so it steps on its own.

Other things on the board keep the same rhythm through IBoardBeat (Balloon/Controller/), which the balancer implements: OffBeats(share) is how many off-beats have passed in the run, an off-beat falling that share of a beat after each beat (BalloonBalancer.OffBeatsAt is the pure count, from the beats passed and how far the clock stands toward the next). A reader keeps no timer, only the count it last saw. Bubble clusters merge on theirs, a quarter beat in (IRipeBubbleSettings.MergeOffBeat), which falls between a snake's step on the beat and its step half a beat in (Plans/PLAN-RipeBubbleCluster.md, decision 19). The count follows the beat exactly: a pause holds it, a doomed glide does not, and a run reset starts it over. With the flight pulse switched off (FlightRebalanceInterval 0) the balancer moves nothing, snakes included, but still counts the beat for its readers. So BeatSeconds and SnakeStepsPerBeat now pace the bubbles' merging as well as the unbreakables.

How a formation runs

  1. Cycle. On each cycle the balancer calls ArrangeOnCycle, before the lone unbreakable's move.
  2. Forming. If at least MinMembers unbreakables are free (in no formation), the service picks a shape for them. The pick is weighted at random from the shape weights. The snake's weight grows by SnakeWeightPerExtraUnbreakable of itself for each unbreakable on the board beyond MinMembers, so a crowded board snakes more often, up to MaxUnbreakableSnakes (2) snakes at once: with that many walking, the pick leaves the snake out. It leaves out every shape the level doesn't allow (see "Shapes a level allows" below). It leaves out the shape just held, and ItemCover finds no spot unless a bomb, a snipe, a lightning or a gift balloon is on the board. A ring is picked only with RingMinMembers (4) unbreakables free: with fewer it finds no spot. If the picked shape fits nowhere, the service tries the next one. If nothing fits, nothing forms and the unbreakables roam as usual. A formation takes at most MaxMembers (a horizontal line, HorizontalMaxMembers); an item cover takes three to six, and a ring four or five (RingMinMembers, RingMaxMembers), never six. While MinMembers or more are still free after it, they form another one.
  3. Moving in. The service hands each member's move to the balancer's relocate routine. That is the same routine roam jumps use, so the members glide in at their normal speed. Each member is marked in formation (IWriteableFormationMember.SetInFormation).
  4. Holding. The formation lasts its shape's hold, counted in cycles and counting the one it formed on: a snake walks SnakeHoldCycles (3), a horizontal or diagonal line holds LineHoldCycles (3), an item cover ItemCoverHoldCycles (3), a ring RingHoldCycles (8). During that time the next cycles only count down: nothing moves but a snake's steps and a ring's turns, and a ring's count also tells it when to turn the other way (see "Rings" below).
  5. Changing. On the first cycle after the hold, each member first rolls JoinSnakeChance (0.35) to leave for the tail of the unbreakable snake with the most room, while it has room (MaxMembers): the joiners fly in one batch to a line of slots on from the tail, away from its body, each balloon on that line going to the slot a joiner left, so snakes grow from the other formations rather than new ones forming. Then a new shape is planned for the members left and any free unbreakables. When the formation changing is a snake done walking, a ring is RingWeightAfterSnake (4) times likelier, and the snake coils into it from its head (see "Rings" below). A formation with a member a lance plowed (see "Plowed by a lance" below) waits for the discharge and changes on the cycle after it. Members that stay in the formation are never marked out, even for a moment. Members the new shape doesn't need turn red and roam again, each once it has rested. If no shape fits, the formation breaks up. Each formation keeps its own count, so they change at different waves.
  6. Breaking up. The service watches SlotGrid.OnChanged. When a member leaves the grid (it was pierced, or the board was cleared) it drops out of its formation. If fewer than MinMembers are left in that formation, the rest are released at once. The other formations stand. The next line's balance, or the timed pulse, floats the released ones up. The service never moves a lone one itself; the balancer does, one a cycle. ResetRun releases everything.

Shapes a level allows

A level says which shapes its unbreakables may form: IActiveLevelParameters.Current.AllowedFormationShapes (Configuration/Level/). The level generator allows the snake alone on the meadow and every shape in the sky and on the water (Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md). So no ring forms on the meadow. FormationShapes.All holds the ring. The service reads it at the start of each shape plan, and a shape that is not allowed weighs 0 (WeightOf): it is never picked, and never kept as the last resort.

What follows on a snake-only level, with no other change to the rules:

  • Free unbreakables form snakes, up to MaxUnbreakableSnakes. Past that cap they have no shape to form, so they stay loose and roam. No cap is lifted for them.
  • A snake done holding has no other shape to change to. It is planned again as a snake, the last resort: it lines up as a row once more and takes in free unbreakables up to SnakeMaxMembers.
  • A line or an item cover left standing from the level before holds its cycles out. Then it turns into a snake, or, with the snakes at their cap, breaks up.
  • With no shape allowed at all, nothing forms and a formation done holding breaks up.

The toughs' snake is not a pick, so the level's set does not touch it: toughs carried over from a sky level still gather. A meadow level brings no toughs of its own.

Snakes

A snake forms as a row, like a horizontal line, of at most SnakeMaxMembers. It sets off along the row, east or west at random, led by the member at that end. From then on it takes SnakeStepsPerBeat (2) steps a beat, on the beat and the off-beat (every 0.85 s of play), quicker than the tough snake: the balancer asks the service to StepSnakes, right before that balance's race, or at once in a doomed shot's last glide. The beat is apart from the balancer's 4 s pulse, which a pause or a doomed glide starts over; a pause only holds the beat. A snake formed or re-planned on a cycle takes its first step on the next tick.

  • The head steps one slot along the snake's heading (HexCoordinates.Step, six directions numbered from east, clockwise). A balloon there trades places with it, going round the snake to the slot the tail leaves (see "Walking through the board" below), so the snake steps on every tick. When that slot is off the board, below the row clearance, held by something it can't trade with, or would wall the board, SnakeWalk tries the gentlest turn instead: 60° right, 60° left, 120° right, 120° left. It never turns back.
  • Each member behind the head steps into the slot the member ahead of it just left. When a member was popped, the one behind it closes the gap, so the snake stays one line.
  • Plowed by a lance. A piercing shot plows through toughs and unbreakables without popping them and shatters them at the next wall, where each was struck (ProjectileHitResolver.DischargePending). Until then each plowed one holds its slot (ProjectileRegistryExtensions.AwaitsDischarge), and the snake splits there: the members ahead of it walk on, on the tick, and it and those behind it wait. With the head plowed, the tail end leads. Once the discharge pops the plowed ones, the members behind close up at the next step. A plowed one never joins a formation, falls in behind the tough snake, or roams meanwhile.
  • Boxed in (by toughs, other unbreakables, anchored or static actors, the edges), the snake turns round in the same tick and its tail leads the way back. Only boxed in at both ends does it miss a step, and it tries the other end on the next tick.

Members stay anchored and blue as they walk (IFormationMember.Walking), and the board settles around each step in the same pulse. The snake keeps the row's limits: never in the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows. Like any formation it changes shape after its hold (SnakeHoldCycles), breaks below MinMembers, and calls reinforcements while untouched.

The shot solver predicts the pulse's rise but not a snake's step, so a shot that meets a step mid-flight can play out differently from its prediction.

Rings

A ring is four or five unbreakables (RingMinMembers, RingMaxMembers) standing in neighbouring slots round one slot, its centre. A slot has six neighbours, and a ring never takes all six, so a gap always stays open for a shot to reach the centre through as the ring comes round. The plan (FormationPlanner, FormationRing) tries each of the six arcs round a centre: the arc's first slot is the neighbour in one direction (HexCoordinates.Step numbers the six from east, clockwise) and the rest follow it clockwise.

  • The centre. It needs all six neighbours on the board and above the bottom BottomClearance rows, so the ring can turn all the way round. The centre is empty, or holds a balloon nothing else holds. A balloon there stays in its slot while the ring stands (the same hold a cover puts on its host, HoldHost), and gets no ring of light (see "Look").
  • Turning. On every beat (TurnRings; not a snake's off-beat step) the whole ring turns one slot round its centre, clockwise or the other way: each member takes the next neighbour in that direction, and the members' heading runs along the ring. A balloon in the slot the arc turns into trades places to the slot the arc leaves, as one batch (SlotMove), as the snake's walk does through the board. The ring starts the way at random, and every RingReverseEveryCycles (4) cycles of its hold it turns the other way.
  • Boxed in. If the next turn meets something the ring cannot pass (a tough, an unbreakable outside the ring, an anchored or static actor, anything SnakeWalk.CanPassThrough refuses), it turns back at once and tries the other way in the same tick, as a boxed-in snake does. Blocked both ways, it misses the beat. A member a lance plowed stops the whole ring until the discharge (see "Plowed by a lance" under "Snakes"), since the ring turns as one. The no-wall rule (see "Rules a formation keeps") applies to each turn: a turn that would link the board from wall to wall is as good as blocked.
  • Holding and changing. A ring holds RingHoldCycles (8) cycles, then changes shape like any other formation, and drops out of its formation below MinMembers like any other.
  • After a snake. A snake done walking leans toward a ring: its ring weight is multiplied by RingWeightAfterSnake (4). It then coils in from its head: the planner keeps only the arcs that have the head's slot at one end, so the head keeps its slot (a member on a formation slot always does), and the ring starts turning the way that leads the head into the open slot, so the arc follows it round. With no such arc, any arc that holds the head's slot will do. The 4-to-5 rule applies as for any ring: the snake's members and the free unbreakables together must number four at least, and the ring takes five at most.

The shot solver predicts the pulse's rise but not a ring's turn, as it does not predict a snake's step.

Tough snakes

The board's toughs and toughers (ToughBalloonModel, an IArmouredFormationMember) are one body: a single snake of their own, walking with the same SnakeWalk as an unbreakable snake but slower, on a clock of its own: a step every ToughSnakeStepSeconds (1.5 s) of play, held by a pause or a doomed glide (BalloonBalancer). Toughs never join an unbreakable formation, and unbreakables never join theirs.

  • Starting as one body. The initial fill deals the toughs side by side on one line (Balloon/Spawner/README.md), so they rise together as the snake's body. The toughs form up the moment ToughSnakeMinMembers (2) of them are on the board, without waiting for them to land: a tough still gliding in counts at the slot it is heading for. So the run is the snake while it rises, anchored, and the balancer, which leaves a formation's members be, never pulls it apart. It starts from the biggest group of touching toughs, so it starts where most of its body already is and not in a pocket the rest can't reach: that group's tough in the deepest row starts it, where it stands, the farther west on a tie, the west end of the run. Hits left play no part. The head is never one in the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows, which a snake never walks. It heads away from a free tough beside it, so toughs that rise side by side are its body as they stand; with none beside it, it heads toward the nearer edge, so its body has room behind it. Every other tough then lines up behind it at once (SnakeBody): a line of slots on from the head, following the toughs where they stand and otherwise bending as little as it can. A balloon on that line moves out to a slot one of the toughs left. The balancer asks every frame (ToughsDueToGather), so the snake forms the moment it can, not at a wave start or on a beat, and even while a level's transition holds the board, when the next level's toughs rise (BalloonBalancer.FormToughsInTheLevelTransition; only the form-up runs then, no step and no balance). The balance a line calls forms them up too, without waiting for the next frame's tick, so a tough or tougher a line brings flies from its entry straight to its place behind the tail. It takes its first step the moment its head has landed (ToughSnakeLanded), so the toughs are moving as soon as they arrive at a level's start.
  • Falling in at the tail. A tough that rises later falls in behind the tail the moment it appears, the same way, gliding straight to its place. Where nothing on from the tail can take it (toughs, unbreakables, anchored actors and anything static block the line) but the head has room, it falls in at the head instead: the snake turns round, as it does in a dead end, and the old head's end is its tail. Where neither end can take it, it waits until something on the board changes and tries again, unless more toughs are free than the snake holds (a snake boxed in a pocket among bushes): then it starts over from the biggest group.
  • Closing up. When a member pops, the members behind it close up at once, each into the slot of the one ahead, so the body stays one line between steps (a balloon in the first one's way goes round to the slot the tail leaves). One with nowhere to go waits for the next step, whose trail closes the gap.
  • Walking through the board. A board in play is nearly full, so a snake that only steps into empty slots would hardly move. Every snake, the tough one and the unbreakables', walks through balloons instead: its head may step into a slot held by a balloon that moves and is held by nothing (SnakeWalk.CanPassThrough), and that balloon goes round the snake to the slot the tail leaves. When a tough joins in the same step, the tail's slot is the joiner's, so the balloon takes the joiner's slot instead. Toughs and unbreakables (free or in a formation), anchored actors and anything static still stand in its way, and then it turns as any snake does. The balancer carries out each step as one batch (SlotMove): every mover leaves its slot before any lands, so the head and the balloon it passes can trade slots.
  • Keeping its shape. The snake never re-orders: each member walks into the slot the one ahead of it left, a tough that's hit keeps its place, and one that joins stays at the tail. Boxed in, the snake turns round like any snake, its tail leading, and walks on in the same order.
  • Walking until the last pops. The tough snake has no other shape to change to, so it never holds or re-plans, and it doesn't break below MinMembers: it walks on with whoever is left. When its last member pops it ends, and the toughs on the board start another at once. It calls no reinforcements.
  • No wall from toughs. A tough gives way to enough hits, so it never counts toward the wall across the board (ShotWall.Walls), and the tough snake's step skips that check.

Members are anchored (IBalanceAnchor) while in their snake, exactly as unbreakables are. Its members look like any tough. As a trial (IBalloonsConfiguration.ToughSnakeSprings, on; the Tough Snake Springs cheat flips it in play), a spring joins each pair of neighbours in the snake, tied to their knots (Balloon/View/ToughSnakeSpringView). Each is a line of points joined by springs between the two knots, the way the launch screen's spring line is simulated (Shared/Math/SpringChain): a little longer than the gap between neighbouring slots, so it sags while the pair stands side by side, stretches as the one ahead steps away and swings as the one behind catches up. A spring lasts as long as the same pair stands next to each other; a pop or a joiner ties a new one. A spring shows only once both its balloons are on screen, and it reveals: it grows out of the knot ahead and reaches for the one behind until it ties on (Reveal Seconds), then stays tied. A snake's springs reveal head to tail, each once the one ahead has tied on. A tough flying in to the tail shows no spring while it is further than Attach Distance from the tail, then the spring grows and reaches for it as it closes in, and ties on as it arrives. Each spring is drawn on its knot's layer, so the scene capture sees it as it sees the balloons: it casts the soft shadow and takes the bounce light (Display/README.md). When a level hands its board off, the level-up's ascent or the loss restart's pop wave, the tied springs stay with the balloons they tie and ride out with them (the view is outgoing content, ITransitionOutgoingContent); each drops once either balloon is popped or pooled. A spring is white with a faint checker along it (Colour and Checker Colour, Checks Per Unit), so it reads as textured; the checker is tiled along the line, so it follows the spring as it swings, not the screen.

Reinforcements

A formation the player doesn't break calls for help. Reinforcements still count waves, not cycles, since they arrive with a wave's first line: at each wave start (RollReinforcementsAtWaveStart), every formation that has had no member pierced since it formed rolls ReinforcementChance, eased while the player is in need (Game/Assist/ThreatEase: half as likely on the last heart). Each success calls in one more unbreakable. A formation sits out the roll at the end of the wave it formed in, so the player always gets a whole wave to answer it. A formation keeps its record through shape changes: once pierced, it never calls again.

The service only counts the calls (IFormationReinforcements). BalloonSpawner takes them right after the formations arrange, since both run at the wave's first line, and adds that many unbreakables to the line. They enter like any other balloon. They don't count against the level's own unbreakable cap or wave quota. Instead, at most ReinforcementCeiling of them are on the board at once (ReinforcementPlan). Calls past the ceiling, or past the line's open places, are dropped. On the board they are ordinary unbreakables: they roam, and once two are free they form a formation of their own.

Rules a formation keeps

  • Room on a full board. A formation slot must be empty, already hold one of the unbreakables it is planning with, or hold a balloon that can trade places (one that moves and nothing holds: SnakeWalk.CanPassThrough). That balloon goes to the nearest slot a member left, in the same batch, so a full board doesn't keep the free unbreakables apart and red. Each balloon moved counts as FormationPlanner.DisplacedBalloonCost (3) slots of travel, so where there is room the formation takes it instead. A formation never plans over another formation's members, toughs, or anything anchored or static.
  • Spawn room. It never uses the bottom BottomClearance rows, so spawn lines always have room to enter.
  • No wall across the board. Every formation keeps its own row short, but two of them, or one and a roamer, could still meet: a row of three beside a snake of four fills a seven-wide row, and two lines one row apart can touch end to end. So nothing that moves an unbreakable may link the board's unbreakables (and deflectors) from one side wall to the other (Slots/Grid/ShotWall). The planner drops every spot that would, a snake's head turns away from a step that would, a ring's turn that would is refused (it turns back, or misses the beat), and BalloonBalancer's lone move drops the landing slots that would (DropWallClosingSlots). A board some other way walled already (the balance race floating unbreakables up, say) is no reason to freeze them, so then the check stands aside.
  • Rows stay up and short. A horizontal line never uses the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows, and takes at most HorizontalMaxMembers: close to the cannon, or across the board, a wall shuts the player out. Diagonals leave lanes open, so they keep BottomClearance and MaxMembers. The unbreakables a row leaves out are free to form their own.
  • A cover is a shield against the cannon. ItemCover stands around a balloon carrying a bomb, a snipe or a lightning (the items that hit hardest; the others are left open). It takes three to six of the free unbreakables, whatever MaxMembers says, and fills the item's neighbours in order: the two below it, toward the cannon, then its two sides, and the two above only once those are taken. Six surround it. With an odd count it tries the left and the right side both and keeps the cheaper. A neighbour off the board is shielded by the edge, so the cover skips it; a corner with fewer than three neighbours on the board gets none. A slot it takes in BottomClearance rules the spot out. Its place is the item's, so it never looks for a spot of its own.
  • A cover prefers a gift. A cover also stands round a gift balloon: any of the four reward balloons (BalloonType.IsReward: golden, heart, disco ball, nuke; Balloon/Reward/README.md), filled in the same order. The shape and its settings keep their names (ItemCover, ItemCoverWeight, ItemCoverHoldCycles), which tests, plans and the levels' allowed shapes already use. When both a gift and an item carrier could be covered, the gift's spot counts as cheaper by GiftCoverPreference (2) slots of travel for each member the cover takes (the owner's ask, 2026-11-19). A spot's cost is the slots its members travel to it plus DisplacedBalloonCost (3) for each balloon they move, so the number reads as: the gift is covered unless covering the item carrier would save every member more than 2 slots on average. Three unbreakables that would travel 10 slots to cover a heart balloon and 5 to cover a bomb cover the heart (10 less 6 is 4, under 5); standing beside the bomb already (2 against 10), they cover the bomb. Where a gift's spot comes out cheapest, the pick among the SpotChoices cheapest stays among gifts' spots, or the dice would hand the cover to the item carrier as often as not. At 0 there is no preference and the pick is as it was. With gifts as hosts a cover finds a spot on more boards, so covers form more often than when only items drew them.
  • Members keep their slot. A member already standing on a formation slot keeps it: assignment takes the nearest pairs first, so its distance-0 pair always wins. As a result, no member ever has to move into a slot another member still holds.
  • A ring's centre is held but not lit. A ring holds the balloon at its centre in its slot while it stands, the same hold as a cover's host (HoldHost sets StandingFormation.Host for both), so nothing that balances the board moves it out of the ring. It is not listed in ICoveredHosts, though: only a cover's host is (StandingFormation.HostLit), so the centre gets no ring of light (the owner's ask, 2026-11-21). The next two bullets are written for a cover's host; a ring's centre is held and let go the same ways.
  • A cover has a host, and holds it. The balloon a cover is planned round is its host (StandingFormation.Host; FormationPlanner.CoveredHost says which slot the plan chose). The service lists the hosts in ICoveredHosts.CoveredHosts, each with its formation's id and the world centre of its slot. Covered means enclosed: the members stand in the slots right beside the host and it stays between them (the owner's ask, 2026-11-19; before it, a host floated up out of an open cover on the next balance and the cover stood round nothing). So for as long as a balloon is a host the service holds it in its slot (IWriteableCoverHost.SetHeldByCover, on BalloonModelBase, so a plain balloon carrying an item and every reward balloon has it), and a held balloon is anchored exactly as a member is (see "Anchoring"). It is held from the plan, before the first member has set off, and while the cover holds that shape, lost members or not. A cover never plans round a balloon another cover holds, so one cover at most holds a balloon.
  • When a host is let go. When the cover's hold runs out (before its next shape is planned, so a line may trade the balloon out, and a cover planned again round it holds it again at once), when the cover breaks, when formations are switched off, and on a run reset. A host that outlives its cover is free again at that moment. A formation's host is written in two places only (HoldHost, LetHostGo), and each rebuilds CoveredHosts, so the list never names a balloon no cover holds, whatever the caller does next (a cover let go at the end of its hold may then find no shape to change to and be set loose). Nothing that balances the board moves a held host, and the service's own moves ask the same question (SnakeWalk.CanPassThrough): a snake turns aside from it, a joiner's line and a closing gap go round it, and no formation plans over its slot. What still takes one off its slot does so on purpose: a pop (the shot, an item, a nuke's blast, which spares only reward balloons), the level's ascent, and the clearing of a run. The service checks on every change of the grid, its own moves included, and lets a host that is no longer on its slot go, so none stays held; the cover then stands round nothing until it changes. Nothing takes a balloon off the grid without such a change: SlotGrid.Place and SlotGrid.Remove are the only writers of its slots and both publish OnChanged, and the cheats and the staging tool go through them. A balloon that later lands on the empty slot is not the host.
  • What a held host changes on the board. One more slot stays fixed while a cover stands, for new lines to route round. A host is always a row above its cover's two lower members (the planner refuses a cover whose lower slots fall in BottomClearance), so it blocks no column from below that they did not block already, but a line's shove can no longer pass sideways through its slot. A covered gift cannot drift into reach, or out of it. A nuke is anchored under a cover whether it is armed or not.
  • A cover's slots take the balloons' places. A balloon is no shield, since a shot pops it, so a neighbour holding one trades it out to a slot a member left, as for every shape.

Anchoring

A member in formation reports IBalanceAnchor.IsAnchored (from Slots/Actor), and so does the balloon a cover holds (BalloonModelBase.IsAnchored: held by a cover, or the model's own reason, which is a formation for an unbreakable or a tough and an armed fuse for the nuke). Everything that moves balloons leaves an anchored actor in its slot:

  • the balance race (BalancePlanner) and BalloonBalancer.HasPossibleMove,
  • the lone move, and the model's own TryPickRelocation,
  • PressurePropagation, where an anchored actor blocks the shove the way a static actor does,
  • the shot solver. ShotBoardGather copies the flag into BalanceProfile.Anchored, and ShotSimDynamicActor reports it, so the solver's predicted pulses leave anchored balloons in place too.

Look

Balloon/Type/UnbreakableFormationLook turns a member blue once it has arrived in its slot (in formation and IsStable). The core glow, the inner rim and the exhaust trail all change, and a GlitterSwirl halo fades in behind the sphere. The halo doesn't sway or spin with its balloon: the service gives every member its formation's heading (IFormationMember.FormationHeading, a world direction), and the look turns the halo so its swirl drifts that way. A line heads along its slots, an item cover heads up at the item, and a ring's members head along the ring, the way it is turning, so the swirls drift round the centre. The unbreakable's periodic burst is tagged blue too, so its specks match. All the blue comes from the palette's UnbreakableFormation entry. See Balloon/Type/README.md.

Under the members, on the ground, a blue ring of light circles each one, and neighbouring rings join into one outline the way metaballs do. A ring lights only as its member draws close to a fellow member: none while the nearest is past FadeDistance (2.2 world units), full at FullDistance (0.8, about one slot apart). So members gliding in to form a line brighten and merge as they arrive, and a snake's rings flow along with it as it walks. Unlike the blue lights, the ring counts a member from the moment it is assigned, gliding in or not, since the drawing together is what it shows. Lone unbreakables and toughs never glow.

A ring never just vanishes. Once its member is disconnected, for whatever reason (it leaves the formation and turns red, its partner leaves or pops so it is left alone, it pops itself, or the board is cleared at a level's end or a run reset), the ring dissolves over DissolveSeconds (0.6). It keeps the brightness it had, its line erodes along a world-space noise with a brighter edge where it eats, and it draws back into its member, so a merge with its neighbours eases off rather than snapping apart. A ring whose balloon has gone dissolves where the balloon was last. Joining works the same way in reverse: when a member forms up, rejoins or joins a snake's tail, its ring dissolves in over the same time at its full brightness, its line filling in along the noise as it grows out of the member and eases into the merge. A member that reconnects mid-dissolve comes back from where it had got to.

Balloon/View/FormationGlowView draws it: one quad (BalloonParty/Sprite/FormationGlow shader) fitted each frame round the glowing members, so the shader only runs where the light can be, and hidden while no member glows. Each UnbreakableFormationLook registers in FormationGlowSources from its bind to its despawn, each spawn its own entry, so a pooled look spawning again never takes over its old ring. FormationGlowField keeps the rings from frame to frame: which are connected, how brightly they glow and how far each has dissolved (at most 24 drawn, the shader's array), and which formation each belongs to (IFormationMember.FormationId, which UnbreakableFormationService gives every member of a formation and which a ring keeps after its member has left or popped). The shader sums a smooth falloff round the members of one formation at a time and draws the ring where that sum crosses the level a lone member's would at RingRadius, at a constant width, with a faint fill inside. The field writes a formation's rings next to each other, which is how the shader knows where one formation's sum ends and the next begins; where two formations' light overlaps, the brighter shows. A dissolving ring adds less to that sum as it goes, down to only the threshold, which is what draws it back into its member, and the noise eats its light as far as the dissolving rings' share of the sum. The light is additive and HDR (HdrBoost, 2.5 over the palette blue), so it blooms. It sorts under the balloons (Balloons -2), built on the Effects layer: the main camera draws it in play and the launch screen's mask takes Effects too (for the specks), but no ring glows there: the quad's renderer is on only while a ring glows. While a ball flies, its lens (Display/README.md, EffectsMapCompositor) bends and fades the rings around it, and the rings are laid over the board with the other effects instead of under the balloons; they sort back under them when the map is released. The renderer is watched by LayerPresence, since it is not pooled. The GI capture (Display/SceneCaptureService) draws it too, so the rings are in the colour the GI smear bounces and their light spreads over the ground beside them. The shader writes colour only, so a ring adds no coverage to the capture and casts no shadow. The capture is 8 bits, so the ring's HDR blue clamps there toward cyan. Rings join only with the rings of their own formation: two formations standing side by side keep an outline each, crossing where they overlap, so each reads as the unit it moves as (the owner's ask, 2026-11-19; before it, every ring in reach joined and a stack of formations read as one wall). A member's brightness likewise follows its nearest fellow of its own formation. The settings are IFormationGlowSettings, BalloonsConfiguration's "Formation Glow".

The balloon an item cover stands round gets a ring of that formation too, so the outline closes over it (the owner's ask, 2026-11-19). A ring's centre balloon gets none: the middle is left unlit (the owner's ask, 2026-11-21), so a ring of unbreakables shows only its members' rings, which join round the open centre. The members' rings alone do not reach the middle: the covered balloon showed as a hole in a full cover and as a notch in an open one. Balloon/View/FormationGlowHosts reads ICoveredHosts.CoveredHosts each frame and keeps one ring source in FormationGlowSources for each host, at the centre of its slot and with its formation's id. The covered balloon's own view knows nothing of it. To the field it is one more ring of that formation: it lights as the members draw close, dissolves in as theirs do, and counts toward the 24 the shader draws. The host is listed, and held in its slot, from the plan, with every member still on its way; its ring follows the nearest member, so it comes in as they arrive and stays for as long as the cover stands. When the service lets the host go, its source is taken out and the ring dissolves where it was, as a popped member's does; the members' rings stay if the cover still stands. The field knows a ring by its source, so a host keeps the same source for as long as the same formation covers it; the same balloon under another formation gets a new one. A frame with the covers standing as they did changes nothing and allocates nothing.

Past the shader's 24 rings the field leaves out the rings that lit last, and a host's ring lights after its members' (they are listed first), so a host's would be the first of its cover to go, which puts the notch back. This is left as it is: the board does not get there. A generated level caps its own unbreakables at 4 (the catalogue's count curve) and ReinforcementCeiling adds 4 at most, so 8 unbreakables stand at once, in at most two covers: 10 rings, and a few more for the 0.6 s a popped member's ring takes to dissolve. Reaching 24 would take, say, 21 unbreakables in formation and three hosts. If the caps are ever raised that far, give the hosts' rings precedence in FormationGlowField.Write.

The rings also cast a local light on what stands round them, in the formation blue, through the scene-light field (Shared/SceneLight/SceneLightFieldService): the bounce above only reaches the ground, and this one lights the balloons' sides. A light per ring would use up the field's 32 lights on one long formation, so FormationGlowBeams puts the rings of one formation that join into one outline (centres closer than FormationGlowField.JoinDistance, about 1.1) in one group, chains included, so two formations light apart, as their outlines stay apart. Each group casts one capsule light between its two members furthest apart: exact for a line or a diagonal, close for a ring round an item or a bent snake. At most 8 groups cast one. A covered balloon's ring is in its cover's group: it stands in the middle, so the capsule's ends are still two members, and a cover down to two members either side of it stays one group through it. The light's strength is LightIntensity (0.6) times the group's average of brightness times presence, so it rises as members draw close and falls away with a dissolving ring. It reaches LightRadius (1.43) from that line and falls off by LightFalloffPower (2). A light is registered only while its group glows, and a formation standing still changes none of its values, so the field does not re-render for it. The unbreakables themselves are not lit by it: UnbreakableMat's Exclude Light Palettes mask names both Unbreakable (the red light a member casts as it moves) and UnbreakableFormation, so a member keeps its chrome under its formation's blue.

Settings

The settings are IUnbreakableFormationSettings, nested in BalloonsConfiguration under "Unbreakable Formations":

  • Enabled (all formations, tough snakes too)
  • ToughSnakes (on: the toughs walk as one snake. ToughSnakeMinMembers (2) toughs on the board start it, it steps every ToughSnakeStepSeconds, and it keeps out of the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows)
  • MinMembers (2) and MaxMembers (5)
  • the clock: BeatSeconds (1.7: seconds of play between beats), BeatsPerCycle (2, so a cycle is 3.4 s) and SnakeStepsPerBeat (2: an unbreakable snake steps on the beat and the off-beat, every 0.85 s)
  • the holds, in cycles: SnakeHoldCycles (3), LineHoldCycles (3, horizontal and diagonal), ItemCoverHoldCycles (3), RingHoldCycles (8), and LoneHoldCycles (3, a lone unbreakable's rest between moves)
  • BottomClearance (2) and HorizontalBottomClearance (4, for horizontal lines)
  • HorizontalMaxMembers (3, for horizontal lines; an item cover takes three to six)
  • SnakeMaxMembers (4)
  • ToughSnakeStepSeconds (1.5: seconds of play between the tough snake's steps, its own clock apart from the beat)
  • SpotChoices (3)
  • GiftCoverPreference (2: slots of travel, for each member, that a cover's spot round a gift balloon counts as cheaper than one round an item carrier; 0 for no preference)
  • ReinforcementChance (0.35, eased in need by Game/Assist/ThreatEase) and ReinforcementCeiling (4)
  • the ring: RingMinMembers (4), RingMaxMembers (5; of the six slots round a centre one always stays open), RingReverseEveryCycles (4: cycles it turns one way before the other; 0 never turns back by itself), and the weights below
  • the shape weights: horizontal 1, each diagonal 1, item cover 2 (round an item carrier or a gift), snake 0.25 (so a snake is the rarest pick), ring RingWeight 1, and RingWeightAfterSnake (4: the ring's weight multiplier when the formation changing is a snake done walking)
  • MaxUnbreakableSnakes (2: a formation that would make a third picks another shape; 0 sets no cap)
  • JoinSnakeChance (0.35: each member of a formation changing shape flies to a snake's tail instead)
  • SnakeWeightPerExtraUnbreakable (0.25: the snake's weight 1.25x with one unbreakable past MinMembers, 1.5x with two)

The design and its open risks are in Plans/PLAN-UnbreakableFormations.md.

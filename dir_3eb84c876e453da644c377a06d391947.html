Detailed Description

Unbreakable balloons normally roam: once per wave each one jumps toward the far side of the board. When three or more are on the board, they gather into a formation instead. They line up, stay in place for a few waves, then switch to a different line. While a formation stands, its members float in midair: the balloons below rise until they reach the formation, and spawn lines stop under it.

Several formations can stand at once. Unbreakables left over after one formation is full form a second one, so two lines can stack into a blockade. A formation the player leaves alone calls in more unbreakables, past the level's usual cap, and those form formations of their own.

How a formation runs

  1. Wave start. BalloonBalancer.Balance(atWaveStart: true) calls ArrangeAtWaveStart before the usual roam pre-pass. Roamers therefore see the formation's slots as already taken.
  2. Forming. If at least MinMembers unbreakables are free (in no formation), the service picks a shape for them. The pick is weighted at random from the shape weights. It leaves out the shape just held, and leaves out ItemCover unless an item is on the board. If the picked shape fits nowhere, the service tries the next one. If nothing fits, nothing forms and the unbreakables roam as usual. A formation takes at most MaxMembers (a horizontal line, HorizontalMaxMembers); an item cover takes exactly six. While MinMembers or more are still free after it, they form another one.
  3. Moving in. The service hands each member's move to the balancer's relocate routine. That is the same routine roam jumps use, so the members glide in at their normal speed. Each member is marked in formation (IWriteableFormationMember.SetInFormation).
  4. Holding. The formation lasts HoldWaves waves, counting the one it formed in. During that time the next wave starts only count down: nothing moves.
  5. Changing. At the first wave start after the hold, a new shape is planned for that formation's members and any free unbreakables. Members that stay in the formation are never marked out, even for a moment. Members the new shape doesn't need turn red and roam again. If no shape fits, the formation breaks up. Each formation keeps its own count, so they change at different waves.
  6. Breaking up. The service watches SlotGrid.OnChanged. When a member leaves the grid (it was pierced, or the board was cleared) it drops out of its formation. If fewer than MinMembers are left in that formation, the rest are released at once. The other formations stand. The next line's balance, or the timed pulse, floats the released ones up. ResetRun releases everything.

Snakes

A snake forms as a row, like a horizontal line, of at most SnakeMaxMembers. It sets off along the row, east or west at random, led by the member at that end. From then on it walks on the board's own beat: the balancer's pulse (FlightRebalanceInterval, every 4 s), so the player can see each step coming. Every SnakeStepPulses pulses the balancer asks the service to StepSnakes, right before that pulse's race:

  • The head steps one slot along the snake's heading (HexCoordinates.Step, six directions numbered from east, clockwise). When that slot is off the board, below the row clearance, or taken, SnakeWalk tries the gentlest turn instead: 60° right, 60° left, 120° right, 120° left. It never turns back.
  • Each member behind the head steps into the slot the member ahead of it just left. When a member was popped, the one behind it closes the gap, so the snake stays one line.
  • Boxed in, the snake turns round: its tail leads the way back.

Members stay anchored and blue as they walk (IFormationMember.Walking), and the board settles around each step in the same pulse. The snake keeps the row's limits: never in the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows. Like any formation it changes shape after HoldWaves, breaks below MinMembers, and calls reinforcements while untouched.

The shot solver predicts the pulse's rise but not a snake's step, so a shot that meets a step mid-flight can play out differently from its prediction.

Reinforcements

A formation the player doesn't break calls for help. At each wave start, every formation that has had no member pierced since it formed rolls ReinforcementChance. Each success calls in one more unbreakable. The wave a formation forms in doesn't roll, so the player always gets a wave to answer it. A formation keeps its record through shape changes: once pierced, it never calls again.

The service only counts the calls (IFormationReinforcements). BalloonSpawner takes them right after the formations arrange, since both run at the wave's first line, and adds that many unbreakables to the line. They enter like any other balloon. They don't count against the level's own unbreakable cap or wave quota. Instead, at most ReinforcementCeiling of them are on the board at once (ReinforcementPlan). Calls past the ceiling, or past the line's open places, are dropped. On the board they are ordinary unbreakables: they roam, and once enough are free they form a formation of their own.

Rules a formation keeps

  • No pushing. A formation slot must be empty or already hold one of the unbreakables it is planning with. A formation never pushes another balloon aside, and never plans over another formation's members.
  • Spawn room. It never uses the bottom BottomClearance rows, so spawn lines always have room to enter.
  • No wall across the board. Every formation keeps its own row short, but two of them, or one and a roamer, could still meet: a row of three beside a snake of four fills a seven-wide row, and two lines one row apart can touch end to end. So nothing that moves an unbreakable may link the board's unbreakables (and deflectors) from one side wall to the other (Slots/Grid/ShotWall). The planner drops every spot that would, a snake's head turns away from a step that would, and BalloonBalancer's roam pre-pass drops the landing slots that would (DropWallClosingSlots). A board some other way walled already (the balance race floating unbreakables up, say) is no reason to freeze them, so then the check stands aside.
  • Rows stay up and short. A horizontal line never uses the bottom HorizontalBottomClearance rows, and takes at most HorizontalMaxMembers: close to the cannon, or across the board, a wall shuts the player out. Diagonals leave lanes open, so they keep BottomClearance and MaxMembers. The unbreakables a row leaves out are free to form their own.
  • A cover is a whole ring. ItemCover takes all six neighbours of the item's balloon, so it needs six free unbreakables, and all six slots on the board and above BottomClearance: an item on the board's edge is never covered. Its place is the item's, so it never looks for a spot of its own.
  • Members keep their slot. A member already standing on a formation slot keeps it: assignment takes the nearest pairs first, so its distance-0 pair always wins. As a result, no member ever has to move into a slot another member still holds.
  • The covered item stays put. With every neighbour taken by an anchored member, the item's balloon has nowhere to move. If it pops anyway (a blast reaches it), the ring stays where it is until the next change.

Anchoring

A member in formation reports IBalanceAnchor.IsAnchored (from Slots/Actor). Everything that moves balloons leaves an anchored actor in its slot:

  • the balance race (BalancePlanner) and BalloonBalancer.HasPossibleMove,
  • the roam pre-pass, and the model's own TryPickRelocation,
  • PressurePropagation, where an anchored actor blocks the shove the way a static actor does,
  • the shot solver. ShotBoardGather copies the flag into BalanceProfile.Anchored, and ShotSimDynamicActor reports it, so the solver's predicted pulses leave anchored balloons in place too.

Look

Balloon/Type/UnbreakableFormationLook turns a member blue once it has arrived in its slot (in formation and IsStable). The core glow, the inner rim and the exhaust trail all change, and a GlitterSwirl halo fades in behind the sphere. The halo doesn't sway or spin with its balloon: the service gives every member its formation's heading (IFormationMember.FormationHeading, a world direction), and the look turns the halo so its swirl drifts that way. A line heads along its slots, and an item cover heads up at the item. The unbreakable's periodic burst is tagged blue too, so its specks match. All the blue comes from the palette's UnbreakableFormation entry. See Balloon/Type/README.md.

Settings

The settings are IUnbreakableFormationSettings, nested in BalloonsConfiguration under "Unbreakable Formations":

  • Enabled
  • MinMembers (3) and MaxMembers (5)
  • HoldWaves (3)
  • BottomClearance (2) and HorizontalBottomClearance (4, for horizontal lines)
  • HorizontalMaxMembers (3, for horizontal lines; an item cover always takes six)
  • SnakeMaxMembers (4) and SnakeStepPulses (1: a step on every pulse)
  • SpotChoices (3)
  • ReinforcementChance (0.35) and ReinforcementCeiling (4)
  • the shape weights: horizontal 1, each diagonal 1, item cover 2, snake 1

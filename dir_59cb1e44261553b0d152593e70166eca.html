Detailed Description

Represents balloons in the game — their state, appearance, spawning, and destruction.

The grid changing, a cluster being hit, a merge of the last pass or a merge held back only mark that there is something to look at on the next off-beat, and with nothing marked an off-beat scans nothing. A pass picks pairs that share no cluster, so a cluster takes part in one merge a beat and a group gathers a step a beat. A cluster that foams leaves the grid, so the clusters it gave bubbles to are looked at again. A merge held back that way stays marked, so it is tried again on every off-beat and is made on the one where the room opens, with no change on the board needed. So six bubbles still always means ripe. It limits what a cluster is given, never what it holds: one already at the cap keeps its bubbles and ripens. The gap is counted on IBoardBeat, the rhythm clusters merge on, so it is over on an off-beat, when the merge service looks. The off-beat count of the last ending is kept with the level it ended on: the beat runs on from level to level, but a level starts with no gap to wait out; a run reset clears it (IRunResettable), and with ripeness off (FuseSeconds 0) it limits nothing. It wakes like the merge service (the grid's version, a hit on a cluster, a merge, which it looks at on the next tick because the merge message goes out before the counts change) and otherwise only advances the fuses it tracks. It tracks the models, not their slots, so a cluster the balancer moves keeps its fuse, and drops one that is no longer the actor in its own slot. A ripe cluster popped by a hit bursts as the player's ripple, and RipeRoom is told it ended. A fuse that runs out hands the cluster's bubbles out (RipeBubbleBurst.PlanFoam, then HandOut): a coat to each neighbour that takes one, then a bubble at a time to neighbouring clusters with room below BubbleMergeMax, and any left over pop harmlessly. The Foam message, published before the models change, carries that plan one entry per bubble. The cluster then leaves the board with no hit and no score (IBalloonPopper.TryLeave), still ripe, so it keeps its warning colour while its view empties. Expired clusters leave the expired list one at a time, so a stop heard while the foam is announced unripens the ones still waiting rather than leaving them ripe with no fuse, and one that left its slot or stopped being ripe in the meantime is only cleared. A due burst leaves the queue before its ripple runs, so one whose hits throw is not run again. Fuses that run out are collected while the fuses are walked and handed out after, so nothing a foam sets off can change the list under the walk. Each burst publishes RipeBubbleBurstMessage (Ripple or Foam, at the slot's world position, with how many balloons it touched: a ripple carries its blast's radius and the count of hits that landed, a foam the neighbours it coated as one bit per direction in HexCoordinates.HexNeighborIndices order), each fast throb in a fuse's last ThrobFastSeconds a RipeFuseBeatMessage, a ripening BubbleRipenedMessage. The hits carry DamageFlags.Ripple and the bursting shot, never DirectHit, so they earn no pop-spawn, and since they land after the pop that set them off they score at the shot's streak as it stands and never change it (no climb, no break); a cluster in the blast loses one bubble and a ripe one bursts in turn, rippling from its own slot. The count it returns is the hits that had an outcome. With the hits go the pieces of a bomb's blast, each at its own smaller scale and with its own setting: a shockwave nudge of the whole board from the burst, sent before the hits (BlastNudgeOverrides), an inward pull then the BubbleRipple profile's outward shock on the disturbance field (BlastImplosionRadiusScale, BlastImplosionStrengthScale, BlastImplosionSeconds), and a flash of light (SceneLightFieldService.Flash with BlastLightRadius, BlastLightIntensity, BlastLightSeconds). Only as many clusters as RipeRoom.FillsLeft allows take the bubble that fills them, counted as the plan fills them (the foaming cluster itself is left out of the count: it is leaving); the others stop a bubble short and that bubble is a leftover. HandOut applies it (one more coat layer, one more bubble, checked against the same limit with its own running count), makes the giver leave the board once its view has emptied (RipeBubbleRule.FoamLeaveSeconds), and presses the water with the BubbleFoam stamp. Rendering and GPU instancing

Balloon materials (BalloonMaterial, SpecularBalloonBlur, etc.) have GPU instancing enabled. Per-balloon color is applied via SpriteRenderer.color, which Unity streams to the shader as unity_SpriteRendererColorArray in the instancing buffer. All 6 custom shaders follow the UnitySprites.cginc pattern with a _RendererColor fallback for non-SpriteRenderer components. Balloon sprites are packed into Balloons.spriteatlas for draw call reduction. Drop shadows are pre-baked where used (e.g. the Balloon and Unbreakable prefabs): SpriteShadowBaker (Shared/Rendering/) bakes the blurred child-sprite silhouette offline into a BakedShadow child sprite, replacing runtime blur-shadow materials. See Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/README.md for the instancing policy and Assets/Sprites/README.md for atlas groupings.

Behaviour

A balloon knows its type, how many hits it can absorb, where it sits in the grid, whether it has settled into position, and whether it carries an item. BalloonModel additionally knows its color (IPaintable). When any reactive property changes, the view updates automatically via UniRx subscriptions.

BalloonController receives its own hits directly: each controller registers with BalloonControllerRegistry on spawn, and HitPipeline (Game/) resolves the owning controller by model and calls HandleHit — no per-balloon subscriptions or broadcast filtering. It routes on the pre-computed msg.Outcome:

  • PassThrough — balloon survived; projectile continues. HitsRemaining was already decremented inside BalloonModelBase.EvaluateHit. Crack animation is driven reactively by HitsRemaining.
  • Deflect — balloon survived and projectile bounced. Publishes BalloonDeflectedMessage and NudgeMessage(Deflect).
  • Pop — balloon destroyed: BalloonPopPresenter.Present plays the pop (colored stamp + rainbow flash + tough smoke + on paper an ink blot smeared along the shot + hit VFX), then removes from grid and returns to pool (deferred if the balloon carries an item). A view binding that implements IBalloonPopPrelude can play first: the balloon leaves the grid and registry at once, and the pop is presented when the prelude ends (a ripe cluster folds into one bubble over IRipeBubbleSettings.BurstSeconds). Overkill damage is not tracked. The hit VFX resolves the authored per-type prefab via the view's overrides, falling back to a palette-colored default only for colored balloons. An override marked AlongHit is turned so its local up follows the shot (msg.ProjectileDirection; a board clear has none and leaves it upright), so a burst authored to fly up flies on through the balloon. An entry whose PopPulse is on doesn't vanish: it leaves the grid, the registry and its type count at once, loses its collider, and BalloonView.PlayPopPulse swells it to the pulse's peak and shrinks it to nothing before it goes back to the pool (the heart balloon's beat) — so colorless heavies rely on their override being wired (set at bind by the controller and, for overflow, in RejectedBalloonEffect.Play).
  • Absorb — the actor destroys the projectile. ProjectileHitResolver dispatches ActorHitMessage(Absorb) via IHitDispatcher.Dispatch and marks the projectile no longer free; BalloonController.HandleHit no-ops on this outcome, so the absorbing actor is not removed from the grid. No balloon model actually returns Absorb — BalloonModelBase.EvaluateHit defaults to PassThrough, and ToughBalloonModel/UnbreakableBalloonModel override the survive case to Deflect; Absorb is produced by other (non-balloon) actor types.

EvaluateHit on BalloonModelBase is state-mutating — it decrements HitsRemaining (or zeroes it for Piercing) and returns the outcome in a single call. ProjectileHitResolver calls it once, wraps it in a DamageContext(1, flags, projectile's source color) (flags always includes DamageFlags.DirectHit for a contact hit), and dispatches the outcome via IHitDispatcher.Dispatch; BalloonController reads msg.Outcome without calling EvaluateHit again.

Neighbor nudges for every hit are handled independently by NudgeService in Nudge/, which subscribes to ActorHitMessage (filtering by IHasNudge) and dispatches push-out → return animations to all 6 grid neighbors that also implement IHasNudge.

Pooling

Balloon views are pooled via PoolManager / BalloonPoolChannel. A CompositeDisposable replaces AddTo(this) for reactive subscriptions — cleared on each Bind() and OnDespawned(). External subscribers (e.g. BalloonController's transient item-activation subscription) register via RegisterDisposeOnDespawn() so they're cleaned up on pool return. IBalloonViewBinding components (e.g. ToughBalloonVariant) receive the same CompositeDisposable so their subscriptions are cleared automatically in the same lifecycle.

Animation phases

Lines arrive on a timer (SpawnClock), not after a shot, so these phases are not a fixed sequence: a hit, a balance pass and a line entering can all be running at once. What follows names the four kinds of motion.

  1. Hit phase — projectile bounces and pops or deflects balloons. Each hit nudges neighbors outward and back (handled by NudgeService). BalloonBalancer.Tick also pulses a rebalance at IBalloonsConfiguration.FlightRebalanceInterval all through play (not only while a ball is airborne) — but only when HasPossibleMove() reports the stack can still settle, and not while paused or while a lone doomed ball glides — so a projectile looping wall-to-wall eventually gets a balloon shifted into its path. SecondsUntilNextPulse tells the shot solver when the next one lands.
  2. Balance phase — before each line spawns, the spawner runs a Balance() pass that consolidates existing balloons upward. Transit slots are reserved per-actor in BalancePathHolder and released when the animation completes. GridBalanceQuery scores each candidate slot by its support cone, plus a per-actor offset injected via IBalanceInfluence.WeightBias. Each balloon subtype interprets the entry's single BalanceBias float with its own strategy: BalloonModel biases toward candidates whose diagonal hex neighbours share this balloon's color (drifting same-color balloons into diagonal lines); ToughBalloonModel biases toward candidates that extend the longest straight line of same-type balloons along one of the three hex axes (forming walls rather than lumps). Each pass is a race round: unbalanced actors act in BalancePriority order (higher intervenes first and wins contested slots; ties keep sweep order), and the entry's MaxBalanceSteps caps how many slots an actor rises per balance window — the runs from one wave's start to the next (pre-line sweeps and deferred post-line balances) share one budget, and each time-paced pulse opens a fresh one, so a 1-step heavy still crawls between waves (0 = unlimited) — so heavier types act late and settle slower. Each balance move animates at a constant speed: its duration is the hop path length ÷ the balloon's resolved MoveSpeed (per-type BalloonPrefabEntry.MoveSpeed, else IBalloonsConfiguration.DefaultBalloonMoveSpeed, with a one-off ± MoveSpeedVariation roll applied at spawn), so a multi-cell rise takes proportionally longer than a single hop instead of racing a fixed duration; TimeForBalloonsBalance now only times the settle scale-in. Balance requests coalesce: a direct Balance() run (the spawner's pre-line sweep) services any pending deferred request, so a BalanceBalloonsMessage published the same frame never duplicates it. Once per wave (the sweep before a wave's first line, Balance(atWaveStart: true)), a pre-pass runs before the race: the balancer finds every IPreBalanceRelocatable actor, offers it the board's resting slots (empty, needing no further settling), and invokes the contract in BalancePriority order — each implementer defines its own placement. UnbreakableBalloonModel implements it as a jump toward the board's opposite side, preferring far slots (squared-distance weighted pick), so it never camps one flank; the race then settles everyone else around the new positions. Before that pre-pass, UnbreakableFormationService.ArrangeAtWaveStart may gather three or more unbreakables into a formation (Formation/README.md): its members are anchored (IBalanceAnchor), so the roam, the race and pressure shoves all leave them in their slots until the formation changes or breaks. Once three toughs are on the board, the toughest starts a single snake of their own, anchored the same way; every other tough lines up behind it at once, pushing balloons out of the way, each tough that lands later falls in behind its tail, and it walks through the balloons in its way, each one it passes going round to the slot its tail leaves. TryRelievePressure is different: it runs PressurePropagation, a directed weighted search that bends a board-up shove through occupied movables — each hop scored by its alignment with the incoming shove minus a heaviness cost (HeavinessPenalty / MaxBalanceSteps), so chains route around heavy movers when lighter routes exist; the step cap and BalancePriority never gate under pressure (a shoved balloon moves regardless), heaviness only prices the path. Actors whose entry sets DirectBalanceMotion (e.g. the unbreakable) animate straight from where they are to the resolve's final slot instead of touring every recorded waypoint — the disturbance wake follows that direct line, matching the visual.
  3. Spawn phase — each clock tick spawns one line, synchronously. Spawn uses standalone DOMove + DOScale tweens (not tracked). ComputePath consults the BalancePathHolder to detect traversal conflicts with still-animating balance moves. Each wave (LinesPerWave consecutive lines, or the initial fill) rolls a per-type spawn quota at its first line (later lines in the wave draw down what is left) from the range's count-weight curves (BalloonTypeWeight.InitialCountWeights / WaveCountWeights, sampled like item counts — chance of 0, 1, n; empty curve = uncapped) alongside the concurrent MaxCountOverride, then pre-picks the batch of entries (the whole initial fill, or one line) and deals them lightest-first by the catalog entry's SpawnWeight (the type's physical spawn weight) — in the initial fill successive lines land progressively lower, and within each line the columns are processed shallowest-first (ProbeEntryRow depth order, random tie-break), so the heaviest types (e.g. tough) always take the lowest available slots. Pure ordering: the batch's type mix is untouched. Active counts are reserved at batch time and released for entries the batch never places.
  4. Post-spawn balance — after each line a BalanceBalloonsMessage triggers a deferred balance pass (next frame) to settle any newly created gaps.

Additionally, a direct projectile pop (DamageFlags.DirectHit — never AOE items) can earn one extra balloon, spawned immediately: each pop rolls IBalloonsConfiguration.PopSpawnChance (global, not per level; 0 = off), eased while the player is in need (Game/Assist/ThreatEase). The loose spawn takes its column's own entry only — no rehoming, no pressure, no overflow charge, columns without room skipped — and is excluded from item checks. Each spawn is budget-gated: the automated spawns stop at grid capacity minus IBalloonsConfiguration.PopSpawnFreeRows × columns (headroom further clamped to one line's worth, one row of columns), so they can never pack the play space solid.

Motion arbitration

Position motion has two independent drivers: DOTween (spawn and balance paths, sequenced by the TweenTracker component on each view) and BalloonMotionTicker (nudge impulses). Rather than one driver cancelling the other, the ticker runs last in the frame as an ILateTickable and reconciles instead of fighting: each tick it compares a view's current position against what it wrote last tick, and if something else (DOTween, a pool teleport) moved the transform in between, it silently adopts that as the new base.

  • Nudge (BalloonView.Nudge) calls BalloonMotionTicker.AddImpulse(this, direction.normalized * distance, duration) — no transform.DOKill(), no interaction with spawn/balance tweens at all. Impulses stack: up to MaxImpulsesPerView (8) run concurrently per view, each contributing an eased out-and-back offset on top of the reconciled base position; past the cap, a new impulse overwrites whichever running one is closest to finishing rather than queuing.
  • Balance (BalloonBalancer.AnimatePaths) calls TweenTracker.Kill() + transform.DOKill(), then appends its DOPath via tracker.Append(tween). It never touches the ticker — any nudge impulses already running on that view keep applying on top of the balance motion; the ticker just adopts the balance tween's positions as its new base each tick.
  • Despawn calls TweenTracker.Kill() + transform.DOKill() + BalloonMotionTicker.CancelAll(this) — drops the view's impulses without a final write, since the pool is about to reposition it anyway.

Idle sway

A balloon rocks about its knot all the time it's on the board: gently while it rests in its slot, widely while it moves (IsStable off: flying in, balancing). The rocking turns one child of the prefab, Ballon, which BalloonView.RotationPivot returns; the baked shine and shadow sit outside it and stay put.

The sway is authored as an Animator controller, Assets/Animation/Balloon/Balloon.controller: an empty default state, a StableIdle and an UnstableIdle clip (3 s loops, ±7° and ±35° about Z), and the IsStable bool switching between them. The plain balloons no longer run it. An Animator on every balloon cost about a sixth of the main thread's frame on a tablet, to write one rotation each. BalloonSwayTicker plays the same sway for all of them in one loop:

  • Baked, not retyped. Tools > BalloonParty > Balloons > Bake Idle Sway (Editor/BalloonIdleSwayBaker) copies the two clips' Z curves and the four transitions' times into IBalloonsConfiguration.IdleSway (IBalloonIdleSwaySettings), with the controller they came from. Change the sway in the controller or its clips, then bake again; ShippedBalloonIdleSwayTests fails while the two differ. The baker refuses a controller that does anything the ticker doesn't play. A curve is baked as the Animator plays it, which is not always as it was drawn: where a key's tangent is weighted (two of StableIdle's are) the Animator plays the stretch as straight lines between the clip's 60 frames a second, up to a hundredth of a degree off the curve, and the baker writes that stretch as a key a frame.
  • Stepped as the Animator stepped. BalloonSwayPlayback is one balloon's state: the playing loop and how far into it, and during a crossfade the loop being entered and the crossfade's progress. Its rules were read off a real Animator step by step. Each step moves what is playing on, then looks at IsStable. A step that finds the other loop is wanted starts a 0.75 s crossfade into it at no progress, the new loop at its beginning, so that step's pose is still the old loop's; from the next step the crossfade moves on, the old loop still playing under it. The two poses mix as rotations, not as angles: their quaternions are weighed by the crossfade's progress, summed and normalised, which is up to a tenth of a degree off a straight line between the angles. A crossfade is never interrupted: a flip during one waits; the step the crossfade ends on plays the entered loop alone, and the step after starts the crossfade back. A spawn is the odd case. The Animator's empty default state has no pose to weigh against a clip's, so nothing fades in: the wanted loop plays whole from the frame the balloon spawns on. The 0.25 s enter transition still runs, uninterruptible, so a flip in a balloon's first quarter second waits for it.
  • Same place in the frame, same clock. The ticker is an ILateTickable on Time.deltaTime: after everything Update spawned, before any LateUpdate.
  • Which balloons. At its first spawn a BalloonView checks whether its Animator plays the baked controller (IBalloonIdleSwaySettings.Source). If so the ticker sways it and its Animator stays disabled: Balloon, Balloon5, Balloon10, BalloonRainbow, SoapCluster and Heart. Any other controller keeps its Animator, because it animates more than a sway: ToughBalloon.controller (ToughBalloon, TougherBalloon, Golden, Disco, Nuke) also scales the body, turns and scales the shine and drives two material properties, and Unbreakable.controller turns the shell on an 8.4 s loop and plays a one-shot. The Animator component stays on Balloon.prefab because those variants inherit it and point it at their own controller.
  • Spawn, despawn, float-away. OnSpawned begins the sway, OnDespawned ends it, and SuspendAnimator ends it for a balloon handed to the level-up float-away, whose tween owns the pivot's rotation. These are the points where the Animator was enabled, reset and disabled.
  • Reuse. A reused balloon starts its sway over, level and on the unstable loop, whatever tilt its last life left on the pivot (a pop mid-swing, a float-away's lean). So did its Animator, whose state and parameters reset each time it was enabled.
  • The measurement switch. CheatState.PerfOff.Animators (dev builds) spawns balloons with no sway at all: the ticker isn't told about them and the kept Animators are disabled.

What proves the match: BalloonSwayAnimatorParityTests steps a real Animator beside a playback and compares the pivot's angle at every step (BalloonSwayAnimatorParityPlayModeTests does the same in the player loop), and ShippedBalloonIdleSwayTests plays each loop on an Animator against its baked curve.

Scale recovery

When balance interrupts a spawning balloon, transform.DOKill() kills the standalone scale tween mid-animation. BalloonBalancer.StartBalanceTween captures the current scale beforehand and creates a parallel DOScale(Vector3.one, duration) if the balloon hasn't reached full size — so it smoothly finishes scaling alongside the balance move. Nudge no longer touches scale at all (it only adds a ticker impulse), so a mid-scale-up balloon that gets nudged keeps scaling on its own original tween.

Interactions

  • SlotGrid — balloons occupy positions in it; stores models and views in parallel arrays. Spawner places, controller removes, balancer relocates. _grid.ViewAt(slotIndex) reaches views from models.
  • BalloonBalancer — moves balloons when gaps appear; runs before each line spawns (pre-line consolidation), after it (deferred via BalanceBalloonsMessage), and at FlightRebalanceInterval intervals all through play (gated on HasPossibleMove(), pauses and a lone doomed ball). Reserves transit slots in BalancePathHolder per-actor; releases on animation complete
  • NudgeService — subscribes to ActorHitMessage and NudgeMessage; nudges neighboring and deflecting balloon views (executed by BalloonMotionTicker via BalloonView.Nudge)
  • ProjectileHitResolver — calls balloon.EvaluateHit(new DamageContext(1, flags, projectile.ColorName.Value)) on collision (flags always includes DamageFlags.DirectHit) and dispatches the pre-computed outcome via IHitDispatcher.Dispatch
  • ScoreController — on each ActorHitMessage with Outcome == Pop or PassThrough, casts actor to IHasScoreColor and calls ResolveScoreAttribution(msg.Context, attributions). All returned ScoreAttribution entries are published together as one scatter group — a single GroupSize covers every point from the pop so the UI fans them out together.
  • PoolManager — BalloonView instances are pooled via BalloonPoolChannel; pop VFX pooled via ParticlePoolChannel
  • TweenTracker — generic MonoBehaviour in Shared/ that manages tween sequencing (append, replace, kill)
  • Item system — balloons are one host for the game-wide item system (in Item/). BalloonView calls ItemDisplayService.Bind()/Unbind() to connect item visuals; the item system itself has no knowledge of balloons
  • BalloonsConfiguration — balloon prefab entries, spawn/balance travel speed (DefaultBalloonMoveSpeed fallback + per-type MoveSpeed, MoveSpeedVariation), balance timing, nudge defaults, pop VFX
  • GamePalette — resolves color name → UnityEngine.Color for renderer tinting
  • DisturbanceFieldService — BalloonController stamps on pop (BalloonPop profile), BalloonBalancer stamps during balance path (BalloonPath profile), BalloonSpawner stamps during spawn path (BalloonPath profile) — both distance-gated (one stamp per Spacing/Radius of travel, not per frame, so wake density is frame-rate independent) — and BalloonController.Deflect stamps at the balloon's slot when a projectile deflects off it and its entry sets DeflectStampScale (BalloonDeflect profile × the scale — unbreakable wide, tough slot-sized; pops already stamp via BalloonPop), and ToughBalloonVariant pulses a warning stamp while a tough sits at 1 hit left — the reserved Tough palette color while alternating a repel/attract pulse each tick (pushing the field out, then pulling it back in — a "breathing" halo) at the ToughWarning profile's Interval (the profile supplies cadence + magnitude + radius; the variant flips the sign; 0 interval = off) — and on the same beat puffs specks at the balloon by publishing a SpeckSpawnRequestMessage (SpeckSource.ToughWarning), so a tough in its danger state grows a swarm that adopts the Tough color from the tag it just wrote. RainbowBalloonVariant does the same constantly (not gated on HP) — a single color-only stamp (RainbowColor, authored Strength = 0 so it applies no force, R stays at rest) that tags nearby specks with the next of the level's allowed colors each pulse, so they cycle the palette. The profile's Interval paces how fast the rainbow cycles colors. UnbreakableBalloonVariant runs a periodic burst (UnbreakableBurst), tagged its own reserved Unbreakable palette color, that shoves the field's clouds out and, at the same point, enables a matching puff of specks by publishing a SpeckSpawnRequestMessage (the generalized "spawn N specks here" request the SpeckField resolves against its SpeckProfile for that SpeckSource). Both the cadence and the force come from the profile: StampProfile.Interval is the seconds between emissions (0 = not a periodic emitter). All these constant emitters pass reportImpact: false so their steady pulses don't rustle bushes. The field's R channel is signed density around a 0.5 rest: a stamp's Strength > 0 bumps R up (repulsion — specks pushed away, the default for pops/bombs/etc.), Strength < 0 digs R down (attraction — specks pulled in), and Strength = 0 writes only the A-channel color tag (pure color, no force, no bush rustle). Specks flow down the R gradient; a color tag lights a speck by the tag's remaining life independent of agitation — so color and force are separable — all via Stamp() using StampProfile from DisturbanceFieldSettings. Creates visible disturbances in Puff clouds
  • SmokeFieldService — BalloonController.Deflect paints at the deflecting balloon's visual position with PaintSource.ToughDeflect (tough balloons only). ToughBalloonVariant paints at the repel apex of its breathing cycle with PaintSource.ToughBreathing while at 1 hit remaining

Colour Balance

Colour balance — crisp gameplay colours over every scene

Keep the colours that carry the game crisp, contrasting and recognisable in all three scenarios at every time of day, and let only real light sources glow. Written 2026-11-11 after the first scenario grade (see ScenarioGradeView) made the meadow look washed out. It measures the eight colours that matter most on the rendered frame, sets targets for them from colour-science research, and changes the tonemapper and the grades to meet them.

Works with HDR Rendering, which switched on HDR rendering, the tonemapper and bloom.

What the research says

The full notes, with every source, are summarised here; the numbers in brackets are the references at the end.

Telling colours apart.

  • Measure differences in CIEDE2000 [1, 2] and design in OKLCh [3]. A difference of 1 is the laboratory threshold; on a real screen, at the size of a mark, people need about five times more, and colours become nameably different above about 20 [5, 6].
  • Clutter and motion raise the threshold [5, 9]. Balloons are judged at a glance in a busy field, so aim for 30.
  • Lightness is the channel that survives small size, colour-blindness, desaturation and darkness [10, 11]. Colours that differ only by hue collapse first. Today red and blue are only 2 L* apart.
  • Deuteranopes keep lightness and the blue–yellow axis only [8]. Today green and red are 8 apart for them, well under 20.

Standing out from the backdrop.

  • An object reads by its lightness edge; colour alone makes a weak edge [11]. The closest standard for graphics is WCAG's 3:1 contrast ratio against the adjacent colour [13].
  • A darker, less saturated surround makes the same colour look more saturated (simultaneous contrast). Low contrast reads as distance [12], so lowering the backdrop's chroma pushes it back; lifting its black only makes it look hazy.

Night.

  • Darkness costs colourfulness (Hunt effect [16]) and contrast (Stevens effect [17]). A dark frame is its own surround, which lowers perceived contrast further [18].
  • A night picture keeps true black and sharp silhouettes and loses low-contrast texture [34]. Night renderings darken, shift toward blue and lose colour, not black [35, 36].
  • So a night grade adds contrast and darkens and cools the backdrop; it never lifts the shadows, and it leaves the gameplay colours as close to their day look as it can.

Glow.

  • A surface starts to look self-luminous at about 1.7× the luminance of the scene's white [19]; saturated colours get there sooner [21].
  • A halo (bloom) around a bright core adds 20–35 % to its apparent brightness, and a Gaussian halo is enough [20, 22, 23].
  • So: keep surfaces below white, author emitters at 2–4×, and set the bloom threshold between the two. Only emitters glow; saturation is not glow.

Tonemapping.

  • Curves applied per RGB channel change the ratio between channels: hues drift toward the primaries and highlights wash out [24–27]. Compress lightness, then reduce chroma at constant hue [27–29].
  • URP's Neutral tonemapper is a per-channel Hable curve [25, 33]. On this palette it takes 25–30 % of the chroma and turns blue 14° toward cyan at 1× (31° at 4×).
  • Khronos PBR Neutral [30] leaves everything below 0.76 alone, compresses only the brightest channel and blends toward white at constant hue. On this palette it keeps hue within 1° and chroma within 7 % at 1×.

Targets

Measured on the final frame (after the tonemapper and the grade), for each scenario at noon and at midnight, by balloons (where each balloon is) and palette_measure2.py (the numbers).

Metric Target Why
CIEDE2000 between every pair of gameplay colours ≥ 20, aim ≥ 30 nameably different at a glance [5]
Same, for a simulated deuteranope and protanope ≥ 15 about 1 in 12 men [8]
Lightness between gameplay colours ≥ 10 L* where the palette allows lightness survives everything else [10, 11]
Each balloon against the backdrop around it ≥ 3:1 contrast, or a rim that reaches it figure–ground [13]
A balloon's colour from scene to scene hue within 5°, chroma ≥ 85 % of noon the player learns one colour, not six
The backdrop's darkest 1 % not raised by the grade a raised black reads as haze [12, 34]
The backdrop's median chroma below about 60 % of the balloons' simultaneous contrast works for the balloons
Pixels above surface white only on emitters glow is for light sources [19]

Phases

Phase 1 — a hue-preserving tonemapper, and no lift · done

  • PBR Neutral in the grading LUT. URP picks the shader that builds the HDR grading LUT from the renderer's PostProcessData asset. Assets/Settings/URP/GamePostProcessData.asset is a copy of URP's with one change: it builds the LUT with Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/Display/LutBuilderHdr.shader, a copy of URP's that tonemaps with Khronos PBR Neutral when the volume asks for Neutral. The tonemapper is baked into the LUT, so the frame costs the same. The Scenario Grade cheat swaps back to URP's curve for comparison.
  • Re-tune the base look in Assets/DefaultVolumeProfile.asset. The +0.35 stops and +15 contrast compensated for URP Neutral darkening the midtones (0.5 became 0.42); PBR Neutral does not. The base is now +15 contrast and −10 saturation. PBR Neutral keeps the authored colours below 0.76 as authored, and the contrast adds some chroma on top, so the frame came out about 35 % more colourful than under URP Neutral; the user found that a bit much, and −10 takes the edge off without moving any pair under 30. +0.2 stops and +20 contrast measured close.
  • Remove the meadow's shadow lift and the global desaturation from ScenarioGradeSettings.asset. A lift adds the same amount to every channel, which is what haze does: it cuts contrast and chroma together.

Phase 2 — night darkens the backdrop, not the balloons · balloon body done

  • Done: the balloon body (Sprite/SpriteDiffuse.shader on DiffuseSprite.mat) takes a quarter of the time-of-day light's colour (_AmbientHue 0.25) and 40 % of its dimming (_AmbientDimming 0.4). Local lights (a pop, an item) still tint it in full. Before, the balloons took all of the blue midnight light: red turned maroon, green turned teal, and blue and purple sank into the night sky. Four settings were compared at night in all three scenes; this one kept every pair furthest apart and every hue within 11° of its noon hue (OKLCh).
  • Done: kept that bright at full chroma, the balloons looked more vivid than the night around them; colourfulness falls with light (the Hunt effect [16]). _AmbientDesaturation (0.5) fades the balloon's colour toward a grey of the same OKLab lightness as the time-of-day light darkens: nothing at noon, about a quarter of the chroma at midnight. A luma grey was tried first and turned red and purple muddy and green pale, because saturated colours look lighter than their luma (Helmholtz–Kohlrausch [15]).
  • Done: the balloon glint (Sprite/SpriteLightDriven.shader on ShineRotation.mat and ShineRotationTough.mat) takes the same hue 0.25 and dimming 0.4 as the body. It had kept the full midnight light, so it went darker than the balloon under it: the balloons read as flat discs in the night sky, and the tough balloon, a black body whose shape is mostly its glint, nearly vanished on the night meadow. The tough body (ToughBalloon.shader) takes the same two controls. The re-grade lives in SceneLightAmbientRegrade (SceneLight.cginc) for all three.
  • Open: the unbreakable and rainbow balloons have their own shaders and still take the full night light; give them the same two controls if they read too dark at night.
  • Open: the backdrops keep their own night look. Cut their chroma at night only if the balloons still don't separate.

Phase 3 — palette · red done

  • Done, signed off by the user: red went from (1, 0.412, 0.380) to (0.84, 0.289, 0.21): the same hue and chroma, OKLab lightness 0.71 → 0.60 (L* 63.5 → 51). Lightness is what a deuteranope keeps, so green–red went from 8 to about 19 for them (authored values), and red moved from 6 to 10 away from the aim line (Prediction). Three candidates were rendered; at full chroma the darker red looked more saturated than the old one, so the chroma stayed where it was.
  • Open: check green against the pale sky and purple against the night sky and water; give either a rim if it misses 3:1.

Phase 4 — glow only on emitters

  • Sparks, the shield halo, lightning and the laser are the emitters (see HDR Rendering). Bloom's threshold is 1.4, above every surface; check no balloon surface crosses it after Phase 1–3.
  • The screen-space light overlay caps how far it brightens: at noon a white balloon stays under the bloom start, at night the bounce keeps its room to colour neighbours (HDR Rendering, task 2.8).

What it shows:

  • Every pair clears 30 in every scene, night included (it was 17–19 at night on the sky and the water). The blacks went down everywhere, so nothing reads as haze.
  • For a deuteranope the closest pair went from 8–11 to 14–27; green–red is no longer the weakest, blue–purple is (13–20, unchanged).
  • At night the balloons are about as colourful as before, but in their own hues; at noon they are more colourful than before, which the −10 saturation tempers.
  • Still short: on the pale noon sky and the water the lightest balloons sit about 1.2:1 from the backdrop, and purple and red are 1.1–1.5:1 against the night sky and water. They are told apart by hue there, not lightness. A rim, or a lighter purple, would fix it (Phase 3, open).

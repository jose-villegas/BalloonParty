Detailed Description

Four balloons are not threats, and no level's mix brings them. Three are rewards; the fourth, the nuke, is a rescue (see The nuke). On a purple level all three rewards are rolled every few lines, each further one rarer: the heart balloon from the level's start, up to five, even at full hearts, where its pop gives an extra heart past the maximum; the golden balloon from 15% done, up to three; the disco ball from 25% done, the first one sure, up to three. Level 5 always gets a single golden one early on, the player's first, and level 8 a single disco ball; after that, any ordinary level may get one or the other, about every 3 levels each: a little likelier early in the run, and likelier the longer since the last. Off a purple level the heart comes to a player who is missing hearts, more often the more are missing. The Party Favors card raises every one of these rolls' chances by 25% a stack (IWeaponStats.RewardBalloonOdds, Game/Upgrades/README.md), the nuke's too. At most one of each is on the board. The plans are Plans/PLAN-RewardBalloons.md and Plans/PLAN-DiscoBalloon.md; the nuke's is Plans/PLAN-NukeBalloon.md. The disco ball's pop paints the board rainbow (see The disco ball's wave).

The golden balloon itself is Balloon/Model/GoldenBalloonModel, a PlatedBalloonModel, whose hit rules a third reward balloon, the disco ball (DiscoBalloonModel, Plans/PLAN-DiscoBalloon.md), shares: the shot's own ordinary hit bounces off it, and it takes hits as an unbreakable does: any Piercing damage pops it. That is a pierce (it is tough, so the pierce plows it and it shatters at the next wall), a bomb's blast beside it, or a laser beam (the laser also marks its damage DamageFlags.Beam, which pops it even without Piercing). An item's plain damage (lightning, paint) has no DirectHit, and its hit is HitOutcome.None. It is colourless and scoreless, resists paint, and carries no item. Its prefab (Prefabs/Balloon/Golden) is built from the tough balloon's, so it looks like a balloon, with a gold-plated body (Shaders/BalloonParty/Balloon/GoldenBalloon.shader) and a dark gold knot. Faint god rays turn and breathe behind it (Balloon/Type/GodRaysLook, the Shaders/BalloonParty/Sprite/GodRays.shader fan on the GoldenRays material), so it stands out on a busy board: they sort below every balloon, so they show in the gaps around it. Their colour and strength are the palette's GoldenRays entry; their shape and motion are the material's. The prefab keeps the tough balloon's variant, which only draws cracks for a balloon with durability, so on the golden one it does nothing. Its pop shatters it into gold plates (PSVFX_GoldenPop, the unbreakable's shatter drawing the Sprites/Balloons/Golden/golden_parts sheet, cut from a capture of the balloon by Tools/art/shatter), lit by the time of day like the balloon (Particle/SceneLit). It has its own cues (CombatSoundRouter): BalloonDeflectGolden when the shot bounces off, the gold pop's chime (Chime 0010) a fifth above the board's key with a high gold glint over it, climbing on each further bounce of the same shot; and BalloonPopGolden when it breaks, the gold-band balloon's pop with a balloon's burst under it and the chime an octave up as a coin ring. Both are layered from clips already in the sound bank (SoundBankConfiguration), so they can be retuned there.

The heart balloon is Balloon/Model/HeartBalloonModel: any hit pops it, the shot's or an item's, whatever its catalogue entry's hit count says. It is colourless and scoreless, resists paint, and carries no item, so its pop leaves the streak alone. Its pop gives back one heart, never to a player already out: up to the maximum, or on a purple level past it, as an extra heart (read from the level being played when it pops, so one popped after a purple level ends heals as on the next level). Its prefab (Prefabs/Balloon/Heart) is the balloon's, with a heart body and bandages drawn by Tools/art/make_heart_balloon.py, and its own highlight (Shaders/BalloonParty/Balloon/HeartShine.shader, lit from the heart's baked normals), because the round balloons' shine is drawn for a circle. Fainter god rays than the golden's turn behind it, white and shorter (GodRaysLook again, on the HeartRays material and palette entry). Its pop is a beat: its catalogue entry's PopPulse swells it to 1.25 and shrinks it away, while PSVFX_HeartPop throws a burst of hearts on along the shot, drawn with the HUD heart's own sprite (HeartTrail_Combined: its shine and its soft baked shadow), spinning only in the board's plane so the shadow keeps falling to the lower right (the override's AlongHit; a board clear throws them up). Its pop cue is BalloonPopHeart, a harp pluck (harp005 by t1mp, CC BY) the sound bank pitches to the board's key.

When each one is due

Only while the level is being played (LevelUpPhase.Playing), and only for a type the balloon catalogue has an entry for.

  • On a purple level (IsExtraColorLevel), both kinds roll every PurpleRollEveryLines (6) lines, each on its own count, and each kind's first roll is on the first line it can. Lines while one of that kind is on the board don't count. Each roll's chance is the next entry of the kind's list, so each further one is rarer and the list's length caps the level: PurpleGoldenChances (35%, 20%, 10%: three at most) and PurpleHeartChances (40%, 30%, 20%, 12%, 6%: five at most). The golden rolls only once LevelCompletion reaches PurpleGoldenAtCompletion (0.15; each colour's points, capped at the goal, over all the goals); the heart from the level's first line, at full hearts too, and the roll while hurt (below) is off there. A won roll stays due until it is placed (a line with no room for it passes it on). A new level starts both counts over and drops anything owed. The disco ball rolls like the golden one, from PurpleDiscoAtCompletion (0.25), on PurpleDiscoChances (100%, 25%, 15%): the first is sure, so every purple level gets one. A line brings one balloon at most: the nuke first (see The nuke), then the golden, then the disco ball, then the heart; the others stay due.
  • Golden, on another level. A single one. Every OrdinaryGoldenEveryLevels (1: every) level that isn't purple rolls once, on its first line, against OrdinaryGoldenChance (10%). The chance eases off as the run goes on: it is halved OrdinaryChanceHalvedAfterLevels (20) levels past the sure level, a third at 40 past, so it never reaches 0. A won level places one golden balloon at GoldenAtCompletion (0.5). SureGoldenLevel (5) needs no roll: it always places its one, and early in the level (SureGoldenAtCompletion, 10% done), so every player meets a golden balloon with the level still ahead. Only levels from the sure level on roll.
  • Soft pity. Each level since the last golden balloon was placed (on any level, purple included; from the sure level before the first) adds OrdinaryChanceGrowthPerLevel (12%) to the roll, so an eased chance never stays low: early in a run 20%, 32%, 44%... and one comes on average every 3 levels (3.1 early, 3.6 past level 45, one gap in a hundred reaching 7, in a 20,000-run simulation). A placed one starts the count over; a level that won one but ended before it was placed doesn't.
  • Disco ball, on another level. The golden balloon's rules on its own numbers, a little more often: every OrdinaryDiscoEveryLevels (1) level from SureDiscoLevel (8) on, against OrdinaryDiscoChance (22%, eased and grown by the same rules, on its own count), placed at DiscoAtCompletion (0.35). Level 8 always places its one, at SureDiscoAtCompletion (15% done).
  • Heart, elsewhere. A roll per line while a heart is missing: HeartChanceMissingOne, or HeartChanceMissingTwo with two or more missing. After one is placed, HeartCooldownLines lines pass before the next roll. Each line rolled without one while the player is in need raises the next line's chance (IAssistSettings.HeartPity, a Game/Assist/PityRoll: on the last heart 12%, 18%, 24…). A heart rolled for a line with no room for it is owed to the next line, unless the player has healed to full in between. Extra hearts past the maximum count as full.

The tuning is IRewardBalloonSettings, a block on BalloonsConfiguration.

The disco ball's wave

When a disco ball pops, DiscoWave grows a disc from where it popped until it covers the whole board, in IDiscoWaveSettings.Seconds (0.9 s), fast at first and slowing at the far side. Every balloon the disc's edge reaches is nudged away from the pop once (NudgeType.Wave). Then Item/Paint/PaintTargets decides the rest, with the same rules as the Paint item:

  • a balloon of an ordinary colour turns rainbow. It bursts in its old colour (IPaintReactive.PlayRecolourBurst, a smaller pop particle, with no pop: nothing scores and it stays on the board), takes the paint drip, and stamps the disturbance field;
  • a balloon immune to paint (tough, unbreakable, the golden and heart balloons) takes the drip and keeps its colour;
  • a rainbow balloon, or anything that isn't a balloon, is only nudged.

The disc is fixed where the ball popped, and a balloon that moves into ground it has covered is still reached, on the next frame. When the time is up a last sweep reaches anything left. A run reset, a level change or game over stops the wave where it is; what it painted stays painted. It runs on scaled time, so a pause holds it.

The party outlasts the wave: the pop also owes the next clock lines rainbow (IDiscoWaveSettings.RainbowLines, 3 by default). Balloon/Spawner/BalloonSpawner hands each clock line's new balloons to RainbowLines.PaintLine once they are placed, before the line's item roll. A line that brings a balloon pays one line, by the wave's rules: a paintable balloon arrives rainbow and a paint-immune one keeps its look. A line that brings none (a full board) pays nothing. A second disco pop while lines are still owed tops them up to the setting rather than adding to them. A level change or a run reset forgets what is still owed.

What the wave paints is booked to the shot that broke the ball (Projectile/Controller/ShotPaintLedger). That shot is still flying (a pierce breaks the ball at the next wall, and the shot behind a laser flies on), and a rainbow pop would climb its streak whatever the colour. So its own pops of what it painted score at its streak as it stands, climb nothing, and refund no streak shield (DamageFlags.OwnPaint). Any other shot, and every later throw, pops the rainbow board as usual. The rainbow lines are booked to the same shot while it still flies, so it can't climb on them either.

DiscoWaveView draws the disc (the quad-sizing part is GrowingDiscView, which NukeBlastView shares): a quad centred on the pop and sized to the disc each frame, on IDiscoWaveSettings.DiscMaterial (BalloonParty/Sprite/DiscoWave), which must be assigned or nothing is drawn. The shader draws a bright edge in the level's own colours, turning by angle, and a soft wash behind it that thins as the disc grows. It fades out over the last stretch, so it's gone when the view hides it. It sorts below the balloons, so it sweeps through the gaps, and the balloons show the edge as they turn rainbow. Each painted balloon's drip is a rainbow one (IPaintReactive.PlayPaintDrip's rainbow). The pop itself takes the rainbow balloon's key-light flash (BalloonPopPresenter).

The ball itself shatters into mirror plates: PSVFX_DiscoPop is the golden balloon's shatter drawing Sprites/Balloons/Disco/disco_parts, which Tools/art/shatter cut from a capture of the ball. Its shards are sized to the disco ball's wider body.

The wave's sound follows two messages it publishes: DiscoWaveAdvancedMessage, the share of its time gone (0 as it starts), each frame; and DiscoWaveReachedMessage for each balloon it paints, the immune ones included, with how far from the pop it sits over the disc's full radius. Audio/Routing/DiscoSoundRouter plays them as a rising harp glissando through the board's key and a quiet pluck for each balloon, higher the farther out it is. The ball's own cues are CombatSoundRouter's: BalloonDeflectDisco, a glassy tick (a ping-pong bump under a glassy chime an octave over the board's key), and BalloonPopDisco, the golden balloon's pop with the item-sighted wind chime shimmering over it.

The nuke

The fourth balloon is a rescue, not a prize. It comes rarely, and more often the more trouble the player is in. Shooting it three times, or hitting it with anything piercing, does not pop it: it arms it. A three-second fuse counts down on its body, and then the blast sweeps the board and pops every balloon but the other rewards, and the spawn clock gives a breather. It scores nothing and is placed by the service like the others (Plans/PLAN-NukeBalloon.md).

The balloon. Balloon/Model/NukeBalloonModel is a PlatedBalloonModel that is also IHasDurability, with its hits from its catalogue entry (3). The shot's ordinary hit bounces off and costs one. The last one, or any Piercing damage or laser beam, arms it (Armed) instead of popping it. An item's plain damage does nothing, as on the golden balloon. Once armed it deflects every ball and takes no more damage, and it is anchored (IBalanceAnchor), so the balancing of the board leaves it where it is. Its prefab (Prefabs/Balloon/Nuke) is the golden one's, with a lead-plated body (Balloon/NukeBalloon.shader: bolted plates, a radiation mark on a yellow placard) whose plates tip and darken as it is dented, and red rays behind it (NukeRays palette entry and material). Balloon/Type/NukeFuseView drives the shader's LED counter and status lamp from the fuse's messages: the lamp is green until the nuke is armed and then red, blinking with each beep, and the rays turn red too. It shatters into lead plates (PSVFX_NukePop, the golden balloon's shatter on the Sprites/Balloons/Nuke/nuke_parts sheet, cut by Tools/art/shatter from a capture of the nuke armed). Cues: BalloonDeflectNuke, a lead clank a semitone lower on each dent, and BalloonPopNuke, a deep boom.

When it comes. NukeSchedule asks, first on every clock line while the level is played: from INukeOddsSettings.FromLevel on, PerLevel a level, CooldownLines lines apart, one on the board at most, and for sure on SureLevel (10), SureAfterLines (10) lines in, past the odds and the cooldown. The roll's chance is BaseChance raised toward MaxChance by the weighted sum of four signals (NukeOdds), capped at 1: the board's fill (FillFrom to FillFull), the spawn clock's pressure, PlayerNeed.HeartsNeed (full hearts to the last one), and hearts lost recently (each WaveDamageMessage adds its loss, and each line keeps LossKeptPerLine of the sum). Party Favors multiplies it, so it still only rises in trouble. The settings are IBalloonsConfiguration.NukeOdds.

The fuse. When the nuke is armed, NukeFuse publishes NukeArmedMessage and counts down INukeFuseSettings.Seconds (IBalloonsConfiguration.NukeFuse, 3 s), publishing a NukeCountdownMessage with the whole seconds left each second. NukeFuseView shows the digit and lamp, and NukeSoundRouter plays the alarm once and a beep for each count, each a step higher. Meanwhile the board is pulled in: NukeBlastView.Implode stamps an implosion into the disturbance field (the clouds, grass and specks lean towards the nuke), and every PullInterval the balloons within PullRadius get an inward NudgeType.Wave nudge, a longer one each time. A level change, a game over or a run reset stops the fuse with no blast.

The blast. When the fuse runs out, NukeFuse calls INukeDetonator.Detonate (NukeBlast). In order: the nuke pops; ClearShotsInFlightMessage makes ThrowerController kill every shot in flight (the ball loaded to throw next stays); NukeDetonatedMessage plays the boom. Then a disc grows from the nuke over INukeBlastSettings.Seconds (IBalloonsConfiguration.NukeBlast), by BoardDisc, as the disco wave does. NukeBlastView draws it on Sprite/NukeBlast.shader (a white flood over the board, white-hot core, ragged orange ring, scorch), lights the scene with a blinding light that fades as the disc spreads, and gives the disturbance field the strongest push on it. NukeCloudView spreads a mushroom cloud seen from above (Sprite/NukeCloud.shader, material NukeCloud: a billowing cap that starts as fire and cools to lit smoke, its shadow on the board, and a dust ring racing out). Every balloon and grid actor gets an outward nudge NudgeLead ahead of the edge, and every balloon the edge reaches pops through IBalloonPopper (BalloonControllerRegistry.TryPopSingle): its pop effects play and its slot is freed, but no hit is published, so nothing scores, no streak moves, no goal advances and no item fires. Other reward balloons and grid actors are spared. A last sweep at the end pops what is left, and then IBreatherGrant.Grant (SpawnClock) starts the breather a big clear earns, even inside the cooldown of an earlier one, and NukeBreatherMessage has NukeSoundRouter play a gust of wind. A level change, a game over or a run reset stops the blast where it is, with no breather.

Leaving the board

Every way off the board (a pop, a board clear, a level change) calls the leave callback, which frees the slot for the next of that type. Only a pop pays, and only a pop rolls for another golden balloon, which is why the payout and the service read the hit, not the leave. The pop's leave runs before its hit is published, so the service guards its roll by the placement, not by whether a golden is on the board. Each placement has its own number, so a callback from an earlier balloon, or from before a run reset, changes nothing. Anything a reward gives belongs on its pop, not on the leave.

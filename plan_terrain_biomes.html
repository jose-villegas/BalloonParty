Terrain & Biomes: sky and ground scenarios

Each level is set either in the sky (today's backdrop) or on the ground (a meadow the board rests on), chosen per level from the level's seed. A level-up Ascent can switch either way, so the run sometimes lands and sometimes climbs back up to the clouds. Actors that need ground under them (bushes first) appear only on ground levels, and the grass reacts to the ball as it flies through. First planned 2026-07-12 as a full seeded biome system; never started. Re-scoped 2026-11-08 to the smallest version that lets bushes ship: one ground, the per-level scenario, the grass reaction. The full biome system is kept below as Later, unscheduled. Status: phase 1 built, awaiting in-editor review.

Why now

Bushes (GridActorType.Bush in LevelPacingConfiguration) were withheld from level generation: over the sky backdrop a bush reads as floating. Owner direction (2026-11-08): the scenario moves between sky and ground in both directions ("once bushes appear that doesn't mean we should stay there, sometimes we go up away to clouds"), and the ground reacts to the bullet, as blades of grass deforming while it moves through.

Verified inventory (2026-11-08)

  • Disturbance field (Shared/Disturbance/, App scope, always running): a camera-sized ARGBHalf ping-pong RT at 8 texels per world unit, published as the global _DisturbanceTex with _FieldBoundsMin/_FieldBoundsSize. R = signed density (0.5 at rest), GB = displacement XY (0.5-biased), A = palette colour tag. The projectile stamps its wake along its path (StampSource.Projectile: radius 0.3, strength 0.8); the diffusion tick (every 0.05 s) decays the displacement and reforms the density, so a wake recovers on its own. Bush leaves (BushLeaf.shader, vertex stage) and puff clouds already read it with one tap.
  • Scenario travel: ScenarioContentRoot moves, the camera never does. Level-up Ascent (LevelAscendCinematic): root goes from +Height to 0 (Height = 15 world units, about 4 s at the authored curve and speed); old content is held at local −Height and slides out the bottom, new content slides in from the top. Game-over restart (GameOverLossCinematic.RiseScenarioAsync): root goes from −Height to 0, old content held at local +Height leaves through the top. LevelAscendStartedMessage fires right before the Ascent.
  • Per-level backdrop state already exists: TimeOfDayCycle (Game/Level/) snaps the light to the level on run start/reset (so the start-level cheat works) and sweeps on LevelUpPhase.Transitioning. The ground controller copies that shape.
  • Seeded levels: LevelGenerationSettings.SeedForLevel(level) hashes the level with a salt, so a retry replays the same mix. PLAN-Coins.md §10 ("luck levels", being written in parallel) rolls parts of a level from the same seed; the scenario is rolled the same way.
  • Baked noise: Assets/Textures/Grid/CloudNoiseTileable.png (made by Tools > BalloonParty > Generate Cloud Noise Texture), sampled through CloudNoise.cginc. The ground reads it for its large patches; the blades are geometry (see How it looks). No per-pixel procedural noise.
  • Lighting: SceneLight.cginc gives the time-of-day colour and the sun direction; the clouds already use both. The floor and the blades read its field-aware forms (SceneLightTintAt, per pixel on the floor and per blade root on the blades), so the local light of pops and items lands on the grass as it does on the sprites.
  • GI capture picks GameObject layers (SceneCaptureService._capturedLayers), not sorting layers. The ground is on the captured Scenario layer, writing colour but not the capture's coverage alpha, so the light buffer gathers the meadow as it gathers the sky. The overlay measures bounce against the meadow's lit average (IGroundSettings.BounceReference × the scene light) on ground levels, as it measures against the sky's clear colour on sky levels: open grass nets to neutral, and a balloon bleeds onto the grass by how it differs from the grass. Measured against the sky blue, every balloon left a dark tinted ring on the grass (owner, 2026-11-09). GI shadows land on the grass everywhere, not just under cloud.
  • Level content: levels are generated (LevelGenerator from LevelGenerationSettings), not authored per range; ranges are debug presets only. The Withheld catalogue flag is being added in separate work; it and the luck levels touch the same settings file, so edits there are coordinated.
  • Stale in the 2026-07 plan, now corrected: "background is just the camera clear" (there are now four backdrop layers); "`LevelParameters` ranges carry per-altitude data" (levels are generated now).

The smallest version

Which levels are ground

  • A seeded per-level scenario, computed by a pure ScenarioSchedule.StretchAt(level) from a small block on LevelGenerationSettings. It answers Sky or Ground, the level's place in its stretch and the stretch's length (a ScenarioStretch); ForLevel is the kind alone.
    • The first ground level is rolled between 11 and 16 (GroundFromLevel, GroundByLevel; revised 2026-11-09, owner: "start to have a chance for it after level 10"). Levels before it are always sky. The roll hashes the scenario's salt, so every run meets the first meadow on the same level: 11 with the shipped salt. Bushes unlock at 11, the window's start, so whichever level it is, they're there.
    • The first meadow is fixed at 4 levels, not rolled: once the run reaches the ground it stays there (owner: "if we enter one we stay there at least n levels").
    • After that the run alternates sky stretches of 2–4 levels and ground stretches of 3–5 levels, each with its own min and max. Bot runs put deep levels at about 30–70 s each, so a meadow is about 2–4 minutes. Two levels flickers once the ~4 s transition each way counts; more than five feels stuck on the harder ground.
    • Each rolled length hashes the level its stretch starts on with the scenario's own salt, so retuning other content doesn't reshuffle the scenarios, and a retry keeps the same one.
    • Pure and cheap: StretchAt walks the stretches from FirstGroundLevel up to the level (a few dozen steps at most), no state, no allocation.
  • A meadow trades its puff clouds for bushes (owner: "initially it will have cluster clouds, but they should quickly disappear half way n levels", then "it gets less clouds and more bushes basically"). A meadow of n levels has puff clouds on its first ⌊n/2⌋ levels (at least one), the last of those at half the count, and the rest clear. Bushes start at half their count under full clouds and grow as the clouds go, so the meadow's clear second half is its bushiest:
Meadow length Puff-cloud count per level Bush count per level
3 half, clear, clear ¾, full, full
4 full, half, clear, clear half, ¾, full, full
5 full, half, clear, clear, clear half, ¾, full, full, full
  • ScenarioStretch.PuffCloudShare and BushShare (= 1 − half the cloud share) hold the rule. LevelGenerator scales the Puff and Bush gates' counts by them after the pieces are picked, so the rest of the level's mix (and its rng stream) is what it would be under full clouds; a clear level drops the Puff gate.
  • The budget the clouds free buys the bushes: once a meadow starts clearing (IsClearingMeadow, cloud share below full), unlocked bushes are always in, even when the threat budget didn't pick them. A puff cloud costs 1 and a bush 2, so the level's total stays about the same while the mix shifts.
  • Sky levels keep both full counts. This is the board's puff-cloud actors, not the cloud backdrop's fade.

Bushes only on ground (built 2026-11-09): a RequiresGround flag on GridActorContentEntry (on IContentPiece, always false for balloons and items), set on the shipped bush. LevelGenerator leaves flagged pieces out of a sky level before any budget is spent, so a bush is never a sky level's pick that comes out empty and the budget goes to the rest. One source of truth: the generator and the ground view both ask ScenarioSchedule. A ground-only piece's featured levels still count from its unlock level, so one unlocked on a sky level first shows at the next meadow, unfeatured; the shipped bush unlocks at 11, the start of the first ground level's window.

  • Triggers (the TimeOfDayCycle shape): snap to the level's scenario on run start and run reset (start-level cheat, restart); animate on the level-up Ascent and on the restart rise when the two levels' scenarios differ. Same scenario on both sides changes nothing.

How it looks

  • The floor: an opaque quad of soft darker and lighter patches (one cloud-noise tap), dark and muted so the saturated balloons stand off it. Tinted by the scene light, so it dims through dusk and night.
  • The blades are geometry: one mesh of about 9,000 tapered blades over the floor, one draw call. The first version drew blades as stretched noise in the floor shader; it read as a texture, not grass, so the owner chose real blades (2026-11-08).
    • Seen from above, a standing blade is a short stroke leaning one way. Blades sway in gust waves that roll across the meadow, and tips turn drier on the floor's lighter patches.
  • The grass reacts to the ball through the existing disturbance field, with no new stamp:
    • Each blade reads the grass wake (GrassWakeBuffer) once, at its root, in the vertex stage. The wake is a small field-sized buffer that keeps only what the disturbance field gained each frame and lets it go in about a third of a second: the shared field holds and spreads a push for seconds, which felt sluggish on grass (owner, 2026-11-09).
    • The displacement (GB) turns the blade toward the push and lays it down: from above it shows more of its length, curving from its root, so where the ball passed the blades part and fan out.
    • The density change (R) lays them flatter still, and laid blades catch the sun toward their tips, so the path reads as a combed stripe.
    • Recovery is the wake's own (BladeRecoverySeconds), not the field's. Pops, bombs and lasers stamp the same field, so the grass answers them too.
    • The field covers the camera's rect, and the blade tile reaches past it: blades outside the field rest.
    • The field is 8 texels per unit, so the wake is soft, about a ball-width wide. A crisper wake would mean raising TexelsPerUnit for every consumer, which is a tuning decision, not planned.
  • The meadow travels with the board: the bushes are planted on it, so the floor and the blades scroll with ScenarioContentRoot one-to-one (_GroundScroll; the blades wrap across their tile). On the Ascent and the restart rise the ground and its bushes move in together; a still ground made the bushes look like they slid in. The disturbance field stays camera-fixed, where the shots stamp it.
  • Clouds on ground levels: the cloud bodies fade out and their drop shadows stay, so cloud shadows drift over the grass, as if the clouds were overhead and out of frame. If the bodies stayed, the ground would read as far below and the bushes would float again. Climbing back to the sky brings the bodies back.
  • The transition rides the scenario root, in either direction. The edge between the two scenarios is a noise-warped line with a white mist fringe (the cloud colour), placed in the root's space halfway between old and new content: local y = −Height/2 on the Ascent, +Height/2 on the restart rise. The old scenario is drawn on the old content's side, the new one on the new side, so one rule covers sky→ground, ground→sky, and both restart directions. The ground and the bushes slide in or out together; the edge needs no timing of its own. The switch is armed when the transition starts and takes effect when the root first moves, so nothing pops while the root waits at 0.
  • Dirt and colour: the floor is muted mid-tone greens, light enough for the time of day to read across it and dark enough for the balloons to stand off it. A few large bare-dirt clusters (IGroundSettings dirt size, threshold and softness) fade into the grass: the floor blends to soil over a wide noise band with a frayed outline, and the blades dry, shorten and drop out across the same band (owner, 2026-11-08: patches blend; fewer, bigger clusters).
  • Shadows on the ground: the sprite and paint drop shadows that fade where there is no cloud or smoke under them (_CLOUD_FADE_ON) stay on ground levels, since they land on the grass (GroundCoverageAt in SpriteLightDriven and PaintBlob).
  • Bush shadow: the bush's drop-shadow distance is checked against the ground in the editor; a long offset shadow reads as height.

Performance (mobile, 120 Hz = 8.33 ms frame)

  • Bake once: no new RT, no per-level bake, no new field. The only baked input is the existing noise texture; the disturbance field already runs every frame for the clouds and bushes.
  • Ground cost: the floor is 3 taps per pixel (patches, dirt, dirt fray). The grass wake is one blit and one copy a frame on a field-sized target (about 45×80). The blades add about 45k vertices (12 bytes each, 16-bit indices) in one draw, each with 3 vertex taps (disturbance, patch noise, dirt) plus the light field, and a flat-colour fragment over about a third of the screen. No alpha test or blending anywhere (a tile-GPU cost). Measure the vertex cost on a low-end phone at the review gate; BladeSpacing trades density for vertices with the inverse square.
  • Sky levels: the ground renderer is disabled. Cost: zero.
  • Ground levels: the ground covers every pixel, so the sky-scatter quad is disabled (SkyScatterService gains a visibility toggle). That swaps the 10-ring sky loop for the 3-tap floor plus the blades; measure it at the review gate.
  • During a transition (about 4 s, gameplay paused): sky and ground both draw.
  • CPU: no per-frame work at rest and no allocations; while a transition runs, one transform read and one material float per frame. ScenarioSchedule.ForLevel runs once per level.
  • Not checkable by dotnet build: the shader needs an in-editor look and a Frame Debugger check of the draw order.

What it reuses

DisturbanceFieldService's _DisturbanceTex and the projectile's existing wake stamps for the grass reaction; QuadRendererBuilder for the quad; CloudNoiseTileable.png + CloudNoise.cginc for the patches; SceneLight.cginc for the tint and sun direction; BackgroundCloud's existing shadow tap (only a global ground-coverage fade on the bodies is new); SeedForLevel's hash for the scenario roll; the TimeOfDayCycle snap/transition shape; ScenarioContentRoot and the two existing transition beats. No changes to the Ascent or restart code.

Shape (MVC)

  • ScenarioKind (Sky, Ground), ScenarioSchedule and ScenarioScheduleSettings (pure, Configuration/Level/), the settings block on LevelGenerationSettings, read through ILevelPacingConfiguration.ScenarioForLevel; EditMode-tested. LevelSeedHash is the hash shared with SeedForLevel.
  • GroundSettings / IGroundSettings (Configuration/Effects/): ground material, sorting order (−15, between sky −20 and clouds −10), overscan.
  • ActiveScenario / IActiveScenario (Scenario/): the model, the scenario shown now.
  • ScenarioController (plain C#, Game/Level/, IStartable/IRunResettable): asks the schedule for the current level and writes the model.
  • GroundView (thin MonoBehaviour, Scenario/View/, parented under the Game scope so the additive Launcher→Game load doesn't destroy it): owns the quad, pushes the _GroundCoverage global the cloud shader reads (Include/Ground.cginc), and hides the sky through ISkyBackdrop. The Launch begin-screen always shows sky.
  • Ground.shader (Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/).

Phases

  1. Ground at rest, with the grass reaction: ScenarioSchedule + tests (determinism, the first ground level always ground, stretch bounds, no flips before it), GroundSettings, controller snap on run start/reset, GroundView, the meadow shader reading the disturbance field, the sky toggle, the cloud-body fade. Seen in the editor with the start-level cheat at 16, firing balls through the grass. Review: architect, test-everything, reviewer, optimizer.
  2. Transitions both ways: the edge in root space on the Ascent and the restart rise, the mist fringe, arm-then-commit. Seen by playing across a scenario flip and by losing on a ground level. Seams to change (architect, phase 1 review):
    • The restart goes through IRunResettable.ResetRun, which runs before the root drops to −Height and can't tell a cinematic restart (rise) from a skip-path one (cut). Move the reset onto RunResetMessage: BoardReset == false arms a rise edge at +Height/2, BoardReset == true and run start snap.
    • ActiveScenario keeps Kind as the committed scenario and gains the pending transition (target, edge side); GroundView commits when the root first moves. It already feeds the root offset to the meadow (phase 1), so the grass travels with the board.
    • Phase 1 visible gap until then: the switch is a cut, so on the Ascent the old board floats out over the new backdrop, and on a cinematic restart a ground board sits on the sky for the spawn wait and the rise.
  3. Bushes on ground only: RequiresGround on the catalogue and in LevelGenerator (with a test that no sky level contains a ground-only actor; built 2026-11-09, with the Configuration README). Bushes un-withheld 2026-11-09. Still to do: tune the bush shadow on grass, run a playtest-bot comparison. Debug presets (level < 1) skip LevelGenerator and are always sky, so a preset that lists bushes would float them; the preset validator should warn.

Decisions (signed off 2026-11-08)

  1. First ground level 16, with the first bushes. Revised 2026-11-09: rolled between 11 and 16, bushes unlocking at 11 and no longer withheld.
  2. Stretches of 2–4 levels per scenario. Revised 2026-11-09: ground stretches of 3–5 levels with the first fixed at 4, sky stretches of 2–4, and a meadow clears its puff clouds halfway (see "Which levels are ground").
  3. Cloud bodies fade out on ground; their shadows stay.

In-editor checks for phase 1

  • The meadow and the grass reaction at game zoom, by day and night (start-level cheat 11).
  • Frame Debugger: the ground draws before the clouds and the smoke, and the sky quad is gone on ground levels.
  • The GI: on ground levels the capture sees the meadow in place of the sky, so check the balloons' glow by day, the GI shadows on the grass, and that sky levels are unchanged.
  • Launcher → Game in play mode: the Ground object lives under the Game scope.
  • The blades on a phone: their width and density at game zoom, and the vertex cost on a low-end device (BladeSpacing 0.085, about 45k vertices on 9:16), and a 4:3 tablet and a 21:9 phone show blades to every edge with no wrap pops.
  • A level-up within a ground stretch (16 → 17, inside the fixed first meadow): the meadow and the bushes travel in together, with nothing sliding over still grass.

Follow-ups from the phase 1 optimizer review

  • Shader prewarm: the ground's first draw lands on the first ground level's Ascent; add BalloonParty/Scenario/Ground and GrassBlades to the shader-variant capture (PLAN-PerformanceRecovery F4), with both blend states once phase 2 switches them.
  • Only if an on-device profile shows ground levels vertex-bound: a per-blade pre-pass. Each of a blade's five vertices repeats the same per-blade work (the wrap, both taps, the bend); a small blit into one texel per blade cell would do it once, and the vertex stage would only evaluate the curve. Estimated 0.15–0.3 ms on a Mali-G52-class GPU. A blit, not a compute buffer: many Mali GLES drivers can't read storage buffers in the vertex stage.
  • Optional, once the phase 2 edge allows it: draw the cloud shadow inside the ground shader and turn the cloud pass off on ground levels (one fewer full-screen pass, five taps).

Later: the full biome system (unscheduled)

The 2026-07-12 design, kept for when more biomes earn it. Its inventory is superseded by the one above. In short:

  • Seeded terrain: seed + level → identical map. Two baked-noise fields (elevation, moisture) sampled at hex centres classify each slot into a biome (water, sand, grass, dirt, stone, lava) through a BiomeProfile; ITerrainQuery.GetBiome(slot) / GetBiomeAtWorld(pos) is the seam for placement gating (bushes only on grass) and biome-tinted specks.
  • Bake once per level into a ~512-wide RT: noise-warped hex distances blend the nearest biomes (height blending recommended over a plain cross-fade), plus a data map with biome id and a signed distance to the boundary (shorelines, wet sand, lava crust, edge AO as one-tap lookups).
  • Runtime: one quad sampling the composite, a world-space detail texture and _DisturbanceTex for reactions: grass shears along the field's displacement, water gets dual scrolling noise plus rings from the density gradient, sand darkens, lava pulses emissive (HDR-ready per PLAN-HDRColorPipeline.md), stone stays inert. Budget ≤ 4 extra taps in the common path; no alpha-tested grass (a tile-GPU cost).
  • Anti-repetition, cheapest first: per-cell hash flips, a macro variation tap, a channel-packed detail atlas, scatter stamps; hex-tiling only where those fail.
  • Composition rules: one sun (SceneLight direction), one wind (shared with the bush wind), no second lighting model; the ground feeds the GI through the same capture as the sky.

The meadow above is the first biome of that system. When it grows, ScenarioKind gains more grounds (the schedule rolls which one a ground stretch uses), the meadow shader and its grass reaction become the grass biome's runtime look, and the sky/ground edge becomes the first boundary.

Meadow Mushrooms

Meadow mushrooms: bumpers that earn a lance

On meadow levels the board grows mushrooms: permanent bumpers the ball bounces off, pinball style. A ball that hits three of them in one flight, without touching a wall in between, becomes the Snipe lance. To make room, the meadow loses its Tough and Tougher balloons, and its unbreakables form only the snake. Talked through with the owner on 2026-11-18 and banked; started on 2026-11-19, when he settled the open points below and decisions A to F. Status: phase 1 (the pure rules) is built, with the kick amended to look one mushroom ahead (decision H, taken for the owner and waiting for him to confirm). Phase 2 (generation) is built: meadow levels have no toughs, allow only the snake and carry a mushroom count. Phase 3 (the live bounce) is built headless: the ball bounces off mushroom models, and the spawner knows how to place a chain. Phase 4 (the reward) is built: a chain pays the Snipe lance, with its sounds and its counters, and its review's fixes are in (one extra charge a flight, whatever walls it meets; the step ends at the bounce that pays). Phase 5 (the mirror and the aim line) is built: the shot solver and the aim line bounce off the mushrooms as the ball does, held by mirror tests. Phase 6 (the view and the art) is built: mushrooms now appear on meadow levels, drawn by a shader with no painted art, and the cap leans away from a hit and wobbles back. The owner rejected its first look, a toadstool seen from the side, and it was redrawn as the board is seen, from above: a cap over the circle the ball bounces off, its stem showing only when it is knocked (Phase 6, the top view). They have been seen in the Editor, in staged scenes and a filmed chain, and not yet played by a person or run on a device. One choice made in phase 3 is open for the owner: what a mushroom bounce does to a shot's run of wall bounces (below, after decision H). Two in phase 6 are his to look at: the cap's colour and the shadow (see Phase 6 as built and Phase 6, the top view). Since then the owner chose walls with a kick (decision I): 2 to 4 walls of caps a level, dealt from a baked catalogue, the kick bending only toward another wall and one hit counting per wall. It is built and replaces the planned chain; where the sections on phases 1 to 5 and on the spacing speak of the planner and its chain, they are the record of what was built then (Walls with a kick as built). Last, he asked that a lance cut the caps instead of bouncing off them, which replaces decisions A and B (decision J). Both halves are built: the rule (The lance cuts caps: the rule as built) and what a cut looks like (The lance cuts caps: the view).

Decision H, taken for the owner: the kick looks one mushroom ahead

Decided by Claude on 2026-11-19 on the owner's behalf while he was away. He has not seen it; it is his to confirm or change. Decision I keeps the lookahead and restates it for walls: the ball is landed for the nearest cap of another wall, where this decision landed it for the next mushroom of a planned chain.

  • What he chose (the bounce, in the rules above), as it was described to him: "a real bounce, but the mushroom bends the outgoing direction toward the next mushroom when the ball leaves within a cone of it. Aim still matters for the first hit; the chain then mostly carries itself."
  • What phase 1 found. The kick as first designed landed every kicked ball at one fixed offset beside the next mushroom's centre. A ball landing at a fixed offset turns by a fixed angle there (151 degrees at an offset of 0.25), whatever the layout. So the planner's legs and the kick disagreed: the ball mostly went A, B, A instead of along the chain, and the chain did not carry itself.
  • What was decided. The kick looks one mushroom ahead. It lands the ball on the next mushroom at the point from which a true reflection sends it on toward the mushroom after that, so each bounce sets up the next. With three or more mushrooms the ball moves on; with two it comes back, which is how two mushrooms give three hits. Where it may land is kept between two offsets, never the centre and never a thin graze. The planner plans only the turns those two offsets can make, and accepts a layout only when the kick flies its chain.
  • Why this and not a tuned fixed offset. No one offset fits a board: the turn a chain needs differs at every mushroom. Looking ahead is the smallest change that makes the sentence he chose true.

Open for the owner: what was decided for him in the bank

Decided on 2026-11-19 while building decision K. He has not seen these; each is his to keep or change.

  • What the bank is worth, measured (The bank, as built, the tables). With the one shield a ball starts with, a flight spends one charge at the most, whatever it banked: it lives through one wall. A chain's lance banks 1.3 charges and spends one in one flight in nine; a Snipe aimed through the most caps banks 4 and spends one in a little over half its flights. The board such a flight clears grows by under two parts in a hundred. With four shields (the card that adds one, three times) it is more: the same Snipe clears half the board where it cleared 39 parts in a hundred.
  • On a board with no unbreakable the bank does nothing at all. A lance there is a lance to its last wall anyway. So on the meadow the bank is a way through the snakes: it is the unbreakables it is spent on.
  • Nothing shows a charge. The game has never drawn how many charges a ball holds, and still does not. What was built is the sound and what a view needs to show it (MushroomCutMessage.Charges); the view's part is not built.
  • How it sounds. Each cut's stroke is a whole tone higher than the flight's cut before it, so a lance through a wall of three is heard climbing. The Snipe's cue rings once a flight, on its first charge, a whole tone above the arming's, which is what a banked charge rang before the bank was dropped. For a chain's lance that is nearly always a moment after the arming's own cue: the kick aims the arming bounce at a cap.
  • A cruise's pierce becomes a Snipe's lance once it has cut a cap. A ball that earned its pierce by cruising between empty walls, with no Snipe behind it, cuts caps as any piercing ball does (decision J), so under "any lance" its cuts bank too. What happens then: the cruise's pierce is spent at the first wall the ball reaches after plowing an unbreakable (or another balloon it cannot pop outright), as it always was; with a charge banked the ball stays piercing there, its cruise ends, and the Snipe arms it afresh, with the Snipe's speed. From that wall on it is a Snipe's lance until its shields run out, re-armed by a charge at each later wall that would end it. Without a plowed unbreakable no charge is spent and nothing changes for it. It is what a Snipe picked up during a cruise's pierce has always done, now earned by a cut. The alternative, not built: bank only for a pierce the Snipe armed (a pickup, the card, a chain, a charge), which the flight can tell by the ball piercing without cruising; a cruise's pierce would still cut caps and bank nothing. It is pinned by a test as it stands (MushroomBankTests), so a change is a decision and not an accident.
  • The charge is a plain one, whatever the lance: a rainbow lance's cuts bank plain charges.
  • A cut nothing flew banks nothing: the stage's cut.
  • mushroom_charges_banked runs level with mushroom_caps_cut in play, since every cut banks. It is kept apart for the day a limit is tried. Nothing counts the charges that are spent, which is the number that would say whether the bank matters in play.
  • The solver banks and gives a charge no value. For a Snipe's lance it never spends one either, since its lance is never spent (an approximation older than the mushrooms).

Open for the owner: what was decided for him in the lance's cut

Decided on 2026-11-19 while building decision J, each the nearest thing to what he chose. He has not seen these; each is his to keep or change. The first four were decided by the coordinator, the rest while building.

  • The bounce that arms is still a bounce. The ball becomes a lance on its third hit and leaves that cap by the kick or the reflection, as before, so the cap that armed it is not cut by it. If the lance comes back to that cap off a wall, it cuts it then.
  • A lance spent at a wall is an ordinary ball again, and bounces off the mushrooms again from that wall on. A new chain can arm it again (Open for the owner: a lance spent at a wall can be armed again).
  • A cap regrows even if its whole wall was cut. Each cap counts its own beats from its own cut.
  • A cut counts nothing and breaks nothing. The chain a lance carries is as it was when the ball was armed, until a wall clears it; a cut cap is not a hit.
  • Any piercing ball cuts, a cruise's pierce included. The game has one state for a ball that pierces, whatever armed it, and the rule reads that state. A ball that earned its pierce by cruising between empty walls cuts caps as a Snipe's lance does. Telling the two apart would need the rule to know where a pierce came from, which nothing in a flight says today.
  • Which beat is the first. The first beat seen to fall after the frame of the cut. So a cap is gone for more than four beats and at most five: 6.8 to 8.5 seconds with the beat at 1.7, and always back on a beat. Counting five whole beat lengths from the cut instead would bring caps back off the beat.
  • Only a ball in flight holds a cap back. A cap that is due waits a beat for each beat a ball's centre is inside the circle it will have (the cap's radius and the ball's). Balloons do not hold it back: the slot was the mushroom's all along, so none stands there.
  • A cut has its own sound, a stand-in: the stroke of the heart's strikethrough (Strikethrough), at 0.8 of its volume, at the cap. The bank holds nothing nearer to a blade.
  • No heads-up names a cut, and none named the chain: there was no text to change.
  • The solver does not model the regrow. A flight is simulated on the board as it stands: a cap cut before the aim stays cut for the whole simulated flight, and one the simulated lance cuts stays cut to its end. A flight is seconds long and a regrow is 7 or 8 away, so the two seldom meet.

Open for the owner: what was decided for him in the blasts

Decided on 2026-11-19 while building Blasts take caps off. He has not seen these; each is his to keep or change.

  • "Next to it" was read as the bomb's own rule for unbreakables. A bomb breaks an unbreakable balloon in the six slots round its own, 0.75 world units away. (A rainbow bomb breaks whatever has its centre within its kill radius of 1.25; the next slots out are 1.30 away, so on the board those are the same six.) Caps go by that rule and by no number of the mushrooms' own, so if the bomb's rule for unbreakables is ever changed, the caps follow it.
  • How often that is. Over the 127 boards of the catalogue, a bomb in each slot that holds no mushroom (7554 of them, every slot counted alike): it takes no cap 56 times in 100, one cap 32, two 11, and never more, since walls stand 1.9 apart. From the third row from the top to the seventh, two bombs in three take a cap; in the top row one in three, and in the two lowest none.
  • The sway was faint as first shipped, and was widened. At the blast's own reach, the kill radius and the cap's own radius, 1.55, the mushrooms that sway are the second ring only, 1.30 or 1.50 from the bomb, at strengths 0.31 and 0.06 on a scale where 1 is the distance of the slots next to the bomb. The setting for widening it is the sway's share of the kill radius (IMushroomSettings.Blast). The view's films settled it at 2.4, a reach of 3.3: the second ring sways at 0.78 and 0.71, the third (1.98, 2.25) at 0.52 and 0.41, the fourth (2.60 to 3.00) at 0.27 down to 0.12 (Blasts take caps off: the view says why).
  • A sway changes nothing in the rule. The cap is whole: the ball bounces off it and the chain counts it as before. Only whole caps sway.
  • A nuke takes every cap, each as the blast's edge reaches it: near mushrooms first, over the blast's one second, each blown away from the nuke. A blast stopped short (a level change, a game over, a reset) has taken only the caps it reached.
  • A blown cap grows back as a cut one does, on the board's fifth beat after it went. So after a nuke the caps pop back together, on one beat or two that follow each other, 6.8 to 8.5 seconds later, onto the board the nuke emptied.
  • A blast is no hit of anyone's chain and breaks none. A ball in flight keeps the count it has and finds the caps gone. Nothing is paid for a blown cap.
  • A cap that was already cut is left as it is: not announced again, its wait not started again, no sway.
  • A blown cap has no sound of its own. It is heard in the blast. The cut's stroke is a lance's alone: a bomb taking two caps would otherwise play two strokes on top of its own bang.
  • Blown caps have their own counter, mushroom_caps_blown, so mushroom_caps_cut goes on telling how much the chain's reward is used.
  • The solver takes a bomb's caps off and knows nothing of a nuke's or of the sway (Blasts take caps off: the rule as built, the mirror).

  • A wall of one cap is used only where a board cannot be filled without it. With 4 to 7 caps that is: two walls of 2 or 3 caps each; three walls of 2, 2 and 2 or of 3, 2 and 2; four walls of 2, 2, 2 and 1. So a level of four walls always has 7 caps and one of them stands alone.
  • The bushes are left 3 slots beside two walls and 2 beside three or four, of the 9 they share with the caps. The level generator does not know which layout a level will be dealt, so it takes the most caps the level's walls may hold (6, 7, 7). Before the walls the bushes had 7 down to 5 slots.
  • A bush may stand between two walls, but not on a slot next to a cap. The ball flies over bushes (they have no collider), so a bush in a lane changes no flight; next to a cap it would read as one more piece of the wall.
  • The band a layout is kept for: 7 to 16 in 100 aim angles arm the lance, the widest run of them spans 2 degrees or more, and at least 20 angles arm. A layout is kept only when it is inside the band with room to spare (8 arming angles at each end and 2 angles of window over its floor, twice what a layout is allowed to drift between runtimes), so another device's rounding does not find it outside.
  • Four walls arm more often than he was told: 13.2 in 100 angles on average, where two walls arm on 9.7 and three on 11.6. A lower top to the band would bring it down and thin the choice of boards of four.
  • The kick's smallest offset is 0.15, down from 0.2. Two walls trading the ball need the cone wider than twice the arcsine of the smallest offset: 23 degrees at 0.2, which a cone of 20 is not, and 17 at 0.15. Measured both ways the boards arm alike.
  • A flight that arms goes on to cut a cap (every arming flight of the catalogue, flown by the solver on an empty board: 1.2 to 1.3 caps a flight). The bounce that arms is aimed back at the wall the ball came from, which used to be the hit that banked a charge and is now the first cap the lance cuts (decision J).
  • No mirrored layouts. The slots of odd rows stand half a slot to the side of the even rows', so a board mirrored left to right does not land on slots. There are 18, 64 and 45 layouts of 2, 3 and 4 walls instead (Open for the owner: the cannon's room).
  • When the rows the balloon spawner keeps leave no layout standing, the level gets one wall fewer, down to two, then none with a warning. With the shipped two rows kept, or three, every layout fits; with four rows kept 6, 20 and 5 of the layouts of 2, 3 and 4 walls do, and with five one of two and one of three.
  • The walls are placed first, and a cloud does not stand next to a cap. The clouds and the bushes are placed with random numbers a retry does not replay, so the walls go before both: a retry stands them where they stood. What is placed after the walls keeps off the slots next to a cap, which used to hold for the bushes alone and now holds for the clouds too.

Open for the owner: a mushroom bounce and the cruise pierce

Built one way in phase 3, where the plan was silent. He has not seen it; it is his to keep or change.

  • As built. A mushroom bounce zeroes an unarmed shot's count of wall bounces, as a balloon contact does. A wall bounce counted earlier in the same step (a wall, then a mushroom on the way back off it) is discarded with the rest. So a shot that alternates walls and mushrooms never earns the cruise pierce: only a run of walls with nothing between them does.
  • The alternative. A mushroom bounce leaves the wall-bounce count alone, so a shot bouncing between walls and mushrooms still builds toward the cruise pierce, and the meadow gets a second route to a piercing ball beside the chain.
  • What does not depend on it. The chain itself (a wall clears it either way), an armed shot's run, and the taps a shot has earned.
  • Since decision J a shot that does earn the cruise pierce is a piercing ball like any lance, so it cuts the caps in its way (Open for the owner: what was decided for him in the lance's cut).

Open for the owner: a lance spent at a wall can be armed again

Found by the review of phase 5. He has not seen it; it is his to keep or change.

  • As built. The chain rule does not cap how often a flight is armed. A lance bounces off no mushroom (decision J), so its chain never completes, but a lance spent at a wall is an ordinary ball again, the wall has cleared its chain, and a new chain arms it again (at most 2 armings with the single starting shield; unreachable on the meadow today because a lance only discharges at a wall after piercing a tough).

Design

Contact is worked out, not left to physics

The live ball only collides with balloons: ProjectileView.OnTriggerEnter2D looks for a BalloonView and calls ProjectileHitResolver. Bushes have no collider, and the Deflector, Absorber and Gatekeeper actors are not reachable in play. So a mushroom is not a new collider. There are at most 7 permanent circles, and ProjectileMotionResolver.Step tests the step's segment against them before the walls, then carries the rest of the step along the new direction, as Deflect does for a deflecting balloon.

  • No tunnelling, and no physics layer, so no ProjectSettings change.
  • The shot solver works event to event, so it mirrors the bounce exactly.
  • At most 7 circle tests a fixed step, nothing allocated.

The forgiving kick

As built in phase 1, after decision H, and restated for walls in decision I. c is the contact point and r the true reflection. An offset is where a ball lands on a cap: its impact parameter as a share of the radius it bounces at, 0 dead centre and 1 a graze. A ball landing at an offset turns by 180 degrees less twice the arcsine of the offset.

  • The target. The candidates are the caps of other walls whose bearing from c lies within the cone (Kick.ConeDegrees, 20) of r, with a corridor clear of other caps (balloons are ignored: they pop or are ricocheted off on the way). The one at the smallest angle wins. With no candidate the ball leaves along r. A cap of the wall just hit is never a candidate: inside one wall every bounce is a mirror reflection.
  • The landing point looks one cap ahead. The ball is aimed at the point of the target from which a true reflection points at a follower's centre. So the ball leaves the target along a reflection that already points at the follower, the target's own kick finds the follower dead ahead, and the step repeats: the rally carries itself.
  • The follower is the nearest cap to the target that is not of the target's own wall, the lower id when two are as near; where a cap stands in the list handed to the rule does not matter. Two walls that face each other are each other's nearest, so the ball is sent back across the gap, which is how two walls give three hits. One fits when the offset it needs is within the kick's two, the reflection's way to it is clear and the kicked line has a clear corridor; the next nearest is tried when it does not. A contact on a cap that is not in the list is not kicked. (Before decision I the follower was the next id along a planned chain. The target was any mushroom in the cone even then, which is why most chains were already flown out of the planned order.)
  • A kicked line clearly leaves its own cap. Within 0.06 degrees of the tangent it counts as blocked: a rounding error could read such a line as entering the cap again. So does a line that starts inside another cap's circle: the caps of one wall overlap, and a ball in the notch between two of them bounces off the second at once.
  • Never the centre, never a thin graze. The offset is kept between Kick.MinOffset and Kick.MaxOffset (0.15 and 0.9). A dead-centre hit reflects straight back, and two caps would trade the ball forever with no wall to stop it; a graze too thin is missed on a small error. When no follower fits inside them, the ball is landed for the nearest follower as near as they allow.
  • The last kick. The bounce that brings the count to the chain target aims at the cap the ball would hit next (the hit that banked a charge until decision J; now the first cap the lance cuts), which is not kicked on. It lands at the smallest offset: a reflection set up to point at a centre, with no kick left to correct it, would be the dead-centre hit the rule exists to prevent.
  • It stops. The kick applies until the count passes the chain target. After that bounces are true, and their spread ends any cycle. Since decision J the ball is a lance from the target on and bounces off nothing, so there is no bounce past it.
  • And the flight is bounded whatever happens. The chain rule counts every bounce off a mushroom since the last wall, the ones that did not count included, and past MaxBouncesPerStretch (12) the mushrooms let the ball through until it meets a wall, which spends a shield. The kick cannot hold that by itself: a ricochet off an unbreakable can send the ball back to the mushroom it just left, a hit that does not count and is kicked again, and true reflections between mushrooms standing too close can trap a ball. A chain is paid in full by its fourth bounce, so the cap costs no reward.
  • The cone the composition needs. Where the follower fits, the reflection points at its centre, so the next kick takes hold inside any cone. Coming straight back, the offset is clamped at the smallest, which leaves the reflection up to twice its arcsine off the way back: 17 degrees at 0.15. Two walls therefore want the cone wider than that, and 20 leaves 3 degrees to spare (MushroomSettings.Validate says so when it is not). The cone is otherwise the dial for how much skill a rally asks: the owner narrowed it from 30 to 20 in decision I.

Placement

As built for decision I (Walls with a kick as built). Phase 1 planned a chain through single mushrooms with a seeded search (Phase 1 as built is the record of it); that planner is gone.

  • A layout is dealt, not planned. The catalogue holds 18, 64 and 45 layouts of 2, 3 and 4 walls. The level's seed picks where in the list to start, and the first layout from there whose slots are all free is the level's.
  • What a layout keeps to by its shape: 2 to 4 walls; a wall is 1 to 3 caps in neighbouring slots, in a line along a row or along a diagonal of the lattice; 4 to 7 caps in all, a wall of one cap only where the board cannot be filled without it; the walls 1.9 or more apart, nearest cap to nearest cap; no cap in an edge column, the top row or the bottom two rows; and the cannon keeps its room (no cap nearer than 3.25 to its pivot, and a cap met within 4 of the muzzle on no more than a fifth of the aim angles).
  • What a layout is kept for: flown from every angle of the aim grid on an empty board, with the shield a ball starts with, 7 to 16 in 100 angles arm the lance, the widest unbroken run of them spans 2 degrees or more, and at least 20 angles arm. Shapes are not judged: walls that send the ball to each other are the ones that measure inside the band.
  • The bake is done ahead of time by an editor tool and committed as a generated file; a test flies every entry again and another holds the numbers the bake depends on against what ships.
  • Inputs at the deal: the board's empty slots, the count of walls, the level's seed, and the rows the balloon spawner keeps. The walls are placed before anything else, so the seed alone picks the layout.
  • When no layout of the count fits: one wall fewer, down to 2; then none and a warning.
  • Caps are placed before the clouds and the bushes, which keep off the slots next to a cap.
  • A cap's place in its layout is the id its bumper carries, and its wall rides with it.

The mirror and the aim line

Every rule a shot meets is mirrored in Solver/ and held by mirror tests. A mushroom is a new contact kind there, and the chain state rides in the solver's flight state. PredictionTraceCalculator already reflects off deflector circles and ignores them while piercing; it takes the same bounce rule (decisions A and C). Built in phase 5: see Phase 5 as built.

Art and the cap's reaction

  • The body is drawn in a shader from the quad's own position, as the nuke, the disco ball and the golden balloon are: the cap and the stem as distance fields, lit through Include/SceneLight.cginc. No painted sprite, one renderer. dotnet build does not compile shaders, so each change needs a look in the Editor.
  • It is seen from above, as the board is (the owner, on the first look: Phase 6, the top view). The cap is a disc over the circle the ball bounces off; the stem stands under it and shows only when a hit knocks the cap aside.
  • The shadow was planned as a baked shadow child, its blurred silhouette written by a small script under Tools/art/. As built the shader draws it (Phase 6 as built says why), and there is no script and no texture.
  • The reaction: MushroomView keeps one DampedSpring2D (the shield view already uses it). A bounce on its own slot adds an impulse along the ball's incoming direction, scaled by its speed; the spring is underdamped, so the cap leans away and wobbles back along that same line. Each frame it writes the lean to the material. As first built, for the side view, the shader bent the stem by the square of the height and carried the cap the whole way. Seen from above it moves the cap off its foot and tips it into the lean, which uncovers the stem on the side the ball came from: that, and the wobble back, are what read as elastic.
  • Nothing ticks at rest: the view switches itself off once the spring is still and on again at an impulse. No tweens, and the spring is reset when the view goes back to the pool.
  • A lance cuts the cap (the owner, 2026-11-19): the same shader on the same quad draws the cap in two pieces that part along the lance's line, fall and fade, then the bare stem, then the cap popping back. See The lance cuts caps: the view.

Phase 1 as built

What the code settled where the design left room. The READMEs of Shared/, Slots/ and Configuration/ describe the types.

Since replaced in part. The layout planner, its planned chain, the order by ids and the figures measured on planned layouts below are the record of phase 1. Decision I replaced them with walls dealt from a catalogue (Walls with a kick as built); the chain rule, the reflection, the kick's geometry and the cap on bounces stand, the count and the kick's follower restated for walls. One thing said here was wrong: that a chain was flown in the planned order. The kick bent toward whichever mushroom lay nearest the reflection, and most arming flights took the mushrooms in another order.

  • A bumper's radius includes the ball's. BumperCircle.Radius is how far from the centre the ball's centre is at the touch, so every rule works on the path of the ball's centre and a corridor is clear of a mushroom when that path stays farther than the radius from it. Phase 3's board adds the ball's radius when it builds the circles.
  • A bumper's id is its place in the chain. The kick finds a follower by the ids, and the planner returns the slots in chain order: the board numbers each mushroom's circle by its place in that list. The order of the list handed to the rule does not matter. In the editor the kick asserts that the ids are unique and that no two contact circles overlap.
  • The bounce rule is asked after the bounce is counted. OnBounce first, then Resolve with that state. The kick applies while the count is at most the chain target: the bounce that reaches the target is the last kick, toward the hit that banks, and from the next one on bounces are mirror reflections.
  • A stretch has at most 12 bounces. MushroomChainState.Bounces counts every bounce since the last wall, and past MaxBouncesPerStretch OnBounce answers PassThrough: the mushrooms let the ball through until it meets a wall. Measured over the 87,000 flights of the forgiveness sweep: the last reward of any flight comes on its fourth bounce; with no cap the longest flight takes 16 bounces; the cap of 12 ends 2 of the 87,000, both long after they were paid.
  • KickAimOffset is gone. In its place the settings carry Kick (BumperKickTuning): ConeDegrees (30), MinOffset (0.2) and MaxOffset (0.9). The planner's own turn range is gone too: the turns a chain may make are the ones those two offsets give.
  • A wall does not give banks back. A second chain in one flight arms again, but the flight has one extra charge in all.
  • The walls are used for one thing: no mushroom nearer a wall than its own radius plus the ball's diameter, so a ball can always pass between the two.
  • The landing point is bracketed, not iterated to. It lies on the arc of the circle both ends see, where the surface normal turns steadily past the bisector of the two ways out, so halving that arc finds it whenever there is one. An answer short of a true reflection (an end a hair off the circle) is refused, never returned.
  • The planner's guards. A corridor keeps a margin of 0.05 world units beyond touching; a chain must fly from the first shots 0.6 of an aim step either side of the ideal one, both inside the aim arc; no two mushrooms stand nearer than a ball needs to pass; and every acceptance is held a margin inside its limit. Without the first two the planner accepted chains that cleared a mushroom by a rounding error, flown by one shot and lost by the next.
  • What the margins do not do. They make an accepted chain fly on every device. They cannot make two devices always accept the same slot: where a number falls within a rounding error of a margin's own edge, one accepts and the other moves on, and the two lay out different boards for the seed, each of which flies. Three seeds' layouts are pinned in the tests, so a runtime that differs shows up there.
  • A kicked line that is blocked falls back before it gives up: to the next follower that fits, then to the first follower as near as the offsets allow, then to the smallest offset on either side. Only then does the ball leave along the true reflection.
  • The search budget covers the whole call, fallbacks included. A count that fails hands what is left of its even share to the next. On the open board a layout takes 5 to 19 iterations on average, and the most over 500 seeds was 290 of the 2000 (more since the spacing: Spacing as built).
  • The settings validate themselves. MushroomSettings.Validate reports a cap on bounces under what a chain takes, crossed offsets, a cone too narrow to send the ball back, a shortest leg under two mushrooms across and the like, through the Level Pacing asset's own validation.
  • First guesses, to be tuned: the cone (30 degrees), a mushroom's radius (0.3), legs of 1.2 to 3.2 world units (1.9 to 3.2 since the spacing), the budget (2000), two bottom rows and one top row kept clear, and a shared limit of 9 slots.

Phase 3 as built

What the code settled where the design left room. The READMEs of Projectile/, Slots/ and Shared/ describe the types.

Since replaced in part. The model, the board, the spawner's three stages and the resolver's bumper step stand, a cap now carrying its wall.

  • A mushroom is solid, like a bush. MushroomBumperModel is a static actor that is not IPassThrough: a rising balloon stops under it and its column is capped there. The plan did not say; the shared limit on permanent things already treats the two alike. It is the owner's to change: a mushroom balloons rise past would cost the board nothing but its own slot.
  • The rows the spawner reserves. The balloon spawner reserves no rows by index. Lines enter from under the grid and rise to the highest slot they can reach, and a solid actor caps its column wherever it stands. The one count it keeps free is IBalloonsConfiguration.PopSpawnFreeRows (2), at the bottom. The planner is given that or the settings' own bottom rows, whichever is more; the top row is the settings' alone.
  • The cannon is the point it turns about, at rest. The muzzle sits half a unit straight ahead of the pivot along the aim, so the line of every shot passes through the pivot, and the pivot is what the planner plans from. Not the pivot as it stands: the cannon rises half a unit as it enters and is punched back by each shot's recoil. The thrower captures the resting point once, when it starts and before its entrance is ever played (where its view stands plus the entrance's offset), and that is IShotOrigin.RestingPivot. The ball's radius is read off the projectile prefab's collider (0.125). Before the thrower starts no chain is placed.
  • The layout seed is the level's seed mixed with the run's: a retry keeps both and replays the layout, a new run rolls a new one. The level is IActiveLevelParameters.Level, the level Current was resolved for, and Current is where the spawner reads the mushrooms' count: the seed and the count come from one place, so they cannot name different levels.   • The bushes keep out of the flight, not out of centre lines. The strategy flies the chain's ideal shot by the kick and keeps the path, up to the hit that banks; a slot within the ball's radius, half a slot and the corridor margin of it is not offered to anything placed afterwards. On the open board that is 12 to 17 of the 66 slots. Measured over 400 seeds from the real cannon: the planner places the count asked for, 2, 3 or 4, every time.
  • The circles act on the point the walls act on, the position the step is handed. The ball's collider sits 0.03 ahead of it along the flight, so the path is the same line and the bounce comes that much late.
  • A step holds its own bounces. ProjectileStep.BumperBounces counts them and the outcome is BumperBounced only when no wall was met: a step can bounce off a mushroom and still reach a wall, and the wall's outcome stands. At most 8 bounces to one stretch.
  • The stretch flown back off a wall is swept too, in the same step. Without it a ball past four times its speed lands inside a mushroom, or beyond it, right after a wall. The stretch is rebuilt forward from the leg that met the wall, wall by wall (a corner is two), and each leg is swept: after a corner the step's direction is not the direction of its last leg, so the stretch cannot be found by walking back from where the step ended.
  • A wall step keeps two directions. ProjectileStep.Direction is where the ball goes next; WallDirection is how it left the wall, and differs only when a mushroom turned it again on the way back. The shield's squash at the wall and the lance's wake take the wall's; the wake then turns at the mushroom too.   • A ball found inside a circle bounces where its line came in. A deflect off a balloon next to a mushroom can set the ball down inside the mushroom's circle; the entry is then behind it.
  • The mushroom being left is skipped while the ball flies the direction it left along, not just for the rest of that step: a step can end on the circle. Any turn ends it.
  • What a bounce does to the speed economy. An unarmed shot's cruise ends and its run of empty wall bounces starts again, as on a balloon contact; its taps stay. The Sweep rule's segment starts again at the bounce, as at a deflect; its run of sweeps stays. An armed shot's run is not touched. The plan did not say, and the first of these is open for the owner (Open for the owner, above).
  • What looks ahead trusts the ball's own state for one leg only. The mushroom being left, and a spent stretch's free pass, hold from the ball to its first wall (BumperLookahead). On every leg a lookahead traces past a wall the mushrooms are in the way: the wall clears the chain.
  • A step's bounces are copied before they are announced. The resolver's list is one for every ball and the next Step of any ball refills it; the view copies it into its own before the first message goes out, so nothing a subscriber does can change what is being published.
  • Where piercing meant unbent in the live flight, and what was done: the pierce telegraph ends its run at the first mushroom; the tunnelling sweep is run leg by leg, into a mushroom as into a wall; the lance's wake turns at the mushroom; and the Snipe pencil line starts its run again there (SnipeLanceTrace.TurnRun). Phase 4 as built

What the code settled where the design left room. The READMEs of Item/, Audio/, Shared/ and Game/Telemetry/ describe the types.

  • When the ball becomes a lance. ProjectileView publishes each bounce from inside the ball's fixed step, after Step has resolved the step and before the ball is moved, and messages are delivered at once. A bounce that pays ends the step on the mushroom (Phase 4, after its review), so the order is: the step is flown up to that bounce, the kick off the mushroom included; the bounce is announced; MushroomChainController arms the ball; the view moves the ball to the bounce point. From there on it is a lance: the next step flies at the lance's speed, and whatever it meets is met piercing.
  • The armer does what it is told. ISnipeArmer.ArmAt(shot, at) arms a ball that is not piercing, its pencil line starting on the mushroom (the ball has not been moved yet when it is armed, so the point is handed over). Bank(shot) saves a ball already piercing one plain charge, which re-arms it at the discharge that spends the pierce it has. Which of the two a ball gets is the chain rule's to say.
  • The controller maps the rule's answer, with no judgement of its own: Arm to ArmAt, Bank to Bank. MushroomLanceMessage carries the answer (Reward), and Armed says the same as a flag.
  • Where the cap lives. In the chain rule, and nowhere else: MaxBanksPerFlight charges a flight. The rule never answers Arm for a ball that is a lance, but it does not count armings (Open for the owner: a lance spent at a wall can be armed again). The Snipe has no limit on banked charges and none was added.
  • Safe by keeping nothing. The controller has no count and no state: the count is the flight's and the answer rides on the message. It pays only a ball the registry still has in flight, so a reward for a ball that died, or was swept off by a run reset, a level change or the game over, is dropped.
  • A ball that completes a chain and dies at a wall in that same step cannot happen any more: the step ends on the mushroom, and the wall is the next step's, met as a lance.
  • The announcement is the lance's own. Arming flips the ball's piercing state, and everything that already announces a lance follows from that with no new code: pierce! at the ball (CombatHeadsUpRouter), the pierce loop (CombatSoundRouter), the spiral and the light (ProjectileView), the pencil line (SnipeLanceLines).   • No heads-up of its own. pierce! is written for every lance, however it was earned: the heads-up has no pattern for naming a lance's source, so none was added, and a banked grant writes nothing, as a banked pickup writes nothing. A line for the chain itself (as x3 ricochet is for deflects) would be a new style in the heads-up settings asset; MushroomBouncedMessage.Count is there for it.

Phase 4, after its review

Five findings of the review of phase 4, fixed with phase 5 because the first two change the rules the mirror has to match.

  • One extra charge a flight, truly (decision B). The rule capped its own Bank, but a wall reset the count, each later chain answered Arm again, and the controller turned an Arm on a lance into a charge the cap never saw: one lance and a charge for every further chain, bounded only by shields. MushroomChainRule.OnBounce is now told whether the ball is a lance as it meets the mushroom (armed earlier by its mushrooms, by a Snipe pickup or the Sharpshooter card, or by its own cruise), and answers:
The count the bounce brings the chain to The ball The answer
Under the target Any None
The target Not a lance Arm
The target A lance Bank while the flight has banked fewer than MaxBanksPerFlight, else None
Past the target Any Bank while the flight has banked fewer than MaxBanksPerFlight, else None

A ball can knock every 80 ms or so, so a run of knocks could hold all four voices and cut a real deflect's tail with each new one. What the code settled where the design left room. The READMEs of Slots/, Shaders/BalloonParty/, Configuration/, Editor/ and Tools/stage/ describe the parts.

  • What a mushroom looks like. A chubby toadstool seen from the front, as the balloons are: an orange domed cap with cream spots, a slim band of gills under its rim, and a fat cream stem. It is sized to fill the circle the ball bounces off (the settings' radius, 0.3), so the ball turns where the mushroom is seen to be.
  • Why orange. The balloons are green, red, blue and purple, and the reward balloons gold, lead and silver. Orange sits in the widest gap between the balloons' hues, red to green, and nothing on the board wears it. The red balloon is a tomato red and the golden one a dark plated gold, and the cap stood apart from both in the captures at day, dusk and night. The shape does the rest: a cap wider than its stalk with a flat underside is no balloon. Where it is weakest: at dusk the meadow and its bare dirt turn a warm brown close to the cap, and there the cream stem and spots carry the read more than the cap's colour does. The colour is the owner's to judge; every colour is a property of the Mushroom material.
  • Drawn, not painted. One quad and one shader (BalloonParty/Grid/Mushroom) that reads no texture: the cap, the gills and the stem are distance fields. The spots lie on the dome, so they flatten toward its edge, and each mushroom's are turned a different way.
  • It takes the light as the balloons do. The scene light is read once at the mushroom's centre: the lit side follows the time of day and any local light, the colour holds at night by the same re-grade as the golden balloon's (hue 0.25, dimming 0.4), and it is capped at white so it never blooms. The one exception is the flare of the spots when a chain pays.
  • The shadow is the shader's, not a baked child. The plan asked for the balloons' baked shadow child with a silhouette written by a script. The shader draws the shadow instead: the same shape a second time, as far down-light as a balloon's shadow lies from a balloon (0.1), soft at the edge, swinging away from a local light and fading with the light as theirs do. Three reasons. The shadow leans with the cap. Through a mist reveal the mushroom and its shadow fade with the meadow, as the bushes do; a child on the balloons' shadow material would have stayed in the mist after its meadow had gone. And with no sprite there is nothing to bake a silhouette from, so the script would have had to copy the shader's shape by hand. So Tools/art/ has nothing for the mushroom. One side effect: being drawn by the mushroom's own renderer, the shadow is in the GI's scene capture, as a bush's shadow is and a balloon's baked shadow is not. This is a departure from the plan, and the owner's to keep or change.
  • Where it stands in the scene. On the Grid layer, with the bushes, which the GI's scene capture reads: the mushroom occludes and bounces light as its neighbours do (the render maps show it in the capture). On the GridActors sorting layer, over the balloons and the ball, as the clouds are. No layer was added.
  • The reaction. A bounce off a mushroom pushes its cap along the ball's way in. The cap leans away, the stem bending under it by the square of the height, and wobbles back along the same line. The cap also tips into a sideways lean and is drawn out or pressed flat by one along the stem, which is what makes a hit from below read. A bounce that arms the lance or banks a charge also swells the cap for a moment and lights its spots.

  • The rule. No two mushrooms of a layout, next to each other in the chain or not, are nearer than MushroomPlacementLimits.MinSpacing, centre to centre: 1.9 world units as shipped, two and a half slots. Slots stand on a lattice, so two of them are 0.75, 1.3, 1.5, 1.98, 2.25 or 2.6 apart and so on: a spacing of 1.9 puts every pair 1.98 or more apart. The shortest leg is never read as less than the spacing, since two mushrooms next to each other in the chain are a pair like any other; as shipped the two are the same number. The spacing and the shortest leg are compared a thousandth of a unit generous, where every other limit is held a margin inside: a spacing is authored as a number of slots (1.5 is two), and pairs exactly that far apart are meant to pass, on every device.

The mushroom settings are a block of the Level Pacing asset that the asset does not hold (nobody has edited it in the Inspector), so the field initialisers are what plays, and they say 1.9. Once the block is saved, a spacing of 0 or less still reads as 1.9 (DefaultMinSpacing): the rule cannot be lost to an empty field or to a block saved before the field existed.

What was built, rule by rule.

  • 1. A lance cuts and flies on. ProjectileMotionResolver.CutCaps: a piercing ball's stretch is swept with the bounce's own swept test, nearest cap first, as often as it finds one, before the walls and on the stretch flown back off a wall while the ball still pierces. Each cap found is cut out of the field at once (IBumperField.Cut) and noted as a CapCut for the view, which publishes one MushroomCutMessage each (ProjectileView.AnnounceCapCuts, from its own copy of the step's cuts, as it does the bounces). The message's point is where the lance's path passes nearest the cap's centre; when only the ball's edge clipped the cap, the point is drawn in to 0.85 of the cap's radius, so that the line the view splits the cap along always crosses it and a sliver comes off. The cap has left the field before the message is sent, so it is cut once and announced once until it has grown back.
  • 2. A cut cap is no bumper. IBumperField.Bumpers lists whole caps only. Everything reads that one list: the flight's sweep, the kick (which is handed it, so it bends no ball toward a cut cap and lands none for one), the lookahead, the aim line and the solver's gather. So no reader needed a rule of its own for a cut cap. The cap's model stays in its slot (MushroomBoard remembers which models are cut), so the slot stays taken, and a cap keeps its id and its wall while others are cut: an index into the list is no longer an id.
  • 3. No bank. MushroomChainRule.OnBounce lost the question of whether the ball is a lance: it is only asked about a ball that is not one, and answers Arm on the hit that reaches the target and nothing otherwise. MushroomChainRules.MaxBanksPerFlight, MushroomChainState.Banks, ISnipeArmer.Bank, the controller's bank, the simulator's (PayMushroomReward), the tally's ChargesBanked, the stage's count of angles that bank and the catalogue stamp's bank number are gone. MushroomLanceMessage now only says that a ball was armed, where and at what count.
  • 4. Regrow on the beat. MushroomBoard.Cut gives the cap IMushroomSettings.RegrowBeats (5) beats to wait. MushroomRegrowController (Item/Snipe/) reads the board's beat once a frame while a cap is cut (IBoardBeat.OffBeats(0), the unbreakables' rhythm), tells the board each beat that falls, and regrows each cap that is due and has no ball in flight inside its circle, publishing MushroomRegrownMessage. The first beat is the first seen to fall after the frame of the cut, so a cap is gone for 6.8 to 8.5 seconds. A cap whose model has left the grid is forgotten by the board at its next rebuild: a level's end, a retry and a reset place new models, which are whole, and nothing is regrown or announced. The bank, as built

Decision K, built on 2026-11-19.

Where a charge is banked. In the flight's own step, where the cap is cut: ProjectileMotionResolver.CutCaps counts the cut (Flight.CapsCut) and adds one to Flight.BankedPierceCharges, the counter a Snipe picked up mid-pierce has always banked on. Nothing else was needed, and nothing was restored: ISnipeArmer.Bank stays gone. The charge is spent by the code that was there: at a wall the lance reaches with a balloon plowed, SpendPierce keeps the shot piercing while it holds a charge, and SnipeItemHandler takes the charge and arms the lance afresh when it hears of the discharge.

It is banked in the step, and not by whoever hears MushroomCutMessage, because of one case: a cap cut in the same fixed step that reaches the wall. The step sweeps its stretch for caps before it settles the wall, so the charge is there when the pierce is spent. A listener would bank it a moment too late, the lance would have ended, and the charge would sit unused on an ordinary ball.

What the view can hook. MushroomCutMessage.Charges, on the message it already hears:

Charges Means
1, 2, 3 ... A lance's cut made in flight. It banked that shot a charge, already banked when the message is heard, and this is the flight's count of them with this cut: 1 for the first cap a flight cuts
0 Nothing was banked: a blast (Cause is Blast), or a cut no flight made (the stage's)

It counts what the flight's cuts have earned, not what the shot still holds: a charge spent at a wall is not taken off it, and a Snipe picked up mid-pierce is not in it. IProjectileFlightState.CapsCut and BankedPierceCharges are there for anything that follows the ball itself. No message says a charge was spent; PierceDischargedMessage is published at that wall as before, and the lance's line restarts there (ISnipeLanceArming.OnLanceRearmed).

The cue. MushroomSoundRouter: the cut's stroke climbs a whole tone with each cap the flight has cut, up to six steps, and the Snipe's cue (ItemSnipe) plays once a flight, on the first charge, a whole tone above the pitch an arming plays it at. That is the cue the first bank used. It is not played for each charge: a lance through a wall of three banks three within a few frames.

Telemetry. mushroom_charges_banked is back, as id 43, appended after the mushrooms' other counters (37 to 43, on this branch alone). Its old id, 39, is mushroom_caps_cut's.

The mirror holds the charges banked equal on both sides wherever it holds the caps cut, over every flight it flies. The ranking gives a charge no value.

The catalogue. Untouched: the rule has no number, the stamp carries none for it, and no layout is kept or dropped by what a lance does after it is armed.

The two bounds on a lance that re-arms itself, in the code and in tests.

  • Its shields. ProjectileMotionResolver.ResolveWalls takes a shield at every wall before it looks at the pierce, and ends the flight at a wall reached with none. A charge changes whether the ball is a lance off that wall, never whether it lives. So a flight has exactly one leg more than it has shields, banked charges or none (MushroomBankTests: a lance with 40 shields on a seven-cap board with unbreakables to plow flies 41 legs from every angle tried, cuts no cap twice and never spends more than it banked).
  • The caps. A cut cap is out of the field until MushroomRegrowController puts it back, on the fifth beat after its cut. Over the real board with the regrow running, a lance with 60 shields cuts a cap again only once it has grown back, never sooner than four whole beats (6.8 seconds) after the cut, and banks one charge a cut (the same fixture).

A cruise's pierce. The rule reads the one state the game has for a ball that pierces, so a pierce earned by cruising banks on its cuts as a Snipe's lance does. At the wall that spends it (the first one reached with an unbreakable or a tough plowed) SpendPierce ends the cruise and, a charge being banked, keeps the ball piercing; SnipeItemHandler.TrySpendBankedCharge then takes the charge and arms it with the Snipe's speed buff. So a cruise's pierce that has cut a cap is a Snipe's lance from that wall until its shields run out. With no cap cut, or nothing plowed, it is as it was before the bank. This follows from "any lance", is the owner's to keep or change (Open for the owner: what was decided for him in the bank), and is held as it is by a test.

How long a flight can last. One leg more than its shields: two legs with the shield a ball starts with, five with the most a ball starts with (four: the Thick Skin card adds one and stacks three times). A leg flown as a lance is a straight line, at most the board's diagonal, 9.3 units. The shipped Snipe flies a lance at 3.93 times a ball's speed (ItemConfiguration, 38.8 units a second), so such a leg lasts a quarter of a second at the most. The smoke is the game's smoke field (Scenario/SmokeFieldService, the field the ball's trail and the tough balloons' pops stamp into). It takes capsule stamps of a source's colour; a new source, MushroomSpores, has its profile on the Smoke Field Settings asset (a pale warm dust, 0.93 / 0.87 / 0.68). A cone is three capsules laid end to end along the burst's way, each wider than the last and thinner (four for a blast), through one new overload, Paint(source, to, from, radius, opacityScale). What the field then does with it is the field's: it lights it as it lights all its smoke, so it does not glow at night; it drifts it on its wind, frays it from its edges and fades it at its own rate. How long a puff lasts is therefore the field's to say, not a number of the mushroom's: a hit's small puff is gone in about a second, a blast's wide cloud in about two and a half. The field also shows nothing of a stamp under about a third of its opacity, so a puff cannot be made fainter to make it shorter: a slow hit's is smaller instead.

The specks are the mushroom's own particles. The game's speck field (Slots/Actor/SpeckField) was looked at first and does not fit: it is one field of ambient dust on the graphics card, a request to it only makes more of that dust appear near a point, and a speck there has no way of its own, no colour of its own (it takes the colour of the light near it, from the light field's sixteen slots) and no end. A spore needs all three. So each mushroom's prefab carries one particle system (Spores, built by the mushroom's builder), never playing by itself: MushroomView.EmitSpores emits into it one speck at a time, with its place, way, speed, size, life and a colour somewhere between a green (0.45 / 0.95 / 0.6) and a blue (0.4 / 0.75 / 1). The system slows a speck, fades it in and out and shrinks it; a speck lives 0.75 seconds, give or take a third, and is 0.06 across. They are drawn by a small shader of their own, Particle/SporeSpeck, a soft dot from the particle's own quad with no texture: coloured dust by day, dimmed with the time of day's light, and glowing a little past white at night by the same measure of night as the cap's warts. No palette slot was added.

Kept quiet two ways (MushroomSporePlanner, plain C#, told the time):

  • A mushroom that has puffed for a hit keeps quiet for 0.25 seconds. A ball can knock a cap every 80 ms; a rally of 25 knocks between two caps in two seconds is 13 puffs.
  • The board shares one budget, counted in hits: 14, coming back at 4 a second. A hit takes up to 1 of it (1.5 when it pays), a cut 1.6, a blast 4.5. What is asked when less is left comes out that much smaller: a hit or a cut under a fifth of itself is not let go at all, and a blast always shows, at a quarter of itself at the least. So a nuke's seven caps let go three bursts whole and four small ones: 90 specks where seven whole bursts would be 154.

Where it lives. MushroomSporeRouter (Slots/Actor/Archetype/, an entry point of the game scope, beside MushroomSoundRouter in kind) hears the two messages, asks the planner, stamps the smoke and hands the specks to the view the grid holds at the slot. A blast's burst waits its delay in a fixed list of eight. Nothing is allocated for a hit: the planner keeps one number a slot, the list is fixed, and emitting a particle allocates nothing. The numbers are IMushroomSettings.Spores (MushroomSporeTuning).

What it costs.

  • A hit: three smoke stamps and up to 8 particles. The smoke field draws its stamps in one pass over its own small texture (16 texels a world unit, about 100 by 180 for the board), up to 32 stamps a pass, whether it has one stamp that frame or thirty-two; it already runs that pass every frame a ball is in flight, for the ball's trail.
  • The worst frame is a nuke's burst: seven caps' bursts fall due within a frame or two of each other, 28 smoke stamps (one pass) and 90 particles emitted, drawn by up to seven particle renderers on one material.
  • At rest, nothing: a mushroom's particle system is stopped and empty, and the router is one comparison a frame.

What the films showed. In the second Editor, in staged scenes, with every bounce published through the game's own publisher and every bomb, nuke and lance the game's own: a hit from the left and from below at the cannon's speed, a soft hit and the hit that pays, frame by frame at twice the game's size; the same hit as smoke alone, specks alone and both; a rally of 25 knocks 80 ms apart between two caps at day, at night and with the spores off; a bomb beside the wall and a nuke on a board of seven caps, at day and night; a real lance through the wall of two.

  • A hit reads: the cap leans, a pale puff stands off its far rim and the specks run out along the ball's way, slow, and are gone in about half a second. The puff is a soft blob more than a cone: the smoke field is 16 texels to a world unit, and a cone 0.6 long is ten of them. It is the specks that say which way.
  • The puff lasts about a second, not the "some seconds" the field's fade alone would give: the field also frays a patch from its edges, and a small patch is all edge. A blast's cloud, being wide, lasts about two and a half.
  • The first numbers were wrong twice, and the films said so. The smoke field shows nothing of a stamp under about a third of its opacity (its display has a threshold), so the first puff, at 0.3, was invisible; and a blast's cloud at full opacity and three hits' width sat on the board for many seconds as a block. The puff is at 0.6 now, a slow hit's is smaller and not fainter, and a blast's cloud is thinner than a hit's puff (0.45) and 0.72 wide.
  • A rally does not fog the board, and it does mark it. Of 25 knocks 13 puff. Where every knock on a cap comes the same way, as in the staged rally, the puffs land on one spot and build a patch of dust about a cap across beside each cap, there for the two seconds of the rally and about a second and a half after. It lies on the far side of each cap, away from the lane the ball flies between them, and covers no cap. With the spores off the same rally is only the caps wobbling.
  • A blast is the biggest of the three. As the bomb's own cloud thins, at about 0.45 seconds, each blown cap is in the air with a fan of specks round where it sat and a few behind it, over a wedge of dust along its throw. At night the specks glow and carry it; the dust is dim then.
  • A nuke's seven caps go off in turn as the blast's edge passes, three bursts whole and four small.

What still reads weak.

  • The smoke is a blob, not a cone, and soft: by itself it does not say which way the hit went.
  • The specks are small, 12 pixels at the game's size at the most. At day on bright grass the bluest ones are easy to miss; at night they are the clearest part.
  • The dust's colour is fixed, a pale warm: over the brown patches of the meadow's ground it is nearly the ground's own colour.
  • A blast's wedge of dust lies where the cap flew from for two and a half seconds, after the cap has gone.
  • A blue or green bomb's own paint is close in hue to the specks, so beside such a bomb the burst's specks do less to tell it apart.

An idle cap

The owner (2026-11-19): "Two things I believe the mushrooms might need an idle animation, the are bit hidden in between all what is happening so might be worth making something that puts focus on then, I think a breather animation on the cap might be enough and for them to react to the disturbance field".

So a cap nothing has touched is never still. Both are looks: the circle the ball bounces off, the kick, the aim line, the solver, the bot and the telemetry know nothing of them.

The breath. The cap swells and settles, 0.08 of its radius at the top of a breath, every 2.6 seconds, resting a moment at each end. It only ever swells: the cap is never drawn smaller than its collider, and at the top it is 0.024 world units past it, about five pixels on a phone, so a ball bouncing off still looks like it touches. From above a swell reads as the cap rising toward the eye, so three things ride it and it is not a flat scale of the picture: the shadow lies up to 0.8 further off, the warts spread over the dome (0.08), and at night their glow swings 0.4 either side of its own strength, from 0.6 of it breathed out to 1.4 breathed in. That last is the strongest of all: by day the breath is a slow swell, at night it is a board of caps lighting up in turn.

Whose breath. Two ways were filmed. With every cap at its own point of the breath by its seed, the board twinkles and no cap belongs to another. With a breath that runs across the board (0.2 of a breath a unit along the diagonal, so next caps of a wall are 0.05 to 0.2 of a breath apart, and a tenth of a breath of the seed's own on top), the caps of a wall swell nearly together, one after the next, and walls apart from each other are at different points. The second is what ships: a wall is the thing the player aims between, and a wall that breathes as one reads as one thing among balloons that each sway their own way.

A cap's place in that wave is the point of the ground it stands on (Ground.cginc's GroundPointAt, the ground's own scroll and scale taken out of its world position), not its place in the world. The mushrooms hang under the scenario root, which a level's ascent carries up and a mist reveal scales, and a place in the world would have run every breath fast or slow by 0.2 of a breath for each unit travelled: a breath of 2.6 seconds on a board carried three units in a second would have gone at two and a half times its pace, or backward. Read in the Editor with the root carried 1.5, 3 and -2 units: every cap's world position moved with it and its ground point did not. Under a reveal's scale the same holds by the root's own arithmetic (content at p shows at scale times travel plus p, and the ground scrolls by scale times travel); that was reasoned, not driven.

The rocking. The shader reads the disturbance field (Shared/Disturbance, the one the grass and the bush leaves answer) once a vertex, at the mushroom's centre, and leans the cap the way the field is pushed, by the path a hit's lean takes, so the cap tips and the stem shows. The field holds a push for a second or more, as the grass stays bent, so a lean alone would be a cap left standing crooked: the lean swings instead (0.6 of it, every 0.45 seconds, the rest held away from what passed) and dies as the field lets go. It goes by the displacement's square, so what the field always holds moves nothing.

The cut's frozen pose is left as it was. The pieces of a cut cap start from the cap's resting size and from the hit's lean alone, so a cap cut at the top of a breath loses 8% of its radius, about five pixels a side on a phone, and whatever the field was rocking it by, in the frame it is cut. Filmed frame by frame with a real lance through a cap at the top of its breath: in that frame the lance's flash lies over the cap and its halves are already parting (0.16 each way in all), and no step shows. Carrying the breath into the pose would be a few lines in the view; the rocking is only on the GPU, and carrying it would rock the falling pieces. Neither was done.

Where the field is. DisturbanceFieldService is registered in the app's own scope, for every scenario and every quality tier, and no setting switches it off. The shader reads it only once the game has pushed the field's bounds and the view the rocking's numbers, both 0 before, so an unplaced prefab and a preview stand still.

Where the work is. In the shader's vertex stage, four vertices a mushroom: a cosine for the breath, one read of the field, a square root, a hyperbolic tangent and a sine for the rocking. A pixel pays nothing new: the one change in the fragment program is that the warts' place on the dome is scaled by a value from the vertex stage where it was a constant, and no interpolator was added (the tilt's grew from three numbers to four). What does grow is the cap's own area, 8% on average and 17% at the top of a breath, and with it the pixels that pay for the warts; the quad's box at rest is the dark under the overhang, which is wider than the swollen cap, so the box does not grow. MushroomView hands the shader three vectors (_Breath, _BreathLook, _Rock) once, when it is spawned, with the radius and the seed, which never change either, and is switched off while its cap is at rest, as before: an idle mushroom costs the CPU nothing a frame, and a moving one pushes only what moves. The quad grew by the rocking's most (its half from 2.084 to 2.149) and the prefab was rebuilt.

The quad's room is worked out in one place, MushroomQuadRoom.Half, from the material's own swell at a full pulse, the breath, the furthest lean, the rocking's most, a cut cap's flight, and the further of the two things that carry the shadow off: a blown cap's lift and the breath's (never both: a cap in the air has left its stem, and a bare stem's shadow does not breathe). The builder sizes the quad by it and a test holds the built quad to it, so a number raised without the prefab rebuilt fails the suite before a cap's edge is cut off square. As shipped the cut's flight is what sets the size (1.19 past the radius against a swollen cap's 0.09), so the breath has room to spare; it was the formula that was wrong, with the swell a constant that the pulse and the breath happened to add up to.

How far a cap may go. MushroomSettings.Validate counts the rocking with the lean: the two together may not pass three quarters of the radius (shipped 0.15 and 0.065, 0.72 of it), past which the cap stands beside its stem and not on it. It was half the radius for the lean alone, and its reason, the quad's room, has not been true since the quad took its size from the numbers.

The numbers are IMushroomSettings.Idle (MushroomIdleTuning): breath 0.08 over 2.6 s, phase spread 0.1, wave 0.2 a unit, shadow lift 0.8, glow swing 0.4, wart spread 0.08; rock 0.16 a unit of displacement squared, 0.065 at the most, 0.45 s a swing, 0.6 of it swinging. Tests hold the breath to what a bounce allows, the rocking under half a cannon shot's lean, and the built quad to the room the numbers ask for.

A waiting burst no longer lands on a new board. MushroomSporeRouter kept a blast's burst for its delay by slot alone and was told of no reset, so a restart or a level change inside those 0.3 seconds let the burst go on whatever mushroom the new board put in that slot. It now keeps the view the burst is for and lets it go only if that view still stands in the slot; it is told of a run reset (IRunResettable) and of a new level (ILevelProgress.Level) and drops what is waiting at either.

The router has its tests (MushroomSporeRouterTests, nine cases): a hit's and a cut's specks go at once and only from the mushroom they are for; a blast's burst waits its delay and is let go once; it is dropped when its mushroom has left the board, and is not let go to another that took the slot; a ninth burst goes at once when eight are waiting; a run reset and a new level each drop what is waiting.

Read back in the Editor: the prefab's Spores particle system simulates in world space, so a speck let go stays where it is when the board's root moves; it does not loop or play on awake, and holds 96 at the most.

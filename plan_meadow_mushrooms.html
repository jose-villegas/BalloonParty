Meadow Mushrooms

Meadow mushrooms: bumpers that earn a lance

On meadow levels the board grows mushrooms: permanent bumpers the ball bounces off, pinball style. A ball that hits three of them in one flight, without touching a wall in between, becomes the Snipe lance. To make room, the meadow loses its Tough and Tougher balloons, and its unbreakables form only the snake. Talked through with the owner on 2026-11-18 and banked; started on 2026-11-19, when he settled the open points below. Status: planned. Nothing is built. Decisions A to G wait on the owner.

Open decisions for the owner

Each has a recommendation; nothing below is built until he answers.

Question Recommendation and why
A Does a lance bounce off mushrooms too? Yes. Today a piercing shot flies on unbent everywhere (the game, the solver and the aim line). A lance that passed through mushrooms would make them vanish from play the moment the reward lands

Design

Proposed by the architect on 2026-11-19 from a read of the code. What it verified and what it assumed is marked under Risks.

Contact is worked out, not left to physics

The live ball only collides with balloons: ProjectileView.OnTriggerEnter2D looks for a BalloonView and calls ProjectileHitResolver. Bushes have no collider, and the Deflector, Absorber and Gatekeeper actors are not reachable in play. So a mushroom is not a new collider. There are at most 4 permanent circles, and ProjectileMotionResolver.Step tests the step's segment against them before the walls, then carries the rest of the step along the new direction, as Deflect does for a deflecting balloon.

  • No tunnelling, and no physics layer, so no ProjectSettings change.
  • The shot solver works event to event, so it mirrors the bounce exactly.
  • 4 circle tests a fixed step, nothing allocated.

The forgiving kick

c is the contact point and r the true reflection.

  • The candidates are the other mushrooms whose bearing from c lies within KickConeDegrees of r, with a corridor clear of other mushrooms (balloons are ignored: they pop or are ricocheted off on the way).
  • The one at the smallest angle wins. With no candidate the ball leaves along r.
  • The ball is aimed beside that mushroom's centre, by KickAimOffset of its radius, on the side r already leaned to. Never at the centre: a dead-centre hit reflects straight back, and two mushrooms would trade the ball forever with no wall to stop it.
  • The kick applies only until the count passes the chain target by one. After that bounces are true, and their spread ends any cycle. MaxBanksPerFlight caps the reward.

Placement

  • Inputs: the slots left empty after the puff clouds, the cannon's origin and aim arc, the walls, the count, the radii, the run's seed.
  • Constraints: the first mushroom is hit by a straight shot inside the aim arc, down a corridor clear of the other mushrooms and the bushes; each leg to the next mushroom is clear the same way; the turn at each mushroom is between about 40 and 140 degrees, so the hit needed is neither grazing nor dead centre; legs are neither too short nor too long; no edge columns and no rows the spawner needs.
  • Search: depth first, candidates shuffled by the seed, within an iteration budget.
  • When it fails: try one mushroom fewer, down to 2; then place none and log a warning.
  • Mushrooms are placed before bushes, and the bushes keep out of the chain's corridors.

The mirror and the aim line

Every rule a shot meets is mirrored in Solver/ and held by mirror tests. A mushroom is a new contact kind there, and the chain state rides in the solver's flight state. PredictionTraceCalculator already reflects off deflector circles and ignores them while piercing; it takes the same bounce rule (decisions A and C).

Art and the cap's reaction

  • The body is drawn in a shader from the quad's own position, as the nuke, the disco ball and the golden balloon are: the cap and the stem as distance fields, lit through Include/SceneLight.cginc. No painted sprite, one renderer. dotnet build does not compile shaders, so each change needs a look in the Editor.
  • The shadow follows the baked-shadow-child convention; a small script under Tools/art/ writes the blurred silhouette, as the heart balloon's script does.
  • The reaction: MushroomView keeps one DampedSpring2D (the shield view already uses it). A bounce on its own slot adds an impulse along the ball's incoming direction, scaled by its speed; the spring is underdamped, so the cap leans away and wobbles back along that same line. Each frame it writes the lean to the material, and the shader bends the stem by the square of the height and carries the cap the whole way, which is what makes the stalk read as elastic.
  • Nothing ticks at rest: the view switches itself off once the spring is still and on again at an impulse. No tweens, and the spring is reset when the view goes back to the pool.

Risks

  • Piercing means "unbent" in three places (the game, the solver, the aim line). Bending a lance off mushrooms (decision A) is the largest change to the mirror.
  • Every cast that assumes only balloons block needs the bumpers added: the cruise lookahead, the doomed last-shield glide (or it passes through a mushroom), the tunnelling sweep and the aim fan. Each gets a test.
  • A mushroom bounce spends no shield (decision F), so the loop guards carry the weight.
  • Balance. Toughs are dropped before the threat budget is spent, so the budget buys other threats; the curves are LevelGenerationSettings.BudgetForLevel and ScenarioScheduleSettings. The target stands: the game picks up after level 10. Compare tunings with the playtest bot before and after.
  • Needs the Editor: the prefab, the material, the config entries, the settings asset on the game scope (decision G), and every look at the shader.
  • Assumed, not verified: which rows the spawner reserves; that the balancer leaves static actors alone; that no shot is in flight while the board ascends between levels; the layer the mushroom should draw on so it occludes for the GI as balloons do; the spring's constants.

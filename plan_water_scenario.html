Water Scenario & Bubble Merge

Water scenario, rising bubbles and the bubble merge

A third backdrop beside the sky and the meadow: a water surface that ripples where the ball and the balloons pass, read from the same disturbance field the grass bends to. On a water level soap bubbles keep landing on the board, more often the longer the level runs, and two bubbles merge whenever they sit next to each other. Planned 2026-11-10; the owner settled the open decisions the same day. Status: building.

Owner direction (2026-11-10)

"Another scenario, a water surface, same using disturbance to draw ripples over the background, here bubbles spawn and the more we stay on this level the more bubbles spawn over balloons, this session also finishes the merge logic for bubbles neighbors, bubbles merge only on nudge when the nudge puts them closer to a neighbor bubble."

Design

1. Water in the schedule

ScenarioKind.Water = 2. Sky stretches still separate every two surface stretches, so the mist reveal is always sky ↔ surface and never ground ↔ water. Each surface stretch after the first meadow rolls whether it is a meadow or water (WaterShare, 50%). A surface stretch's length is rolled as before, in the meadow's range, before its kind: turning water on leaves every stretch where it was, and only some meadows become water. The kind roll hashes the stretch's first level with its own key, mixed with the schedule salt and the run seed like every other roll, so a run's layout is stable across retries and each run gets its own. The first meadow stays fixed: bushes still arrive on time.

The water surface has no clouds or bushes, and nothing on it bounces shots but the unbreakable balloon (owner rules). IContentPiece.KeptOffWater marks what a water level leaves out: puff clouds, bushes, the Tough and Tougher balloons (they deflect until their last hit), and the Deflector and Gatekeeper actors. LevelGenerator drops those pieces before any budget is spent, as a sky level drops ground-only ones, so their budget buys the level's other threats rather than a pick that comes out empty. Clouds aren't faded out as they are over a clearing meadow: on water they're simply absent, and ScenarioStretch's puff-cloud and bush shares are 0 there. RequiresGround stays a ground-only flag beside it.

2. The water backdrop

GroundView becomes the view of every surface scenario: the floor quad swaps its material between the meadow floor and a new BalloonParty/Scenario/Water material, and the grass blades and their wake draw on ground only. The mist reveal, the depth scaling, _GroundCoverage and the sky hiding all apply to water unchanged (the planner and the curve read "not sky" instead of "ground"). The water settings (material, colours, ripple tuning) live on GroundSettings next to the floor's, through IGroundSettings.

  • The surface moves by a cheap shallow-water simulation on the GPU (WaterRippleBuffer, WaterRipple.shader), 16 cells per world unit over the disturbance field's bounds, stepped 120 times a second of game time. It has flow (water runs from high to low, so waves travel and a ball drags a wake), surface tension (short ripples outrun long ones, so a splash spreads into a train of wavelets), resonance (the screen's edges are walls the waves reflect off), height (crests read lighter, troughs darker) and breakup (a crest past the break slope loses flow, and foam shows on the fold of a sharp crest and moves with it). Every impact the game reports (ImpactEventBus: each step of a ball's flight, pops, bombs, balloons gliding in) presses the water for 0.12 s and drags it along the impact's heading. The surface's slope (Include/WaterSurface.cginc) is the simulation's plus a slow swell of two noise layers on different headings, the smaller slower, travelling with the content root. Each wave also throws a bright and a dark band on the sand and sheens the surface whatever the sun's angle, so it reads at any time of day.
  • The water has depth. A noise map lays sandbanks and deep channels (0.6 to 4.5 world units). The bottom is seen as from a camera above the middle of the screen, so a deeper bottom shrinks toward the middle and slides slower when the board scrolls; the surface's slope bends the view of it in proportion to depth. The water absorbs each colour by its own rate (red first) and scatters its own colour back: shallows read as sand, deep water as teal. The sand carries ripple bands.
  • Caustics are a second texture rendered under the surface, as the owner proposed: each frame WaterCausticBuffer draws one tile of a moving caustic net (WaterCaustics.shader) into a 256² R8 target that wraps, with mips. The net is the borders between drifting cells, warped so they curve, bright on the border and fading into each cell. Water.shader lays it under the surface twice, at two scales and angles: the first draws the lines, the second, read softer, varies their brightness along them, so the net never looks tiled. It's wobbled by the noise, shifted away from the time-of-day sun (_SceneLightDir), split into colour fringes along it, and blurred by up to 1.5 mips where the water is deep.
  • Layers, bottom up: the sand lit by the caustics (shaded around its own colour, never added past it, so nothing blooms), the water body's absorption and scatter, then a light veil of water colour. On top, the slopes facing the sun catch a soft highlight, and the crests turned just so between the sun and the eye glint.
  • Lit by SceneLightTintAt (at 0.7 influence, so a warm evening tints the water rather than turning it olive; light above full is eased in so a pop's glow doesn't clip to white) and premultiplied by GroundCoverageAt, like Ground.shader.
  • What didn't work: the first look read as neon and dark (bright additive caustics over saturated deep blues). Caustics drawn from the surface's own curvature into a low-resolution buffer came out as soft blobs that didn't read as caustics. Sparkles from fine noise waves read as static. Rings drawn straight from the field (sin(k·push + ωt)·push) kept the push's shape and wobbled in place like gel, and a flat two-colour bottom gave the water no volume (owner: "behaves more like a gel, ripples don't travel. Also lacks depth"). A wave-equation simulation came next. Driven by the soft, board-wide field it only sloshed in big blobs; driven by the impacts, its dozens of rings a second interfered into a chop in which no ring read (owner: "I don't see ripples"). Rings drawn one by one from each impact read, but never met anything (owner: "there is no flow, tension or resonance, no height or breakup"): the shallow-water simulation replaced them, pressed smoothly by each impact rather than re-driven by the whole field every frame. Its first foam came from every steep slope and every big splash and covered half the screen after a board clear (owner: "it should be bound to where the normals suddenly change, so around peaks"), and its waves barely showed through the swell and the caustics until they stood taller, shaded their crests and bent the bottom more. Tuning that against one shot on calm water misled twice: a crest that breaks too early kept a normal hit's peaks under the foam threshold and its waves faint (owner: "barely visible", "is the foam even present?"), while letting it break later, with the waves lasting 2.5 s and the walls reflecting them, built up so much energy in real play that the water saturated and foamed everywhere. The sim is tuned while the game plays: waves fade over a second, crests break past 0.15 a cell. Foam from per-cell curvature still barely showed (owner: "foam should basically trail behind the peaks"): the waves are several cells wide, so only the crests right at an impact bent sharply enough. Foam from a crest's height, lasting most of a second as a trail, foamed the whole top of every big swell in a coarse lace (owner: "too strong now, and doesn't feel local to the peaks, almost like it's own thing"; "maybe instead of trailing just appear at the inflexion, this will carry it"). Foam now comes from the fold: how sharply a crest standing past 0.15 folds over in its sharpest direction (axes and diagonals), which a crest line does across itself and a broad swell never does. It lasts a tenth of a second, so it sits on the fold and moves with the crest, in a fine, light lace. The tile's cell wrap uses id - n·floor((id + 0.5)/n), not fmod: GPU division is approximate, fmod(10, 5) can return 5, and the tile then shows a seam.

3. Bubbles that build up on water

Bubbles arrive with the clock's lines, taking a growing share of each line the longer the level runs, until a line brings more bubbles than balloons. SpawnClock counts the level's playing seconds (ISpawnClock.SecondsOnLevel, under the clock's own holds: Game screen, Playing, not paused; reset on level-up and run reset). On a water level, BalloonSpawner rolls each pick of a clock line against the bubble share before the level's weighted pick: a hit takes a bubble in that place, a miss the usual balloon. The initial fill has no bubbles.

The share is a pure function of seconds on the level (BubbleRiseCurve.ShareAt(settings, seconds)): none during GraceSeconds (6 s), then it climbs linearly from StartShare (15%) to MaxShare (65%) over RampSeconds (60 s) and holds. MaxBubbles (12) caps the bubbles on the board at once; past it the pick falls back to the usual balloon. A water bubble starts as StartBubbles (1) bubbles, so merges have room to grow it. Settings: IBubbleRiseSettings, a [Serializable] block on LevelPacingConfiguration.

The bubble uses the balloon catalogue's BubbleCluster prefab entry, even while the level generator's entry is Withheld: the water rise is its own path.

4. The merge

Two bubble clusters in neighbouring slots merge, whatever brought them there: a spawn, the board settling, a pop opening the way. BubbleMergeService (Balloon/Controller/, a tick) looks again whenever the grid has changed (SlotGrid.MutationVersion), a cluster was hit, or its own last pass merged something, and merges every neighbouring pair where the larger still has room. BubbleMergeRule is the pure rule, tested on its own.

  • The smaller cluster gives its bubbles to the larger (between equals, the slot order decides), as many as fill the larger to 5. A giver left with none leaves the grid silently and slides into the receiver; one with bubbles left stays where it is, smaller: a 3 beside a 3 makes a 5 and a 1.
  • A cluster between two neighbours gives to the one it makes the bigger cluster.
  • Each cluster merges at most once a frame, and a pass that merged looks again on the next frame, so a group gathers a step a frame. A hit on a cluster also wakes it: knocking a full cluster down makes room for its smaller neighbour to give again.
  • A first version merged only when a nudge pushed two bubbles closer; the owner dropped that: "Just merge them when next to each other, no nudge".

Owner decisions (2026-11-10)

  1. How often water comes up: a coin flip for every surface stretch after the first meadow.
  2. "Over balloons": "they spawn more than balloons increasingly": bubbles come in the lines, their share growing with time on the level.
  3. The curve: the ramp from the proposed 6 s grace over 60 s (the proposal was a spawn wait shortening from 8 s to 2 s; carried over as a share rising from 15% to 65%).
  4. Clusters bigger than two: first, a merge happened only when the sum fit in 5 bubbles. The owner changed it: "a cluster only gives as much it can to its higher neighbor until it hits the limit (5)". The smaller gives what fits and keeps the rest.
  5. When they merge: whenever two sit next to each other, no nudge needed (it first followed the direction above, "only on nudge"; the owner changed it after trying it).
  1. What water leaves out: "water surface has no clouds or bushes", and "no deflector balloons except for unbreakable": no Tough, Tougher, Deflector or Gatekeeper on water either.

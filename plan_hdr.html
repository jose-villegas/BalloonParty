HDR rendering — where the range goes, and how to switch it on

Turn on HDR rendering in the URP pipeline and use the range where the game already wants it: pops of light on lit surfaces, glitter, shine and specular sweeps, item glows, the level-up. Written 2026-11-10 from an audit of the pipeline assets, the runtime render targets, every shader under Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/ and every material under Assets/Materials/. Nothing is switched yet. The decisions taken after the audit are at the end.

This plan replaces the HDR part (Wave B) of HDR Color Pipeline. That plan made the switch to Linear colour space a prerequisite, because it read URP as refusing HDR colour grading in Gamma space. URP 17.6's source says otherwise (see Gamma colour space below), so HDR can ship first and Linear stays a separate, later choice.

What HDR buys this game

Today every colour is clamped to 0–1 when it is written to the screen buffer. Three kinds of content lose something to that clamp:

  1. Light that is already brighter than 1. The scene-light field adds up to AccumulationCeiling (3) on top of the ambient light (about 1) around a pop or an item, so SceneLightTintAt() reaches about 4×. Every shader that multiplies a surface by it clips the middle of the pop to flat white. The water shader eases it by hand (light /= 1 + max(light - 1, 0) * 0.7) because the pale water clipped worst.
  2. Additive highlights on a bright base. A shine band, a glint or a glitter speck added to a sprite that is already near white clips at once, so the highlight has no core.
  3. Darkening after brightening. The screen-space light overlay multiplies the finished frame (Blend DstColor SrcColor). A shadow that falls across a clipped white pop darkens the clipped value, not the real one.

HDR keeps those values above 1 until the end of the frame. On a normal (SDR) phone screen, range above 1 only becomes visible through something that reads it: a tonemapper that rolls it off smoothly instead of clipping, bloom that spreads it into a glow, or a darkening pass that brings it back into range. HDR with post-processing off costs a full-screen copy each frame and shows nothing, so this plan always pairs HDR with post-processing.

Cost and trade-offs

Format

URP picks the camera colour format in MakeRenderTextureGraphicsFormat: B10G11R11 (32-bit) when the precision is 32-bit and the device can blend into it, else R16G16B16A16 (64-bit) when it can blend into that, else whatever the device calls its HDR default (possibly 8-bit, so effectively LDR). The fallback is automatic.

B10G11R11 (32-bit) R16G16B16A16 (64-bit)
Bandwidth Same as today's RGBA8 Twice: each full-screen read or write of the colour buffer costs double, which matters on a fill-rate-bound tile GPU
Alpha None (fine for the main camera; the game reads no alpha from it) Yes
Precision Floats with 6-bit (R, G) and 5-bit (B) mantissas. Between 0.5 and 1 the steps are 1/128 (R, G) and 1/64 (B), against 1/255 today. 10-bit mantissa: finer than today everywhere
Range 0 to about 65000, no negatives ±65504

Because the game renders in Gamma, the buffer holds gamma-encoded values, and the bright half of every gradient is exactly where B10G11R11 is coarsest. The sky's clear colour has blue at 0.92, so the sky, the mist and the water are the first places to band. Camera dithering (on with post) hides most of it.

Fixed cost of HDR

  • HDR forces an intermediate colour target and a final pass to the screen every frame.
  • Post-processing must be on all the time: without it the HDR buffer is copied out and clipped with no dithering. With it, URP runs its uber post pass (grading, tonemapping, dithering, and bloom's composite if bloom is on): one full-screen read and write. Since Phase 1 it runs every frame (before it, only while ScenarioGradeView held post-processing on).
  • The HUD overlay camera (Phase 1 item 3b) stops post-processing from writing straight to the screen: the base camera post-processes into an intermediate, the HUD draws over it, and a final copy goes to the screen. One more full-screen copy per frame.
  • Measure this "empty post" cost, with the HUD camera, on device first (Phase 1). It is the go/no-go number for the whole plan, against the 8.33 ms frame budget at 120 Hz (see Performance Recovery).

Bloom

URP 17.6's Bloom has the mobile knobs: filter (Gaussian, Dual, Kawase), downscale (Half, Quarter), maxIterations (2–8), highQualityFiltering (keep off), threshold, clamp. A mobile starting point is Dual or Kawase, Quarter, 4 iterations, threshold above 1. Bloom's down-and-up chain is the second-largest new cost after the post pass. It is also the only way an SDR phone shows range above 1 as glow rather than just less clipping.

On-tile post-processing

URP 17.6 ships an On-Tile Post Processing renderer feature (OnTilePostProcessFeature) built for tile GPUs: grading LUT, tonemapping, vignette and film grain in one on-tile pass, no bloom. If the decision is "tonemap without bloom", it is the cheap path. Untested on the 2D renderer; check before relying on it.

Platforms

  • Android, Vulkan (primary): both formats are widely supported for rendering and blending.
  • Android, OpenGL ES 3 (fallback): float render targets need EXT_color_buffer_float; without it URP falls back to the HDR default, possibly LDR. fixed may run at lowp here (item 8).
  • iOS, Metal (iOS 15, A9 and later): both formats supported.
  • WebGL 2 (the web demo): float render targets need EXT_color_buffer_float, which most desktop browsers have; the demo may fall back. Check it in the web build before shipping.
  • HDR display output (HDR10 panels) is a different feature and stays out of scope.

Gamma colour space

URP 17.6 handles HDR in Gamma: UberPost.shader converts sRGB to linear before grading and tonemapping and back after (#if UNITY_COLORSPACE_GAMMA), and Bloom.shader squares on the way in and takes the square root on the way out. Grading mode depends only on settings.supportsHDR (UniversalRenderPipeline.CreatePostProcessingData), not on colour space.

What that means in practice:

  • Every authored value above 1 is in gamma units. A stored 2.0 is about 5× in the linear units the tonemapper and bloom work in, so effects look hotter than URP's docs suggest. Tune thresholds and intensities by eye.
  • Blending is still in gamma, as now: the look of every alpha and additive blend stays the same. That is what lets HDR ship without the art re-tune a Linear switch needs.
  • Linear stays available as its own later step (HDR Color Pipeline Wave A). Doing it after HDR means re-tuning HDR values once more; doing it first means re-tuning every colour before getting any HDR payoff.

What happens to the scenario grade

ScenarioGradeView drives one global Volume (Color Adjustments, White Balance, Shadows Midtones Highlights) blended between the sky's and the surface's grade by BackdropMix. Today it runs in LDR grading mode: the LUT grades the clamped 0–1 frame.

With HDR on, URP switches the LUT to HDR grading mode: the frame is encoded in a log curve before the lookup, contrast pivots around mid-grey in a log space, white balance and shadows/midtones/highlights act on the unclamped range, and the tonemapper runs inside the same LUT. The same ScenarioGrade numbers give a different picture, so every grade (sky, meadow, water) needs re-tuning after the switch. Two consequences:

  • Exposure starts to mean something: a negative exposure now pulls highlights back from above 1 instead of greying a clipped white.
  • ScenarioGradeView must stop being the thing that turns post-processing on and off; under HDR post is always on, and the view only drives its Volume.

Tune the grades once, after the HDR decision. Tuning them in LDR now and again in HDR later is double work.

Pixels past 1.02 at half resolution, uncapped → capped, at noon: meadow 134k → 18k, sky 74k → 11k, water 145k → 7k, a pop on the water 207k → 32k; what is left is the emitters, the rainbow balloons' swirl and glints on the water. At night at rest the cap changes nothing (4.5k either way). The orange bands at the top and bottom of the noon frames are the damage vignette, there with bloom off too. Where bloom lives. In the default volume profile, beside the base look, so the launcher and the game share it (decided 2026-11-11). Not on the scenario grade's Volume: it switches itself off with the grade. URP keeps every component in the default profile, so Bloom was already there at intensity 0; Phase 2 only set its values. A device gate, if the measurement needs one, would set the stack's bloom intensity to 0 on weak devices.

The threshold. URP converts the threshold to linear, adds a soft knee of half of it, and in Gamma colour space squares each pixel before comparing (BloomPostProcessPass, Bloom.shader). So bloom picks pixels up from about half the linear threshold:

Threshold What blooms
1.0 12.5% of pure white and about 7% of the 0.92 sky: the whole frame hazes
about 1.37 Nothing at or below 1
1.4 (shipped) Starts at gamma 1.02, full from about 1.77

What ACES meant for this plan (superseded by Neutral)

  • Phase 1 is a look change, not a silent switch. ACES darkens the whole 0–1 range and adds contrast, and it shifts the hue of saturated colours. Expect an exposure lift on the global Volume, and check the balloon palette for identity: gameplay colours must stay easy to tell apart. The Phase 0 baselines are what this is judged against.
  • Grading runs in ACES colour spaces. With ACES selected, URP does every grading operation in ACES spaces, so the scenario grades are re-tuned under ACES, in the same pass as the exposure lift.
  • No extra per-pixel cost against Neutral. In HDR grading mode the tonemapper is baked into the grading LUT, so ACES and Neutral cost the same lookup per pixel.
  • The UI is tonemapped too. The game and launcher canvases are Screen Space – Camera, and score, hearts and heads-ups are World Space, so all of them went through the post pass. This audit first said a separate UI camera could not escape it; that was wrong. URP 17 post-processes per camera in a stack, so an overlay camera with post off draws after the base camera's tonemapping. That is the fix that shipped (Phase 1 item 3b).

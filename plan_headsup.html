Handwritten Heads-Ups

Working plan for short, handwritten heads-ups that tell the player what just happened on the board, written on as if by a pencil, plus pencil lines drawn over the board.

Status: Phase 4 built on 2026-11-10, awaiting the owner's in-editor look: the BalloonParty/Sprite/PencilLine shader (ragged edge, paper grain, wavering pressure, from the heads-ups' pencil noise in world space), the heart trail redrawn as a vermilion pencil that flies to the row lifted and keeps every pass of its scribble until the row pops, and a pencil underline under a streak from x6! (HeadsUpStyleSettings.UnderlinesAt), drawn once the line is written. Phase 3 is done (2026-11-10): HeadsUpQueue, HeadsUpService, the four routers, and UI/HeadsUp/HeadsUpLayer drawing pooled HandwrittenHeadsUp lines, with ReloadHeadsUp and BounceHeadsUp retired into the routers; playtested by the bot, with the lines seen on screen.

Phase 2 built on 2026-11-10: the four signals of §5 (StreakGrewMessage from ScoreController, IActiveProjectilePierce.PierceArmed, ThrowerReadyMessage.AfterCapWait recorded by ThrowerReload.WaitedAtCap, and TrailEndpointKeys.Cannon on the cannon's pivot), each EditMode-tested, plus ProjectileBuffGainedMessage for the rainbow heads-up the owner added in §10; nothing consumes them until the routers of Phase 3. StreakGrewMessage announces streaks from two up, so the streak style's own threshold (§6) picks which ones to write. Two facts for the router: a pop no tracked ball caused (a late pop from a ball already retired) grows the shared streak, so its StreakGrewMessage.Projectile is null and its merge key must allow that; and at a ball cap of 1 the shot fills the only slot, so every ready has AfterCapWait. Phase 1, as built: the Patrick Hand font file (Assets/Fonts/, with its OFL licence), UI/HeadsUp/WriteOnText (the slanted soft wipe on the stock shader, EditMode-tested reveal maths), TooltipBanner writing through it, the TMP font asset, and two early heads-ups written through plain HandwrittenHeadsUp lines, ahead of the router and queue of Phase 3: ReloadHeadsUp writes ready beside the cannon for as long as it's ready, and BounceHeadsUp writes +n bounce where a ball picks up a shield and -1 bounce where a wall spends one. Those lines moved off the canvas into world space on 2026-11-09 (the HeadsUps prefab), as §3 always had it. The pencil text shader (BalloonParty/Text/Pencil: the grain and the ragged edge, on the font's own material) and the pencil tip sprite on every heads-up line were built on 2026-11-10, awaiting the owner's in-editor look; with that, Phase 1 is done. §10's open questions 2-4 matter from Phase 3.

1. Why

The fast-paced mode (PLAN-FastPaced.md) made the board busy: several balls in flight, lines landing on a clock, items going off. Playtests found the player can't read what is happening:

What happens How the game says it today The problem
The cannon has reloaded The held ball slides to the muzzle; a reload sound Easy to miss with several balls out; you can't tell it's ready without looking at the cannon
A colour streak grows A small x3 held at that colour's progress bar (UI/Score/ProgressNotice) It's at the bar, far from the pop that earned it
A shot earns a speed tap off a wall, or starts piercing The shot dips and winds up faster; a rising note No text; the player doesn't learn the rule
A blocked spawn line costs a heart (strikethrough) A heart trail flies to the row, scribbles across it, then the row pops (UI/Health/HeartTrailController) Reads as an effect, not as "you lost a heart because this row had no room"
A level-up passes in play A Level N line in the top tooltip (UI/Tooltip) Fine, but a different look from everything else

A single heads-up language fixes all of them: one or two handwritten words, written on where the thing happened, gone a second later. It fits the game's paper look, and a pencil writing on reads as "the game is telling you something", not as one more effect.

2. What the player sees

  • Heads-Ups. A word or two in a handwriting font, written on left to right, with a small pencil tip moving along the line and a soft scratch sound. It holds briefly, then fades. Examples: x4! at the pop, tap! at the wall, pierce! when a shot starts piercing, ready by the cannon, Level 5: Quick Hands at the top.
  • Pencil lines. The strikethrough's scribble across a blocked row becomes a pencil stroke, and a heads-up that matters (a big streak) gets a pencil underline.

Heads-Ups are short, never cover the aim line for long, and run on unscaled time so they keep writing through slow-motion beats; under the pause menu they freeze with the game (§10).

3. Architecture

Same shape as the audio routers (Audio/Routing/*SoundRouter): small plain C# routers turn gameplay messages into heads-up requests, and one service does the rest, the way the sound routers all call one ISoundPlayer. No presentation message bus. One router for every event would subscribe to about 17 sources, so the rules are split by what they're about.

Piece Layer What it does
CombatHeadsUpRouter, BounceHeadsUpRouter, CannonHeadsUpRouter, ProgressHeadsUpRouter Controllers, Game/HeadsUp/Routing/ Entry points. Each turns its signals into HeadsUpRequests and hands them to IHeadsUps; they know no thresholds, looks or limits. Tested with IHeadsUps substituted
IHeadsUps / HeadsUpService Controller, Game/HeadsUp/ Request(request) returns a HeadsUpHandle; Release(handle) ends a held one. Owns the queue, calls the presenter, clears everything on RunResetMessage and navigation to the launch screen (at once; the level-up and game-over screens follow a fading clear of their own, which an instant one would cut short) and on game over and the level-up popup opening, LevelUpPhase.Pending (animated; not at Completing, the winning pop's slow-motion beat, when the last lines matter most). Takes no requests outside NavigationState.Game. Ticks the presenter on unscaled time, but hands it zero "live" time while PauseService.IsAnyPaused, so heads-ups freeze under a pause and keep going through slow-motion (§10)
HeadsUpQueue Model, Game/HeadsUp/ Owned by the service, plain C#. Applies each style's argument range, then a merge key (a showing line with the same style and key is rewritten), then the style's cooldown, then the cap: MaxConcurrent slots, a full queue evicting its lowest-priority, oldest line for an equal or higher request and dropping a lower one. Held styles (ready) sit in their own lane outside the cap. Every handle carries a generation, so a late "finished" from a reused line can't free its new occupant
HeadsUpRequest Data, Game/HeadsUp/ A struct: a HeadsUpStyleId, an int argument, a HeadsUpAnchor (a world point, or a TrailEndpointRegistry key), an optional merge key (an owner object and a channel string) and an optional word. No string is built per request: the view formats with TMP_Text.SetText(format, arg)
IHeadsUpPresenter Game/HeadsUp/ Show, Rewrite, Release, ClearAll(animated), Advance(liveDelta, unscaledDelta) and a Finished stream. Implemented by HeadsUpLayer
HeadsUpLayer View, UI/HeadsUp/ Registered with RegisterComponentInHierarchy<HeadsUpLayer>().As<IHeadsUpPresenter>(). Takes pooled HandwrittenHeadsUp lines, resolves anchors (an endpoint line follows its anchor, re-read every frame, since the heart and the bars are on the camera-bound canvas), packs new lines clear of the ones showing (HeadsUpPacking, across every line) and reports each line that finished
HandwrittenHeadsUp View, UI/HeadsUp/ One pooled line: a world-space TextMeshPro with WriteOnText and the pencil tip, hand-ticked through write, hold and fade by the layer rather than by DOTween, so a pause freezes it
IHeadsUpSettings Configuration/HeadsUp/ MaxConcurrent and one entry per HeadsUpStyleId: format, look (HeadsUpStyle: colour, size, growth, weight), priority, cooldown, hold or held, offset from the anchor, argument range, and the argument the size starts growing from. The pencil itself (halo, write speed) stays on the line prefab. Added to the CLAUDE.md list of config interfaces

"At the ball" means where the ball was when it happened: a line moving at ball speed can't be read while it writes, or packed. A router that needs the ball's place reads it from IProjectileRegistry and skips the heads-up for a ball no longer in flight.

The early heads-ups fold in. BounceHeadsUp and ReloadHeadsUp (Phase 1) become BounceHeadsUpRouter and CannonHeadsUpRouter behind tests of what they do today, and the two components go in the same change. Left apart, they would break the cap, the pause freeze and the one home for looks.

The top banner stays outside the queue. TooltipBanner keeps its own replace-on-new behaviour and its own handling of BonusCardDealtMessage (held until play resumes, since the card is dealt behind the game-over screen). Under the queue a level-up line could be dropped in a burst. Phase 1 only gives its label the write-on reveal and the handwriting font. Its one TooltipMessage publisher stays LevelUpPopUp.

Board heads-ups live in world space. A canvas position converted once (RectAnchorMath) wouldn't follow the camera rig's zoom and shake, and the pencil strokes are world-space trails anyway. Only the top banner stays on the UI canvas.

Clearing. All heads-ups clear on RunResetMessage, game over and the level-up popup opening (not the winning pop's slow-motion beat before it), and none start off the game screen, the way ProgressNoticePresenter dismisses its notices (animated when there's time, instant otherwise).

4. Rendering

4.1 Write-on text

DECISION (owner): a ragged pencil wipe. Of four styles demoed (a plain letter wipe, a ragged wipe, tracing the outline then filling, true stroke order with a single-line font), the owner picked the ragged wipe: a left-to-right wipe whose edge is soft, grainy and slightly slanted, with a pencil grain through the letters. Real stroke order would need stroke data for every glyph (or a thin, plotter-like single-line font) and isn't worth it: at game speed (about 0.25-0.35 s a word) a good wipe reads as writing.

How it's built:

  • TMP lays the text out once. Each reveal frame sets the vertex alpha of the glyph being written, from its left edge to its right (a 4-vertex gradient), with every earlier glyph opaque and every later one hidden, then pushes colours only (UpdateVertexData(TMP_VertexDataUpdateFlags.Colors32)). A few hundred colour writes a frame: cheap for several heads-ups at once.
  • A pooled heads-up is set up with SetText, ForceMeshUpdate() and every glyph hidden in the same call, or it flashes fully drawn for a frame. Not maxVisibleCharacters: it re-lays out the text on every step.
  • The tick follows UI/Score/ProgressNotice: hand-ticked appear, hold and exit on unscaled time, writing nothing once settled.
  • The pencil tip (a small sprite) follows the write head on the baseline, with a little wobble.
  • The ragged edge and the grain need a small custom text shader: a copy of TMP's SDF shader with a noise texture that roughens the wipe's edge and multiplies a grain into the face. The plain wipe above is its first step and can ship on the stock shader if the custom one takes longer.
  • The top banner is UGUI. A RectMask2D whose right padding animates, with softness, is a cheaper wipe there (no mesh pushes, joined-up script stays continuous), but its edge is straight. Use it only if the banner can't take the custom shader.
  • Handwriting fonts overlap their joining strokes, so a per-glyph step can show seams. Check in the playtest; the font choice (§10) matters here.

4.2 Pencil lines

No new stroke system. Two existing pens already leave a line behind them:

  • The strikethrough is already a pooled FlyingTrail ribbon that flies to the blocked row and scribbles across it in jittered passes (HeartTrailController, StrikethroughPasses, StrikethroughJitter), then pops the row. It gets a pencil material and a pencil-tip head. It owns the row geometry and the timing, so nothing else needs to know where the row is.
  • The underline is the heads-up line's own: an Underline child (a LineRenderer in the line's space) that the line draws under its ink once it's written, and fades with it. Planned as the item preview's pen (Item/Preview/HighlightTrail), but a pen records its ribbon in world space, so an underline would slide off a line that follows its endpoint, and it would need a pool and a ticker of its own for a two-point line.

Both draw through one shader, BalloonParty/Sprite/PencilLine, which takes the heads-ups' pencil noise in world space.

5. New signals

The heads-ups need four facts the game doesn't publish yet. Each has one owner that already holds the data, and each gets EditMode tests.

Signal Owner Why the existing one isn't enough
StreakGrewMessage(Projectile, Color, Streak, WorldPosition) ScoreController.PublishAttributionGroup, when the ball's streak goes up ScorePointsGroupMessage.Multiplier isn't the streak: night doubles it, item pops repeat it without growth, it goes quiet once a bar is full, and a multi-colour pop sends one per colour. StreakChangedMessage has no position
IActiveProjectilePierce.PierceArmed (an IObservable<IProjectileModel>) ActiveProjectilePierce, on each ball's IsPiercing going false to true It only exposes "any ball piercing", which stays true when a second ball arms. Counting taps double-fires after a Snipe re-arm and misses a Snipe arm with no tap
ThrowerReadyMessage.AfterCapWait ThrowerController, set when the thrower had no free slot at any tick since the last ready Working it out in the router would copy the thrower's slot rule and its resets (level-up, un-fire, a cap card)
ProjectileBuffGainedMessage(Projectile, BuffId) ProjectileBuffService.Apply, when a ball takes on a kind of buff it didn't hold Nothing announces a buff. Asking HasBuff each frame would poll, and a Snipe re-applying the rainbow to a ball that has it must stay quiet

The cannon gets a TrailEndpointRegistry key, so the ready heads-up anchors there.

6. What gets a heads-up

Event Signal Heads-Up Rule
Streak grows StreakGrewMessage x{0}! at the pop From a configured streak up (say 3). Merge key (ball, colour), so a growing streak rewrites itself and two balls don't overwrite each other. An underline from a higher streak (say 6)
Speed tap SpeedTapMintedMessage tap! at the wall contact TotalTaps <= N only, so a long cruise doesn't spam
Pierce PierceArmed pierce! at the ball Once per arming, a Snipe arming included (§10)
Cannon ready ThrowerReadyMessage ready, beside the cannon, held while it's ready Built early, on every ready (now CannonHeadsUpRouter), cleared when the ball fires or play stops, and the reload is now near-instant with nothing flying but 2 s while a ball flies, so a ready cannon under fire is a beat worth marking. If it reads as noise, narrow it to AfterCapWait
Shield picked up ShieldGainedMessage +{n} bounce at the shield's balloon, n counting the ball's pickups this flight Built early (now BounceHeadsUpRouter), on every pickup
Wall spends a shield WallHitMessage -1 bounce at the wall contact, kept on screen Built early (now BounceHeadsUpRouter), on every bounce; quiet graphite, vermilion when it leaves the last shield
Ricochet DeflectChainMessage x{n} ricochet at the deflecting balloon Built early (now BounceHeadsUpRouter), from the second deflect in a row without a wall; every third pays +1 bounce (DeflectChainController)
Heart lost to a strikethrough StrikethroughArrivedMessage -1 at the heart (TrailEndpointKeys.Heart) Every time
Pierce discharge PierceDischargedMessage x{n} shattered at the wall contact (Position) From 2 toughs (ToughCount): what the pierce paid, closing the loop pierce! opened
A colour bar fills ProgressBarCompletedMessage {colour} done at that colour's bar (its TrailEndpointRegistry endpoint, keyed by colour name) Every time: the colour scores nothing from here, which is why its streak goes quiet
Rainbow picked up ProjectileBuffGainedMessage with RainbowShield any colour! at the ball Once per pickup; a Snipe re-applying it is not a pickup
Level-up, bonus card TooltipMessage, BonusCardDealtMessage Top banner, as today Unchanged, outside the queue

Each row is a style in IHeadsUpSettings, so thresholds and wording are tuned without code.

The bar's x3 stays. It shows the current streak, held until it changes; the pop heads-up marks the moment it grew. They don't say the same thing.

8. Tests

  • The new signals (§5): StreakGrewMessage only on growth, not on night doubling or item pops; PierceArmed once per arming, including a second ball arming while the first pierces; AfterCapWait only after a capped wait (ThrowerControllerRapidFireTests).
  • HeadsUpRouter: each rule in §6, with the presenter substituted.
  • HeadsUpQueue: the cap, the drop order, merge rewrites, cooldowns, freeing a slot on finish.
  • The views (reveal, tip, pencil trails) are playtest-only.

9. Risks

Risk Mitigation
Heads-Ups become noise on a busy board The queue's cap and cooldowns, thresholds in config, short words only. Playtest with rapid fire at cap 3
They cover the aim line Anchors offset away from the cannon's aim cone; heads-ups are short-lived
Cost on the pop hot path The router allocates nothing per pop (an int argument, no strings); mesh pushes only during the reveal; pooled views. The optimizer reviews each phase
A canvas heads-up re-batches the whole canvas Board heads-ups are world space; the banner sits on its own nested canvas, with no layout groups and raycasts off
The font lacks a glyph TMP fallback font; keep heads-up wording plain ASCII
Seams in joined-up script during the wipe Pick the font with the wipe in mind (the soft edge of the ragged wipe also hides small seams)
The custom shader is fiddly (TMP's SDF shader is large) Phase 1 ships the plain wipe on the stock shader first; the shader is a drop-in material swap after

10. Open questions

  1. Font. DECISION (owner): Patrick Hand (SIL Open Font License, Google Fonts). Picked over Caveat, Gochi Hand and Kalam: it reads best at phone sizes, and its letters don't join, so the wipe shows no seams. Ships with its OFL licence text beside the .ttf.
  2. Pierce from a Snipe. DECISION (owner): yes. A Snipe arming a ball writes pierce! like a tap arming does. A banked charge keeping a ball armed through its discharge is not a new arming, so it writes nothing (PierceArmed doesn't fire for it).
  3. Under the pause menu. DECISION (owner): they freeze with the game. A heads-up mid-write, holding or fading stops while any pause source is held (PauseService.IsAnyPaused) and carries on where it was. Unscaled time still applies to slow-motion beats, which aren't a pause.
  4. More events. DECISION (owner): three more (§6): x{n} shattered on a pierce discharge, {colour} done when a colour bar fills, and any colour! when a ball picks up the rainbow. Left out: an item going off (its effect is already the loudest thing on screen), the next line about to land (the danger heart already counts it down as -N), and coins (they fly to their counter).

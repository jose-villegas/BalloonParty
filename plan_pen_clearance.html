Pen Clearance

Every ball in flight carries a constant clear circle. Score shapes slide around it, score trails bend round its rim, and the effects that draw over the ball (puff clouds, laser, lightning, the bomb burst, paint blobs) thin out inside it, so the player can always see what their ball is doing, however busy the board gets.

Dropped (2026-11-16). Phases 1 to 3 were built and played on the phone, and the owner found they didn't help at all: a circle a little wider than the ball moved too little and faded too little to keep the ball readable. The code was reverted. The ball now draws above every effect instead (see "Draw order" in Projectile/README.md). This page stays as the record of the idea.

Goals

The owner's idea, in their words: "for the view to be able to visibilise what is happening with each pen, we have circle packing already, having a constant sphere around each pen could make other circle orbit around it so this part of screen stays easier to see, trails could avoid this area as a side effect as well, while other effects could be smooth blended with alpha so they don't fully cover what is happening around the pen, things like lighting, smoke from bombs, paint, etc."

  1. A clearance circle per ball. Each ball in flight carries a circle of one fixed world radius. It is the one shared fact every consumer below reads; none of them looks at the balls itself.
  2. Score shapes orbit it. The BigScore formations already push apart from each other (FormationOverlapResolver). The circles join that packing as fixed obstacles, so a shape that blooms on the ball, or that a ball flies into, slides aside and around instead of sitting over it.
  3. Trails bend round it. A score trail whose head enters a circle is carried along its rim, so the ribbon it leaves curves round the ball instead of crossing it.
  4. Effects thin out inside it. Every effect that draws over the ball fades towards a floor alpha inside the circle, with a smooth edge. It stays visible, so nothing the player needs to know disappears, but it no longer buries the ball.

Non-goals: no change to gameplay, hits, or what any effect does. Nothing here changes a balloon's own colour or paint state: only effects drawn over the board fade.

What a "pen" is

A pen is a ball in flight (confirmed by the owner, 2026-11-15): the Extra Pen card is "+1 pen in flight" (it raises IWeaponStats.MaxBallsInFlight), and Thick Skin is "+1 shield on every pen". The word also names two drawing tools in the code, the trail heads that trace score shapes (ShapeFormationTicker) and item range figures (Item/Preview/); this plan calls those formation pens and range pens where it must name them.

The ball held in the thrower gets no circle: it sits below the board, where nothing draws over it.

What can cover a ball

The game draws with the URP 2D renderer, by sorting layer, bottom to top: Default < Balloons < Projectile < PowerUps < GridActors < ScoreTrail < Sky < UI. The ball is on Projectile, so only what draws on PowerUps or above can cover it. That settles which effects matter:

Effect Layer Covers the ball? Size
Puff clouds (Grid/PuffCloud.shader) GridActors Yes, and the ball flies through them Several slots
Laser (Particle/Glow.shader, Sprite/SpriteShine.shader) PowerUps Yes A board-wide cross up to 3 units thick
Lightning (Particle/Glow.shader on LineRenderers) PowerUps Yes Thin bolts across the board
Bomb burst (PSVFX_BombRange, built-in particle shader) PowerUps Yes 2.5 to 4 units across
Paint blobs (Paint/PaintBlob.shader) and splash particles (built-in) PowerUps Yes Local
Score trails and formation ink (TrailRenderer, built-in shader) ScoreTrail Yes Thin streaks; formations up to board-wide
Bushes (Grid/BushLeaf.shader, Grid/BushBranch.shader, drawn by Graphics.DrawMesh) none set Unknown: check in the Frame Debugger Several slots
Screen-space light overlay (Display/ScreenSpaceLightOverlay.shader) Sky Tints and darkens, never hides Full screen
Smoke field, paper ink, god rays, the disco wave, paint drips, balloon pops, background Default / Balloons No, drawn under the ball —

So the smoke field and paint drips can't bury the ball: they draw under it (and under the balloons too). What reads as "smoke from bombs" is the bomb's burst particles, PSVFX_BombRange, which are on PowerUps; the bomb doesn't stamp the smoke field at all. "Paint" over the ball is the paint item's flying blobs and splash particles. Those are what this plan fades.

Left alone:

  • The ball's own shield halo (Display/EMShieldField.shader, Projectile layer). It belongs to a ball; another ball's circle would carve a hole in it.
  • Bloom (post-processing) can spill a bright effect's glow over the ball. A per-shader mask can't reach it; fading the effect also fades its bloom, which is most of it.
  • UI text (heads-ups, streak notices, the level-up popup): drawn by TextMesh Pro and UGUI, outside this approach, and short-lived.

Clearance field

Projectile/Clearance/ (new feature folder, with a README):

Type Layer What it does
IClearanceSettings / ClearanceSettings config SO Radius, rim softness, fade in/out, effect floor alpha, formation push cap (table below)
IClearanceField model (read-only) Count, CircleAt(i) → centre (xy), radius, strength 0..1; Deflect(Vector3) for trails
ClearanceField model Fixed-capacity circle slots (8). Pure C#, no Unity objects, EditMode-tested
ClearanceFieldTicker controller ITickable, game scope: reads the registry's balls in flight each frame, writes the field, and pushes it into the shader globals (Shader.SetGlobalVectorArray), as ShaderTimeService and SceneLightFieldService push theirs

A circle's life. A ball that launches gets a slot keyed by its IProjectileModel; its strength eases from 0 to 1 over FadeIn (0.15 s), so a circle never pops in. While the ball flies, the circle follows it. When the ball leaves the registry (it landed or retired), the circle stays where the ball was last seen and eases to 0 over FadeOut (0.4 s): a ball's last pop is where most effects start, and a circle that vanished on the frame the ball did would let them all close over that spot at once. The effective radius is Radius × strength, so formations and trails also let go gently.

Eight slots: the cap is 3 balls, plus up to 3 Extra Pen cards, plus a couple still fading. When all eight are busy, a new ball takes the weakest fading slot.

Formations orbit the circle

ShapeFormationTicker.ResolveOverlaps already gathers every formation occupying space this tick and grows each one's persistent SeparationOffset (see Game/Score/README.md, Overlap Resolution). After the formation-against-formation pass, a second pure pass, FormationOverlapResolver.ResolveAgainstFixed, pushes each moving formation out of every clearance circle:

  • The circles never move for the packing (they follow the balls), so the formation takes the whole push.
  • The push goes into the same SeparationOffset, so the existing relax brings the shape back onto its path to the bar once the ball has passed. That slide out and back is the "orbit".
  • The principal is pushed too. Its PrincipalPushDamping steadies it against other formations, because the level-up camera follows it. A ball matters more, so the clearance pass gives every formation full weight. Once the level-up cinematic takes the principal it is Paused, and paused formations are never moved.
  • A ball flies at about 10 world units a second and a formation's radius is up to about 0.9, so the per-frame cap the formation pass uses (half the formation's radius) would let a ball cross a shape before it got out of the way. The clearance pass has its own cap, FormationPushFraction.
  • A formation still blooming from a point has almost no radius, so the push it can take grows with it: a shape born on the ball grows out to one side of it rather than jumping away.

Trails bend round the rim

The trails that fly a tween or follow path (FlyingTrail.Setup, SetupBurst, SetupFollow) set their position absolutely each frame: DOTween writes Lerp(start, end, ease(t)) in Update, and FlyingTrail.Update does the same for a follow. So the bend can be stateless: in LateUpdate, after the tween has moved it, a flying trail replaces its position with IClearanceField.Deflect(position). The ribbon records the bent position, so the curve round the ball is drawn into the trail itself.

Deflect moves a point inside a circle (or in its soft rim) radially out with a smooth, monotonic falloff, so a trail beside a circle bends gently and one passing through never snaps. Overlapping circles apply in turn.

Which trails bend: the one-per-point score trails, the heart trail (UI/Health/HeartTrailController), and any other FlyingTrail on a tween or follow path. Which don't:

  • Formation pens are placed by ShapeFormationTicker every frame, rigid in the shape's own frame, and never run a tween. The whole formation moves round the circle instead (above); bending each pen would shear the drawn figure.
  • Range pens (Item/Preview/HighlightTrail, a separate class) draw where an item will strike. Bending them would draw the range wrong.

The field reaches each trail through TrailSpawner, which hands it over on spawn. A trail with no field (edit-mode previews, the level-up popup's trails) doesn't bend.

Effects thin out inside the circle

A new shader include, Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/Include/Clearance.cginc (guard BALLOONPARTY_CLEARANCE_INCLUDED, a row in the shader README's include table):

float4 _ClearanceCircles[8]; // xy: world centre, z: radius (already x strength), w: strength
float _ClearanceCount;
float _ClearanceFloor; // alpha left at the centre of a full-strength circle
float _ClearanceSoftness; // rim width as a share of the radius
// 1 outside every circle, falling smoothly to _ClearanceFloor at a circle's centre.
half ClearanceAlpha(float2 worldXY);

The loop runs to _ClearanceCount (usually 1 to 3), and with no ball in flight it costs one compare. Each shader below includes it and multiplies its output by ClearanceAlpha(worldPos.xy): the whole colour for the premultiplied ones, alpha alone for straight alpha.

Shader Effects Work
Grid/PuffCloud.shader Puff clouds World position already in the fragment; fade every return (body and shadow)
Particle/Glow.shader Laser beams, lightning bolts Add a world-position interpolator (mul(unity_ObjectToWorld, v.vertex)); it is world space already for LineRenderers
Sprite/SpriteShine.shader Laser shine and shadow Fade its output; check its other users first, and gate it behind a material toggle if any must not fade
Paint/PaintBlob.shader Paint blobs World position already in the fragment
New Particle/ParticleClearance.shader Bomb burst, paint splash and drip particles, score trail ribbons These use Unity's built-in legacy particle shader, which can't take the include. A small replacement that matches its look (texture × vertex colour, alpha blended) and fades; swapped onto their materials
Grid/BushLeaf.shader, Grid/BushBranch.shader Bushes Only if the Frame Debugger shows them drawing over the ball. The leaf shader needs one interpolator

The floor is an alpha, not zero: a laser over the ball still reads as a laser, a cloud as a cloud, but the ball shows through. 0.3 to start; tuned on the phone.

The score trail ribbons fade as a backstop. The formations and trails above keep most of them off the ball. Where one still crosses it (a formation's ink while it is pushed off, a trail caught when a circle sweeps onto it), the ribbon thins out rather than crossing over.

Settings

Setting Default Why
Radius 0.6 world units The ball's sprite is about 0.54 long, so 0.6 covers it and the balloon or two either side that it's about to hit. The board is 5.2 wide
RimSoftness 0.4 (share of radius) Width of the smooth edge, for the shaders and Deflect alike
FadeIn 0.15 s No circle pops in at launch
FadeOut 0.4 s The last pop's site stays clear while its effects start
EffectFloorAlpha 0.3 How much of an effect is left at the ball
FormationPushFraction 1.5 Per-frame push cap for formations, as a share of their radius: fast enough to clear a ball at 10 u/s
PushFormations / BendTrails / FadeEffects on Each consumer can be switched off on its own, to compare in a playtest (FadeEffects off sets the floor to 1)

ClearanceSettings is a new SO under Assets/Configuration/, bound as IClearanceSettings in GameLifetimeScope like the other effect settings.

Testing

EditMode (test-everything decides the final list):

  • ClearanceField: a ball launching eases in, a ball landing leaves a fading circle at its last position, the ninth circle takes the weakest fading slot, strength 0 frees a slot.
  • Deflect: a point outside the rim doesn't move, a point inside ends on or beyond the rim, the output is continuous across the rim (no snap), a point exactly on a centre goes somewhere deterministic.
  • FormationOverlapResolver.ResolveAgainstFixed: a formation over a circle is pushed out along the line between the centres, the push is capped per call, a circle of strength 0 pushes nothing.

In the Editor and on the phone: the shaders (dotnet build doesn't compile them), the look of the bend and the orbit, the floor alpha, the replacement particle shader against the built-in one it replaces, and the cost on the phone.

Open questions

  1. Should the circle show? This plan draws nothing for the circle itself: the clearance is felt through what moves and fades around it. A faint ring could come later if the owner wants one.
  2. "Lighting". The owner wrote "lighting". Lightning is covered above. If it meant the light and shadow over the board (the screen-space light overlay, which darkens the ball at night), the overlay could ease towards neutral inside the circle, a soft spotlight that follows each ball. It changes the night's look, so it is left out unless the owner wants it.

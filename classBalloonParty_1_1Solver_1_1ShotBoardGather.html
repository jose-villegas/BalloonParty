staticpackage

The i'th sweep angle over [arcMinDegrees , arcMaxDegrees ], paired with ResolveSweepSampleCount.

Continuous aim (stepDegrees <= 0) lerps evenly across continuousSampleCount samples, exactly as before quantization existed. A quantized aim instead walks the absolute step grid — the same grid ThrowerController.QuantizeAimDirection snaps player input to — rather than lerping and hoping the result coincides with a reachable angle. That grid is anchored at multiples of stepDegrees in absolute terms, not at arcMinDegrees , so the first sample is the smallest step multiple at or above arcMinDegrees — not necessarily arcMinDegrees itself. When the step is wider than the whole arc (no multiple falls inside it), this returns whichever neighbouring grid line sits closest to the arc, so the single guaranteed sample from ResolveSweepSampleCount is still a reachable angle.

staticpackage

How many angles a sweep over [arcMinDegrees , arcMaxDegrees ] needs.

Continuous aim (stepDegrees <= 0) keeps the caller's fixed continuousSampleCount unchanged — today's behaviour. A quantized aim instead counts the step multiples that land inside the arc, so the sweep only ever visits angles ThrowerController.QuantizeAimDirection can actually reach — a ~31° arc at a 5° step is ~7 samples, not thousands. Always at least 1, even when the step is wider than the whole arc.

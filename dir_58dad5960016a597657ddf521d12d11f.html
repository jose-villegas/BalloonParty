Detailed Description

The shared field services and the views that render the play-area frame — the visible boundary the projectile bounces off.

SkyScatterService

SkyScatterService.cs — owns the sky-scatter backdrop: a single stationary quad rendered immediately after the camera's flat clear colour and behind every other backdrop layer (BackgroundCloud, WallNet, SmokeField) — a naive 2D stand-in for atmospheric scattering. Unlike BackgroundFieldService below it bakes nothing: the look (a stack of circles sharing one tangent point toward the scene light, growing outward from the light's own colour to an authored horizon colour, with a slow per-ring wave and cloud-perturbed edges) lives entirely in Shaders/BalloonParty/Display/SkyScatter.shader, driven off uniforms the shader already has for free — SceneLight.cginc's flat globals (_SceneLightDir/ _SceneLightColor/_SceneLightIntensity) and BackgroundField.cginc's shared cloud-density map. The service's only job is building and sizing the quad once: a plain-C# DI singleton (IStartable/ IDisposable, no MonoBehaviour) that builds the quad via the shared Shared/Rendering/ QuadRendererBuilder (also used by ScreenSpaceLightService's GI overlay), assigns the material/sorting layer/order from ISkyScatterSettings, and scales it to IGameDisplayConfiguration. ReferenceWorldWidth/Height × OverscanMultiplier. It stays stationary and generously oversized rather than tracking the camera, so a shake or cinematic pan never reveals an edge — the same trade BackgroundCloud makes. Registered in AppLifetimeScope (the persistent app root, not GameLifetimeScope) so the sky reads correctly from the Launch begin-screen's first frame. Tuning lives on SkyScatterSettings (ISkyScatterSettings: the display material + overscan + sorting layer/order) — the actual ring count/colours/wave/cloud-distortion knobs are all Properties on the material itself, not on the settings SO (mirroring how BackgroundFieldSettings.DensityMaterial owns the cloud roll's tuning).

This is the one exception in Scenario/ to "a `Renderer` is owned by a View, never a plain-C# service": the quad is built once and never touched again (no Tick, no per-frame state), so a MonoBehaviour View would only exist to satisfy RegisterComponentInHierarchy's requirement that the component already sit in a prefab's serialized hierarchy — a materially riskier hand-edit than the single settings-asset field reference this design needs instead. If a future revision gives the quad per-frame behaviour (tracking the camera, reacting to input), split the renderer-owning half into a View at that point rather than growing this service's Unity-API surface further.

The service also implements ISkyBackdrop (SetVisible), so the ground can hide the sky while it fully covers it (see the next section).

Sky and ground scenarios

Each level is set either in the sky (the backdrop above) or on the ground, a meadow the board rests on, so actors that need ground under them (bushes) don't look like they float. Which one is a per-level, seeded choice: ILevelPacingConfiguration.ScenarioForLevel (see Configuration/README.md) is sky until the first ground level, rolled between levels 6 and 8, stays on the ground for a fixed first meadow, then alternates sky and ground stretches of a rolled length. The rolls mix in the run's seed, so each run lays out its own, and a retry keeps the same scenario. A meadow arrives under puff clouds and trades them for bushes as it goes: by the level's place in its stretch, the level generator thins and then drops the board's puff-cloud actors and grows the bushes, so the meadow's clear second half is its bushiest. Sky levels never have bushes: the catalogue flags them RequiresGround, and the generator leaves them out. Plan: Plans/PLAN-TerrainBiomes.md.

  • IActiveScenario / ActiveScenario: the model, the scenario the backdrop is on, as a ReactiveProperty<ScenarioShift>: the kind, and the seconds a mist reveal into it takes (0 for a cut). Written only by Game/Level/ScenarioController, which cuts to the current level's scenario on run start and on an immediate restart, and reveals it over the beat's own length on a level-up Ascent (LevelAscendSettings.AscentSeconds) and on the restart rise after a loss (RestartRiseSeconds, from RestartRiseStartedMessage, once the new scenery is placed). A reveal into the scenario already showing changes nothing.
  • The mist reveal (GroundRevealCurve, pure): sky to ground, a mist rolls in over the sky (IGroundSettings.MistInShare, the first third), then a hole opens from the middle of the view and grows until the meadow fills it. Ground to sky plays the same curve backwards: the meadow closes into the mist, which then clears to the sky. The edge wanders from a circle (RevealEdgeWander), so it opens like parting cloud rather than an iris. The same reveal plays the drop between the layers (GroundRevealCurve.Depth): the ground sits RevealDepthZoom (1.5) times deeper than the sky, and both scale about the camera over the beat, so going down the meadow grows into place while the clouds grow past, and going up the meadow shrinks away while the clouds settle. Whichever layer the beat ends on reaches 1 as it ends, and the one on screen at the start starts at 1, so nothing jumps. GroundRevealPlanner (pure) decides whether a change cuts, reveals or does nothing, from the shift, the navigation and what is showing.
  • View/GroundView.cs: a MonoBehaviour the Game scope creates on a new GameObject (RegisterComponentOnNewGameObject, so no prefab edit), parented under the Game scope's transform: the Game scene is preloaded additively, so an unparented object would land in the Launcher and die with it. It builds one stationary floor quad with QuadRendererBuilder from IGroundSettings (material, overscan, sorting order −15: above the sky at −20, below the clouds at −10) on the Scenario layer, like the sky it replaces, so SceneCaptureService captures the meadow for the GI. Both ground shaders write colour only (ColorMask RGB): the capture's alpha is its sprite-coverage mask, and a full-screen ground writing it would read as one big occluder. The floor draws through its own instance of the material, so a reveal can switch its blend state without touching the asset. Over the quad it builds one mesh of grass blades (GrassBladeMesh, drawn through MeshRendererBuilder at sorting order −14) with its own material. At rest on ground it shows both, hides the sky (ISkyBackdrop, an optional lookup because the sky only registers when its settings are wired) and sets the global _GroundCoverage to 1; on sky it reverses all of it. During a reveal it shows the sky and the ground together, turns on the mist quad, switches the floor to premultiplied blending and pushes the hole and the two layers' scales each frame (_GroundReveal, _GroundRevealShape) on unscaled time, like the beats it rides on, and scales the board's content with them (ScenarioContentRoot.SetDepth: the incoming scenery with the incoming layer, the Outgoing holder with the outgoing one, so the bushes stay planted on their meadow); at the end it cuts to the target and every scale returns to 1. Entering or leaving the Launch begin-screen cuts (it is always sky); other navigation leaves a running reveal alone, since the reveal rides a beat of its own that navigation doesn't end. It also pushes _GroundPatchSize and _GroundDirt, so the floor and the blades read the same patches and dirt. The quad stays put, but the meadow scrolls with ScenarioContentRoot (the global _GroundScroll, the root's travel times the meadow's scale, pushed in LateUpdate after the beat moves the root): the bushes stand on this ground, so on the Ascent and the restart rise the whole meadow travels in with them. A still ground would show them sliding over it. The clouds scroll with the root too, but at a parallax, because they are far away. On destroy it hands the App-owned sky and clouds their sky look back and frees the blade mesh and the floor's material instance (the floor's quad mesh is shared, so it stays).
  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/GroundMist.shader: the reveal's mist, on a second overscanned quad that GroundView turns on only while a reveal runs, at sorting order −9 (over the cloud backdrop, under the paint and the board) on the Default layer, which SceneCaptureService leaves out, so the GI never reads it as a bright bounce. Thick outside the hole, it thins inward over MistReach (1.6 world units), frayed by two drifting taps of the shared cloud noise, and it is never thinner than the sky showing through the ground's soft edge, so no seam shows. At its thickest it is still see-through (MistOpacity, 0.6), so the sky shows faintly behind it. Lit by the scene light, so it dims at night.
  • View/GrassBladeMesh.cs: builds the blade mesh once, from a GrassBladeLayout (tile size, spacing, seed). A jittered grid over the camera's view plus a margin (BladeTileMargin); each blade is a tapered five-vertex strip whose vertices all sit on its root, with per-blade randoms for lean, length, shade and sway phase. A jittered grid repeats without a seam, so the shader can wrap the roots across the tile as the meadow scrolls. Vertices are packed to 12 bytes (root UNorm16, corner SNorm8, randoms UNorm8) and the blade count is capped so 16-bit indices always fit: about 9,000 blades (45k vertices) in one draw on a 9:16 phone at the default spacing.
  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/GrassBlades.shader: places, bends and wraps every blade in the vertex stage; the fragment stage only writes the interpolated colour. Seen from above, a standing blade shows a short stroke leaning one way, swaying in gust waves that roll across the meadow. Each blade reads the grass wake once at its root (_GrassWakeTex, below): where a ball, a pop, a bomb or a laser stamped the disturbance field, the blade turns toward the push and lies down, so it shows its full length, curving from its root, and its tip catches the sun. The response saturates (_WakeBend, "Wake Response"), so a blade answers the first light touch of the wake instead of waiting for the push to build. One patch-noise tap per blade turns the tips drier on the floor's lighter patches; a second, the dirt mask, dries, shortens and drops out blades across a dirt patch's fade (each at its own point, so the grass frays into the dirt). Lit by the scene-light field at its root (SceneLightTintAtLOD), like the sprites' VTF light: the time of day, plus the local light of pops and items.
  • View/GrassWakeBuffer.cs + Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/GrassWake.shader: the grass's own short memory of the disturbance field (Shared/Disturbance/README.md), owned by GroundView and stepped in its LateUpdate while the ground shows. The shared field holds a push for seconds and spreads it, which suits the clouds' slow drift but made the grass feel sluggish. The wake keeps only what the field gained since last frame and lets it go on its own recovery (IGroundSettings.BladeRecoverySeconds, 0.35 s), so the blades snap down as the ball passes and stand back up quickly behind it. Field-sized, in the field's UV space, half-float (the push is signed): one small blit and one copy a frame. Restarted each time the ground appears, so a push the field still holds doesn't land on the blades at once.
  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/Ground.shader: the floor under the blades, five taps per pixel: soft darker and lighter patches from the shared tileable cloud noise, and a few large bare-dirt clusters (a coarse tap for the cluster, a finer one to fray its outline) that fade into the grass, all over granular microdetail (Include/FloorDetail.cginc, two taps) that the scene light rakes and a few grains glint in. Muted mid-tones, so the saturated balloons stand off the ground and the scene light carries the time of day across it; lit per pixel by the scene-light field (SceneLightTintAt), so pops and items light it locally too. It writes premultiplied colour times the coverage at the pixel: opaque at rest (coverage 1), blended over the sky during a reveal. The blades shorten by the same coverage at their root, so outside the hole they collapse and draw nothing, and the opaque blades need no blending.
  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Include/Ground.cginc: the ground's shared globals, all pushed by GroundView: coverage, scroll, patch size and the dirt settings (_GroundDirt), with GroundPatchUV, GroundDirtUV and GroundDirtMask so the floor and the blades read the same patches. GroundCoverageAt(worldPos) is _GroundCoverage at rest; while a reveal runs (a uniform branch, so rest pays almost nothing) it is the hole: 1 inside, 0 outside, soft over RevealEdgeSoftness, from GroundRevealDistance, a signed distance from the hole's wandering edge built from two sine waves, with no texture so every reader can afford it. Every reader follows the hole during a reveal with no change of its own. GroundRevealGroundScale and GroundRevealSkyScale give the two layers' scales (1 at rest), and GroundPointAt the ground point a world position shows through the meadow's scroll and scale. The floor and the blades read their patches, dirt and gusts through it; the blades keep their world spacing so the tile still covers the view and only shorten with the scale. BackgroundCloud samples its cloud bodies at the sky's scale, which is never below 1, so the taps stay inside the view-sized cloud field; the shadows it casts on the meadow stay unscaled for the same reason. The puff clouds (_RestScale) and the bushes (their offset matrix) follow the content root's scale. BackgroundCloud multiplies its cloud bodies by 1 − coverage × _GroundBodyFade and keeps the drop shadows, so on the ground the clouds read as overhead and out of frame, their shadows drifting over the grass. With the bodies fully faded it takes a shadow-only branch that skips the body's lighting. SpriteLightDriven and PaintBlob also read it: their drop shadows normally fade where there is no cloud or smoke to land on (_CLOUD_FADE_ON), and on the ground they land on the grass instead, so the balloon, projectile and paint shadows stay. The GI smear (ScreenSpaceLightSmear) gates its shadow the same way.

Water

The third scenario: after the first meadow, each surface stretch is a meadow or water by a coin flip (ScenarioKind.Water). Water levels have no puff clouds or bushes, and nothing on them bounces shots but the unbreakable balloon; the longer a water level runs, the more of each spawn line is bubble clusters (Balloon/Spawner/BalloonSpawner). Plan: Plans/PLAN-WaterScenario.md.

Water is a surface like the meadow, so everything above about reveals, depth, coverage and scrolling applies to it unchanged; the planner and the curve treat any kind but sky as a surface. Sky always sits between two surfaces, so a reveal never goes from meadow to water. GroundView draws water on the same floor quad through its own instance of IGroundSettings.WaterMaterial (no blades, no grass wake), and swaps the GI's bounce reference to WaterBounceReference.

  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/Water.shader: one pass, drawn as a body of water over a sandy bottom. The bottom's depth drifts across the level from the shared noise, from sandbanks to deep channels, blurred over a few world units (_DepthBlur) so the banks fade into the channels with no edge between them. It is seen as from a camera above the middle of the screen: a deeper bottom shrinks toward the middle and slides slower when the board scrolls, and the surface's slope bends the view of it more over deep water. The sand carries ripple bands, lit grains (Include/FloorDetail.cginc, read nearly unbent by the surface: bent fully, grains this fine smear into contour lines) and the caustic net (below), shaded around the sand's own colour, never added past it. The water over it absorbs each colour by its own rate per world unit (red first, blue last) and scatters its own colour back, so shallows read as sand and deep water as teal. Each simulated wave throws a bright band and a dark band on the sand (light focused under its trough, spread under its crest) and sheens the surface, whatever the sun's angle; crests read a little lighter and troughs darker. On top, slopes facing the sun catch a soft highlight and crests turned just so between the sun and the eye glint, and the simulation's foam is drawn as a lace of noise that it fills more of the thicker it is, so fading foam breaks into islands and specks. Lit by the scene-light field at 0.7 influence (a warm evening tints the water rather than turning it olive), with light above full eased in so a pop's glow doesn't clip to white; premultiplied by GroundCoverageAt, like the floor.
  • Shaders/BalloonParty/Include/WaterSurface.cginc: the surface's slope: a slow swell of two noise layers on different headings (the smaller slower, as shorter waves are), travelling with the board, plus the simulated waves (below). The slope is what bends the bottom and catches the light.
  • View/WaterRippleBuffer.cs + Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/WaterRipple.shader: the water's motion, a shallow-water simulation on the GPU over the disturbance field's bounds (IGroundSettings. WaterRippleTexelsPerUnit, 16; about 86×154 cells on a phone), published as _WaterRippleTex (r = height above rest, g/b = the flow across each cell's right and top edges, a = foam). Each fixed step (120 a second of game time, at most 4 a frame, so a pause holds it) flow runs from high water to low and the heights follow, so a dip travels out as a wave. The screen's edges are walls: waves reflect and the water sloshes between them. Surface tension pulls on curvature, so short ripples outrun long ones and a splash spreads into a train of wavelets; viscosity smooths the flow at the grid's own scale. A crest steeper than the break slope loses some of its flow. Foam shows only where a crest (past the foam height) folds over sharply in its sharpest direction (past the foam fold), where the normal turns suddenly, and lasts a tenth of a second, so it sits on the fold and moves with the crest. Every impact the game reports to Shared/ImpactEventBus (each step of a ball's flight, pops, bombs, balloons gliding in) presses the water for 0.12 s, eased in and out, and drags it along the impact's heading (Impact.Direction), so a ball leaves a V-shaped wake. The presses are splatted as soft quads into a force target once a frame (pass 0), then the steps run (pass 1). Both cells on an edge compute its flow from the same texels, so no water appears or leaves. It restarts calm whenever water appears. WaterImpactCollector (Game scope, IPostTickable) copies the bus after Update, because the bus clears in VContainer's LateTick, which runs before GroundView's LateUpdate. The tuning (wave speed, tension, fade, push, drag, break slope, foam) is on the ripple material. Three earlier versions failed. Rings drawn straight from the field's push (sin(k·push + ωt)) stayed in the push's shape and wobbled in place like gel. A wave equation over the height alone, driven by the field, sloshed in board-wide blobs, and driven by the impacts its rings interfered into a chop. Rings drawn one by one from each impact travelled but never met anything: no flow, tension or resonance, no height or breakup (owner).
  • View/WaterCausticBuffer.cs + Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/WaterCaustics.shader: the caustics. Each frame on water, one blit draws a tile of the moving caustic net into a 256² R8 target that wraps, with mips, published as _WaterCausticTex. The net is the borders between drifting cells, warped so they curve. The water shader lays the tile twice, at two scales and angles (the second varies the first's brightness along its lines, so no tiling shows), shifts it away from the sun, splits it into colour fringes along the sun and blurs it where the water is deep. The tile is not tied to the view, so it costs the same on any screen.

Shader changes are checked in the editor only; dotnet build doesn't compile them.

BackgroundFieldService

BackgroundFieldService.cs — owns the shared cloud field: an RT (_BackgroundDensityTex) blitted from the BackgroundFieldDensity material on a cadence timer rather than every frame (ICadencedEffect, weight 1 — see Shared/Cadence/README.md), with the disturbance field baked in so clouds part around a wall hit or a pop. The cadence gate is bypassed while the scenario root is moving (launch ascend, level-up, game-over descent), so the density texture never visibly lags the scrolling camera during a transition. It publishes the RT plus its world bounds as global shader properties, so every consumer reads the SAME clouds with a single tap rather than each running its own noise. It is the ONE cloud generator in the project; consumers only sample, never roll their own — currently the BackgroundCloud backdrop, sprite drop-shadows (SpriteLightDriven), the GI light smear (ScreenSpaceLightSmear), and the wall net below.

It is a plain-C# DI singleton — IStartable/ITickable/IDisposable, no MonoBehaviour and no scene GameObject — exactly like its siblings DisturbanceFieldService / SceneLightFieldService. Its bounds come from IGameDisplayConfiguration via the shared DisturbanceFieldCoordinates (origin-centered, so it aligns with the disturbance/light fields and needs no camera). It is registered in AppLifetimeScope — the persistent app root, not the per-run GameLifetimeScope — so it exists before either scene loads, which is what makes the launch begin-screen's clouds live from the first frame rather than depending on the Game scene's preload. Its tuning lives on BackgroundFieldSettings (IBackgroundFieldSettings: the density blit material + resolution + transition parallax). The GPU-side tap is Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/Include/BackgroundField.cginc; generation is BackgroundFieldGen.cginc + the BackgroundFieldDensity blit shader. See Shared/Disturbance/README.md.

SmokeFieldService

SmokeFieldService.cs — the smoke trail field: a persistent screen-space RT that accumulates palette-colored capsule stamps and decays them into animated smoke wisps. The 4th field service alongside Disturbance, SceneLight, and Background.

Profile-based stamping: every stamp originates from a PaintSource (ProjectileTrail, ToughPop, ToughBreathing, ToughDeflect). The service resolves the source to a PaintProfile (via ISmokeFieldSettings.GetProfile) that defines radius, opacity, and how color is chosen (PaintColorMode.Dynamic — caller supplies a palette index, Palette — profile names a fixed palette entry, Custom — profile carries a fixed RGB color). The public API is:

  • Paint(PaintSource, Vector3, int paletteIndex = -1) — single stamp.
  • PaintScatter(PaintSource, Vector3, int count, float scatterRadius, int paletteIndex = -1) — multiple stamps scattered in a random ring.

Pipeline: each frame the projectile is moving, ProjectileView calls Paint with PaintSource.ProjectileTrail (capsule from previous → current position). The service batches up to 32 stamps per blit pass into SmokeFieldStamp.shader, then runs a decay pass (SmokeFieldDecay.shader) that advects, expands, erodes, and fades the accumulated paint. The result is published as the global _SmokeTex, sampled by the display shader.

Decay model: wind advection (swinging direction via sin oscillation), turbulent perturbation, radius expansion, noise erosion, and linear alpha decay — all driven by _SmokeTime (which respects TimeScaleService pause/slow-mo). Fresh stamps resist wind (age-based bias); fast projectile stamps get dampened wind so they don't smear.

Lifecycle: clears the RT on LevelUpDismissedMessage (new level = blank canvas) and on GameOverMessage (descend/game-over = blank canvas).

SmokeFieldResources

SmokeFieldResources.cs — owns the GPU resources: two ARGBHalf ping-pong RTs and the stamp + decay materials (instantiated from the shaders on ISmokeFieldSettings). Handles blit-and-swap; separated from the service so resource lifecycle is testable independently.

SmokeFieldView

View/SmokeFieldView.cs — a MonoBehaviour that renders the smoke field RT as visible smoke behind the cloud backdrop. Builds a viewport-sized quad at startup, assigns the SmokeTrailDisplay material, and sits on the Default sorting layer at order −1 (behind the clouds at 0). Registered via RegisterComponentInHierarchy<SmokeFieldView>().

Shaders

Shader Role
SmokeFieldStamp Capsule SDF batched blit (32 stamps/pass)
SmokeFieldDecay Smoke dispersion: wind + turbulence + expansion + erosion + decay
SmokeTrailDisplay Display: curl-noise swirl, 5-tap bleed, sigmoid edges, wisp noise, paper grain, sky transmission, shadow lift, scene-light integration
SmokeField.cginc Consumer include — exposes global _SmokeTex

Configuration

ISmokeFieldSettings / SmokeFieldSettings SO — stamps shader, decay shader, texels-per-unit, decay rate, decay tick interval, wind speed/influence/age-bias/direction/swing, and a PaintProfile[] array (each entry maps PaintSource flags → radius, opacity, PaintColorMode, palette name / custom color). GetProfile(PaintSource) resolves the active profile for a given source.

WallNetView

View/WallNetView.cs — a MonoBehaviour that builds the four net-strip meshes framing the play area, one per edge of the logical play rectangle. It reads that rectangle from the injected IProjectileFlightConfig.LimitsClockwise (unpacked via WallLimits), so the visible frame is sourced from the same rectangle the projectile billiard reflects off — no hand-syncing a separate visual frame.

Each strip is a flat quad band laid outward from its wall by _stripWidth (away from the play area, so its reveal never covers the balloons), tessellated into a quad grid (_cellsPerUnitAlong × _cellsAcross) fine enough to deform smoothly. The meshes are built once in Start and never rewritten from C#: all motion lives in the shared net material's vertex stage, so there is zero per-frame CPU. Per vertex the view bakes:

  • normal = the edge's outward direction, so the shader can un-extrude each strip to a thin line at rest and billow it back out along that same direction.
  • UV0 = (across 0→1, along 0→1) — normalised, for the band-edge feather and the unfurl/billow maths.
  • UV1 = the net-pattern tiling (cells kept square: the across cell size sets the along tiling).

The strips render through generated child MeshRenderers sharing _netMaterial, on the configured sorting layer. Registered with RegisterComponentInHierarchy<WallNetView>() in GameScopeRegistration so its IProjectileFlightConfig injection resolves; the GameObject lives as a static child of the Scenario prefab (identity transform, so mesh world coordinates map straight through).

The net's motion and look now read two shared fields, not just one:

  • Disturbance — the material samples the shared _DisturbanceTex in the vertex stage (the BushLeaf precedent): the projectile's existing wall-contact stamp unfurls the strip from a thin resting line to its full depth and billows it along the impact direction, and the same disturbance amount shifts the net's colour toward a "struck" gradient and pushes its visibility toward _DisturbVisibilityTarget — no wall-hit event, no CPU sim.
  • Cloud field — the net's baseline visibility and idle breathing both read BackgroundFieldService's shared field via BackgroundField.cginc: it fades in only where there's cloud (so it reads as part of the clouds rather than a separate frame) and its idle undulation is phase-warped by the cloud noise instead of a rigid marching sine.

WallNetView also drives a _TransitionFade on the material, independent of the fields above: the net starts hidden, fades in on the first shot (ProjectileFiredMessage), and fades back out on a Game transition (ScoreLevelUpMessage, RunResetMessage) until the next run's first shot.

See Assets/Source/Shared/Disturbance/README.md for the disturbance field.

Shader

Assets/Shaders/BalloonParty/Scenario/WallNet.shader — the shared world-space net material: a procedural tennis-net pattern (grid lines from UV1, feathered band edges, HDR-emissive tint so bloom carries the glow) driven by the disturbance-field unfurl/billow/colour-gradient, the cloud-field visibility/breathing, and the scene-light tint described above. Shader/visual changes are validated in-editor, not by dotnet build.

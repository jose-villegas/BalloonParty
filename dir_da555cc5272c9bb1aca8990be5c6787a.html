MushroomChainRule / MushroomChainState / MushroomChainRules The meadow mushrooms' chain (Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md): a ball that bounces from one wall of mushroom caps to another enough times in one flight, with no wall of the board in between, is paid the Snipe lance. MushroomChainState is one flight's tally: the count, every bounce since the last wall (Bounces, the ones that did not count included) and the wall of caps hit last; its default value is a flight that has hit nothing. OnBounce counts a hit, told the wall of the cap that was hit (BumperCircle.Wall), and answers MushroomReward.None or Arm (the count reached ChainTarget, 3); OnWall starts the count and the bounces again. The rule is only asked about a ball that is not a lance. A lance, however it was armed, bounces off no mushroom: it flies straight on and cuts every cap its path enters (IBumperField.Cut), which counts nothing and breaks nothing. So nothing is paid past the bounce that arms, and no charge is banked (MushroomReward.Bank is never answered; the member stays only until the mushroom's view and its tests, which still name it, drop it). The armings are not capped: a lance spent at a wall is an ordinary ball again, the wall has cleared its chain, and a new chain arms it again. A wall is one hit: the wall hit last does not count again until another has been hit, whichever of its caps the ball meets, so a rattle along one wall is worth one hit and two walls allow three. A balloon pop and a ricochet off a balloon leave the count alone, so the rule has no call for them. A stretch between two walls has at most MaxBouncesPerStretch bounces (12). A mushroom bounce spends no shield, so without the cap a ball could be kept for good (a ricochet off an unbreakable can send it back to the mushroom it just left, a hit that does not count and is kicked again). Past the cap OnBounce answers PassThrough and changes nothing: the caller must not turn the ball and must not ask the bounce rule, and the ball flies through every mushroom until it meets a wall. IsSpent is the same question without counting, for anything that looks ahead of the ball. A chain is paid by its third counted hit, so the cap never costs a reward. MushroomChainRules holds the numbers and the repeat-hit switch, authored on IMushroomSettings (Configuration/GridActors/). The projectile (ProjectileMotionResolver), the shot solver (ShotSimulator) and the aim line (PredictionTraceCalculator) all call it, in the same order
IBumperBounceRule / BumperCircle / BumperContact / TrueReflectRule / ForgivingKickRule Which way a ball that is not a lance leaves a permanent bumper (a mushroom): a lance leaves none, it cuts the cap and flies on. The game asks the one rule (Projectile/Controller/ProjectileMotionResolver), and the shot solver and the aim line are meant to ask it too. BumperCircle is a bumper (a mushroom's cap) as the ball's centre meets it: an id, a wall, a centre and a radius that already includes the ball's. No two caps share an id. The caps of one wall share its Wall, which is what the chain counts by and what the kick never bends toward; a cap built without a wall is a wall by itself. Caps of one wall may stand so near that their circles overlap; caps of two walls may not. BumperContact is the touch: the ball's centre, its incoming direction and the bumper. TrueReflectRule is the mirror reflection. ForgivingKickRule is the bounce with the forgiving kick: it reads IMushroomSettings on every call and asks BumperKick (next row); with ForgivingKick off it is the mirror reflection. Resolve takes every bumper in any order, always gives the same answer for the same arguments, and allocates nothing. What a caller owes it: MushroomChainRule.OnBounce first, then Resolve with that state, and no Resolve at all after a PassThrough; the contact is the exact point where the ball's centre enters the circle; and after the bounce the bumper just left is not tested again on the same step, since the ball starts on its circle
BumperKick / BumperKickTuning The forgiving kick's geometry, asked by ForgivingKickRule. From a contact it takes the mirror reflection and picks the cap of another wall whose bearing lies nearest it within ConeDegrees (20) and whose corridor is clear; a cap of the wall just hit is never picked, so inside one wall every bounce is the mirror reflection (none: the ball leaves along the reflection; a tie goes to the lower id). It then looks one cap ahead: the ball is landed on that target at the point from which a mirror reflection points at a follower's centre, so the target's own kick finds the follower dead ahead and repeats the step, and the rally carries itself. The follower is the nearest cap to the target that is not of the target's own wall (the lower id when two are as near), whatever the list's order: two walls that face each other are each other's nearest, so the ball is sent back across the gap, which is how two walls give three hits. The next nearest is tried when one does not fit. A contact on a mushroom that is not in the list is not kicked. An offset is where a ball lands on a mushroom, as its impact parameter over the contact radius (0 dead centre, 1 a graze); a kicked ball always lands between MinOffset and MaxOffset (0.15 to 0.9), never the centre and never a thin graze, and when no follower fits inside them the ball is landed as near to one as they allow. A ball landing at an offset turns by 180 degrees less twice its arcsine (TurnDegrees), so the two offsets are the range of turns a kicked ball can make (MinTurnDegrees 52, MaxTurnDegrees 163). The bounce that brings the count to the chain target (the last kick) lands at MinOffset, since the hit it aims at is not kicked on; past the target every bounce is a mirror reflection. TryFindLandingPoint brackets the landing point on the arc of the circle both ends see and halves that arc, so it finds the point whenever there is one, and it answers false, never a wrong point, when rounding leaves the result short of a true reflection (an end a hair off the circle). A kicked line must clearly leave the cap it starts on: within 0.06 degrees of the tangent it counts as blocked, and so does a line that starts inside another cap's circle (the caps of one wall overlap, and a ball in the notch between two of them bounces off the second at once). In the editor and in checked builds the kick asserts that the ids are unique and that no two contact circles of different walls overlap. Where the follower fits, the next kick needs no cone to speak of (the reflection points at the follower's centre); coming straight back leaves the reflection up to twice the arcsine of MinOffset (17 degrees) off the way back, so two walls want the cone wider than that
IBumperField / IMushroomRegrowth / IMushroomBlast The board's bumpers as a ball meets them, and the caps a lance has cut (Slots/Grid/MushroomBoard is both). IBumperField.Bumpers lists whole caps only, each with its id and its wall, so an index is not an id. Cut(index) takes a cap out of the list there and then (the same list, one shorter; a field that cannot cut the cap leaves it as it was, which the flight reads as nothing cut and stops sweeping for): from that moment nothing bounces off it, counts it, is kicked toward it or looks ahead to it, for the flight, the aim line and the shot solver alike, since all read the one list. Its slot stays taken and the caps left of its wall keep their wall. IMushroomRegrowth is the same board for whoever keeps the time a cap grows back by (Item/Snipe/MushroomRegrowController): how many caps are cut, in the order they were cut (one comparison while none is), CountBeat(caps) (the first so many cut caps are a beat nearer; the ones cut since the controller last looked are left out, so a beat that fell in a cap's own frame is never its first), IsDueAt, the circle and the slot of a cut cap, and Regrow, which puts it back with the id and the wall it had. A cut cap whose mushroom has left the board is forgotten, not regrown. The flight reads the field on every fixed step, so an implementation answers from a cache and allocates nothing. IMushroomBlast is what a bomb and a nuke tell as they go off, so that neither has to know what a mushroom is: Bomb(centre, bombSlot, isRainbow, killRadius) and BlowOff(slot, centre). Item/Bomb/MushroomBlast implements it over the field
MushroomBlastRule / MushroomBlastTuning The sway a bomb's blast gives the mushrooms whose caps it leaves on (Plans/PLAN-MeadowMushrooms.md, Blasts take caps off: the rule as built), as plain arithmetic. SwayReach(killRadius, capRadius, tuning): the kill radius times the sway's share, and the cap's own radius, since the blast reaches a cap as it reaches a balloon, when the two circles overlap; 0 when the share is 0. SwayStrength(distance, nearest, reach): 1 at nearest (the slots' spacing, where a cap is blown off instead) down a straight line to 0 at the reach. Away(blast, cap): the unit direction a blast pushes a cap, straight up when the two are one point. MushroomBlastTuning holds SwayShare (1.0 as shipped, never read as under 0), authored on IMushroomSettings.Blast. Which caps a bomb takes off is not here: it is the bomb's own rule (Item/Bomb/BombBlast.Pierces)
BumperSweep The swept test of a ball against the bumper circles: TryFindFirst gives the first circle a straight run enters and how far along, skipping the bumper the ball is leaving. A whole run is tested, so no speed carries a ball through a mushroom between two fixed steps, and the answer is the exact entry point. A run that starts inside a circle reports the entry behind it (a distance of zero or less). Asked by the flight (ProjectileMotionResolver) and by everything that looks ahead of the ball (ProjectileView), by the shot simulator and the aim line, and by the kick, to know a ball that stands inside the next cap of its wall
IShotOrigin Where shots come from and how big the ball is (IsAvailable, RestingPivot, ProjectileContactRadius), for board-side code that plans or resolves against a flight: the mushrooms' circles are sized by the ball's radius, and their layouts were baked for the resting pivot. RestingPivot is the point the cannon turns about once it has entered and is not recoiling, captured once and not read off the cannon as it stands: a layout is planned for the whole level. Nothing in the game reads RestingPivot any more: the walls are dealt from a catalogue baked ahead of time, and the baker reads the cannon's resting place off the scene and the prefab itself. Implemented by Thrower/ThrowerOriginProvider, which the thrower fills in when it starts
XorShift32 A small seeded generator of whole numbers, written out as integer arithmetic so the same seed gives the same sequence on every device and runtime. The mushroom layout shuffles with it

Messages

Messages are the signals that decouple systems from one another. A publisher fires a message; any number of subscribers react independently.

This table is a curated subset, not an index. It lists the gameplay-visible consumers of each message — the ones whose absence would break a mechanic — not every subscriber. Game/Telemetry's GameplayMetricsService and Game/Flight's FlightStatsService between them subscribe to roughly 21 of these messages without being named in a single row below; they read the game, they don't drive it, so listing them on every row would bury the consumers that do. Check the publisher's own file for the exhaustive subscriber list when it matters.

A blast: Item/Bomb/MushroomBlast, for each cap a bomb or a nuke blew off (Blasts take caps off: the rule as built). The cap has already left the bumper field when it is published, so a cap is announced once until it has grown back MushroomView (the mushroom at Slot loses its cap and shows its bare stem; the look of a blown cap is the view's to build), MushroomSoundRouter (a stroke at the cap, for a lance's cut only), MushroomSoundRouter again for the bank (the stroke a step higher with each cap the flight has cut, and the Snipe's cue once for its first charge), GameplayMetricsService (mushroom_caps_cut for a lance, mushroom_caps_blown for a blast, mushroom_charges_banked for a cut that banked) — carries the mushroom's Slot, the Cause (MushroomCutCause.Lance or Blast; the constructor without one is a lance's), Charges (decision K: every cap a flight cuts banks that shot a lance charge, and this is the flight's count of them with this cut, 1 for the first; above 0 says this cut banked and the number says which; it counts what the cuts earned, not what the shot still holds; 0 for a blast and for a cut no flight made, such as a staged one; the charge is already banked when the message is heard, so a view can flare the pieces or a heads-up can name the charge from it alone), and a Point and a unit Direction in world space that the cause gives their meaning. Nothing else hears it: the cap is whole and the rule is as it was — carries the mushroom's Slot, the unit Direction from the blast's centre to the mushroom, and a Strength above 0 and at most 1 (1 at the distance of the slots next to the bomb, a straight line down to 0 at the blast's reach; with the shipped share of 2.4 the board sends 0.78 and 0.71 to the second ring of slots, 0.52 and 0.41 to the third, and 0.27 down to 0.12 to the fourth)
MushroomRegrownMessage MushroomRegrowController, when a cap a lance cut or a blast blew off grows back: on the board's beat, the fifth after its cut, a beat later for each beat a ball is in its way. Every projectile message (ProjectileLoaded/Fired/Destroyed, ThrowerReady, StreakChanged (null for the shared streak), StreakGrew, ProjectileBuffGained, Doomed*, Cruise*, ShieldLost, PierceDischarged, BalloonDeflected, ForceDestroyProjectile) names the ball it is about (IProjectileModel). A subscriber keys its per-ball state off that ball instead of caching "the last loaded one"; see IProjectileRegistry in Projectile/README.md for when a ball counts as gone.

NudgeMessage lives in Nudge/ (not Messages/) — it is specific to the nudge system and carries NudgeType and NudgeOverride[] types from that namespace.

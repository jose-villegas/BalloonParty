Ripe Bubble Cluster

The ripe bubble cluster: a full cluster is a bomb with a fuse

On water, a bubble cluster that reaches the cap of 5 becomes ripe. It swells, and a fuse starts. Popped before the fuse runs out, its burst is the player's: a ripple knocks a hit off every neighbour. Left alone, its burst is the level's: it sprays foam, and each neighbouring balloon gets a one-bubble coat that must be popped first and washes the shot's colour. Planned 2026-11-18. Status: proposed. The decisions below are open; nothing is built.

Owner direction (2026-11-18)

"I'm trying to come up with an idea for the bubbles in water levels to make a mechanic after they reach the cap of 5 but I cannot think of any, water levels are kinda boring now, as the only pressure is a more compacted board from the bubbles."

Four ideas were put forward (a fused bomb, foam that spreads, a burst that shoves the board, a cluster that swallows the shot). The owner chose the first: "I like 1".

Standing rule that shapes it: difficulty comes from danger to the player, never from a scoring penalty.

Two things in the code as it stands get in the way and are fixed by the plan:

  • The solver does not see clusters as targets. ShotBoardGather.TryBuildTargetSnapshot skips actors that are not IHasScore, and BubbleClusterModel is not one. The playtest bot aims through the solver, so no water comparison can be trusted until this is fixed (phase 0). Found by reading; not yet run.
  • "How many hits until it pops" is read from HitsRemaining in four places (IsTough, the resolver's one-hit check, ShotBoardGather, ShotSimulator). A ripe cluster and a coated balloon both break that reading, so it moves behind one extension.

Ripening and the fuse

A cluster is ripe when its count reaches BubbleMergeMax: the existing cap is the threshold, with no second number. Ripening starts a fuse of FuseSeconds (6) on ISpawnClock.SecondsOnLevel, held as a deadline.

The fuse is in seconds on the level clock, not in spawn lines. Lines arrive anywhere from 0.55 s to 1.7 s apart, and their rate is multiplied by things the player cannot see (up to 8 times faster on a sparse board, 3 times faster in flight, slower in a breather). The level clock holds through exactly the moments a timed threat would be unfair: pause, the level-up cinematic, game over.

Popped in time: the ripple

A ripe cluster is fragile: any hit bursts the whole cluster. A cluster loses one bubble a hit, so without this the first hit on a ripe 5 would make it a 4 and the choice would be between two bad rules. If that hit defused it, the ripple could only come from a one-shot pop (a pierce, a bomb, a laser) and the reward would be out of reach. If it stayed ripe, the ripple would cost five hits and five streak breaks. Fragile makes the answer one accurate shot.

The burst sends one hit to each of the six neighbours through IHitDispatcher, so scoring, items and chains behave as for any other hit:

  • Damage 1, a new flag DamageFlags.Ripple for audio and telemetry (no rule reads it), no DirectHit: it scores at the live multiplier but cannot climb the streak or trigger pop-spawns, like an item's pop.
  • Reward balloons and balloons that deflect an ordinary hit (the unbreakable) are skipped.
  • A ripe neighbour bursts in turn, after ChainDelaySeconds (0.12) so a chain reads as one.
  • A ripple strips foam coats, so popping a ripe cluster cleans the foam round it.
  • A burst scores the bubbles the cluster held.

Fuse runs out: the foam

The cluster sprays foam and is spent: it drops to SpentBubbles (1) and stays, so the clump is still there to merge and ripen again. Each neighbouring colourable balloon takes a coat:

  • The first hit on a coated balloon removes the coat and passes through. The balloon is untouched.
  • That contact washes the shot's stolen colour, as a cluster does.
  • It scores nothing and does not touch the streak. The cost is the extra hit and the lost colour.
  • A piercing or beam hit pops the balloon, coat and all.
  • One coat at most. Unbreakables, reward balloons and other clusters take none: a coat that added a bubble to a cluster would ripen it and start the next fuse, foam breeding bombs.

Architecture

State sits on the models. One small controller beside BubbleMergeService makes the decisions, woken the same way. The merge service itself stays as it is: it has no state and no teardown, and the fuse has both.

New messages: BubbleRipenedMessage and RipeBubbleBurstMessage (ripple or foam, and how many neighbours it reached). A coat coming off needs none: it is already an ActorHitMessage with PassThrough.

Points that are easy to get wrong:

  • The ripple is never dispatched from inside a hit handler. ProjectileHitResolver reads the shot's streak straight after its own dispatch to grant shields; a ripple nested inside that dispatch would change it underneath. The service queues the burst when it hears the hit and drains the queue in its own Tick, with an index loop, so a chain is picked up without recursion. At most 12 clusters exist (MaxBubbles) and each pops once, so a chain is bounded.
  • The service holds models, not slots, and checks each tick that the model is still the one in its slot, as NukeFuse does, so a cluster the balancer moves keeps its fuse.
  • A ripe cluster is not anchored. An anchored actor can seal a pocket off from the spawn lines.
  • The wash is read before the hit is evaluated. After it, the coat is gone. One extension, WashesProjectileColor, answers for both a cluster and a coated balloon; the resolver and the solver's gather both use it. BalloonModel does not take the IWashesProjectileColor marker itself: the solver reads that marker as "resists paint".
  • A ripe cluster is not tough. IsTough treats more than one hit remaining as plough-through and discharge at the wall; a piercing shot must burst a ripe cluster on contact.

The coat: why it is state on the balloon

Option Verdict
A Coat count on BalloonModel, checked in its EvaluateHit Chosen. The balloon keeps its identity: slot, registry handle, item, rainbow paint, type counts. One override, one overlay on the prefab
A coat on every balloon model, through the base class Touches four models to coat actors that should take none
Swap the neighbour for a "coated" actor Loses the item, the paint and the pool counts
A service that intercepts hits outside the model Hits are evaluated inside the model by six or more callers; the check has to live there

Nothing is saved mid-level and models are built new for each spawn, so ripe state and coats need no reset or save work of their own.

Presentation

  • Ripe: the cluster shader's _SpriteScale for the swell (not the transform, which the wobble resets), _FloatSpeed for the faster float (with _TimeOffset rebased so the phase does not jump), a wobble punch once a second of fuse, and a tint toward a warning colour. No shader edit.
  • Ripple: a new stamp profile in the disturbance settings; the water ripples through the path it already has. A growing ring over it (GrowingDiscView is a possible base).
  • Foam: the bubble merge's hop (BubbleMergeView): pooled bubbles arcing to each coated neighbour.
  • Coat: a child renderer on the colourable balloon prefabs using the soap bubble material with one bubble, scaled to enclose the balloon. No new shader; prefab edits.
  • Audio: cues for ripen, burst and a coat coming off, in CombatSoundRouter.
  • A digit counting the fuse down, and foam drawn on the water's surface, are both shader edits. Neither is in the plan.

All of it needs a look in the Editor.

Settings

Setting Default Meaning
FuseSeconds 6 seconds of level clock from ripe to foam; 0 turns ripeness off
ChainDelaySeconds 0.12 wait before a ripe neighbour hit by a ripple bursts
RippleDamage 1 the hit each neighbour takes
SpentBubbles 1 what a cluster keeps once its fuse has run out
CoatLayers 1 the most coats a balloon holds
Look values swell scale, float speed-up, throb strength, warning tint

No water-only switch is needed: clusters spawn only on water, and the board clears on every level change.

Open decisions for the owner

Each has a recommendation; the plan above is written as if all are taken.

  1. A hit on a ripe cluster: it bursts whole (recommended), or it un-ripens, or it stays ripe and needs all 5 hits.
  2. The fuse's clock: seconds of level clock, 6 to start (recommended), or spawn lines.
  3. A cluster whose fuse ran out: spent to 1 bubble and stays (recommended), or leaves the board, or re-arms at 5. Leaving trades five hits of cluster for at most six one-hit coats, which is close to no danger at all. Re-arming makes a foam fountain.
  4. Foam on a neighbouring cluster: nothing (recommended), or one more bubble, which ripens clusters with foam and can run away.
  5. A ripple reaching a ripe neighbour: it bursts too (recommended).
  6. A hit on a coat: washes the colour and nothing else (recommended): no points, no streak break.
  7. Clusters arriving from the lines: at most one below the cap (recommended), so a cluster always ripens through a merge and none arrives ripe from level 61.
  8. A ripe burst's score: the 5 bubbles it held (recommended). Today a piercing pop of any cluster scores 1, less than chipping it down, which is a quirk worth fixing with it.

Tests

  • RipeBubbleRule: the ripe threshold; which neighbours a ripple hits and which take foam (rewards, deflectors, clusters, already coated); fuse left from the deadline.
  • BubbleClusterModel when ripe: ordinary, piercing and indirect damage all pop it; it scores what it held.
  • BalloonModel with a coat: an ordinary hit removes the coat and the balloon survives; piercing and beam pop it; indirect damage removes the coat.
  • RipeBubbleService: ripens at the cap; the fuse fires foam once; stops on level change, game over and reset; follows a moved cluster; a burst heard during a dispatch is not dispatched inside it; two ripe neighbours each burst once.
  • ProjectileHitResolver: a coated balloon washes the shot and does not count as a one-hit pop; a ripe cluster is not ploughed through.
  • Existing: BubbleClusterModelTests, ProjectileHitResolverTests, the solver's EffectBoardMirrorTests.

What to watch

A ripe cluster in the middle of a crowd cannot merge or move out of the way, so the fuse partly rewards how the board happens to lie. One-hit bursts and a 6 s fuse should keep that fair; the bot runs in phase 4 are where to check it.

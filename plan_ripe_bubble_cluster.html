Ripe Bubble Cluster

The ripe bubble cluster: a full cluster is a bomb with a fuse

On water, a bubble cluster that reaches the cap of 6 becomes ripe. It swells, and a fuse starts. Popped before the fuse runs out, its burst is the player's: a small bomb, whose ripple knocks a hit off every balloon within its radius. Left alone, its burst is the level's: it hands its bubbles out as foam and leaves. Each neighbouring balloon gets a one-bubble coat that must be popped first and washes the shot's colour; neighbouring clusters get the bubbles left after that. Planned 2026-11-18; the owner settled the open decisions the same day, and the player's burst was reworked into a blast after it was played on a phone (decisions 13 to 17). A later run on a phone turned into one explosion after another, so the number of clusters full at once is now limited and spaced out (decision 18), and merging itself was put on the board's beat, with the gap counted in beats (decision 19). Status: building.

Owner direction (2026-11-18)

"I'm trying to come up with an idea for the bubbles in water levels to make a mechanic after they reach the cap of 5 but I cannot think of any, water levels are kinda boring now, as the only pressure is a more compacted board from the bubbles."

Four ideas were put forward (a fused bomb, foam that spreads, a burst that shoves the board, a cluster that swallows the shot). The owner chose the first: "I like 1".

Standing rule that shapes it: difficulty comes from danger to the player, never from a scoring penalty.

On the two endings, once the first version was built: "Basically we could use the shader so when it ripples basically goes from 6 to 1 in sequence forming a big single bubble and then pops. But for the foam, it stays in warning color, and goes from 6 to zero, keeping the bubble division, giving its entries to anything closer, until it runs out." Its two questions were answered the same day (decisions 10 and 11).

On the player's burst, after playing it on a phone:

  • "the blast from the bubbles needs to be a lot stronger, even impactful with more particles maybe, and converting into one single bubble faster, it each bubble adding to the scale as a pulse out. the spinning is too slow compared to the shape of the spiral so it doesn't match"
  • "maybe we could increase its radius a bit"
  • "so it behaves like a bomb, just a smaller scale bomb"

This is about the hit ending only: the fuse-out ending, the foam's hand-out to the six neighbours, stays as it was. Decisions 13 to 17 record what was built from it.

On how many clusters are full at once, from a later run on a phone:

"we need to make a way to reduce the amount of full bubble clusters in higher spawn clocks and higher levels it converts into a chain of constant explosions, so it becomes full spam, this happened in my current play"

Decision 18 records what was built from it.

On hearing that rule, the owner asked for the merging itself to keep time with the rest of the board:

  • "Maybe in the spirit of other mechanics the bubbles could merge under syncopation"
  • "It's what we use for the unbreakable cycles"
  • "Or the snake movement of toughs everything has a rhythm"

Decision 19 records what was built from it.

Ripening and the fuse

A cluster is ripe when its count reaches BubbleMergeMax: the existing cap is the threshold, with no second number. Ripening starts a fuse of FuseSeconds (6) on ISpawnClock.SecondsOnLevel, held as a deadline.

The fuse is in seconds on the level clock, not in spawn lines. Lines arrive anywhere from 0.55 s to 1.7 s apart, and their rate is multiplied by things the player cannot see (up to 8 times faster on a sparse board, 3 times faster in flight, slower in a breather). The level clock holds through exactly the moments a timed threat would be unfair: pause, the level-up cinematic, game over.

Merging on the beat

Clusters merge on the board's rhythm (decision 19), the one the unbreakables keep: a beat is IUnbreakableFormationSettings.BeatSeconds (1.7 s of play), an unbreakable snake steps on the beat and half a beat in, and two beats make a cycle. The bubbles' off-beat falls MergeOffBeat (0.25) of a beat after each beat: a quarter beat, between the snake's two steps, so a merge never lands on a snake's step.

  • BubbleMergeService runs a merge pass only on an off-beat, and at most one on each. A pass still picks pairs that share no cluster (BubbleMergeRule.Choose), so a cluster takes part in one merge a beat, and a group gathers a step a beat where it gathered a step a frame.
  • Whatever could open room no longer sets a pass off at once. It marks that there is something to look at on the next off-beat: the grid changing, a hit on a cluster, a merge of the last pass, or a merge the room for full clusters held back. With nothing marked, an off-beat scans nothing.
  • The beat is BalloonBalancer's own clock, read through IBoardBeat.OffBeats(share): how many off-beats have passed in the run. A reader keeps no timer, only the count it last saw. The clock runs with no unbreakable and no snake on the board, so it ticks on every water level. A pause holds it. A doomed shot's last glide does not: the rhythm comes first there, as it does for the snake's step. A new run starts the count over, which a reader takes as a start and not as an off-beat.
  • Who gives to whom, how long an absorbed cluster takes to slide in (BubbleMergeSeconds) and the fuse, on the level clock, are as they were.

Room for full clusters

Not every cluster may be filled to the cap at any time (decision 18). RipeRoom answers what a cluster may be filled to right now, and everything that gives a cluster bubbles asks it: the merge pass (BubbleMergeService) and the foam's hand-out (RipeBubbleBurst).

  • It is BubbleMergeMax while fewer than MaxRipeClusters (2) clusters on the board are full, and RipenCooldownBeats (2, one cycle) of the bubbles' off-beats have passed since a ripe cluster last ended, by a hit or by its fuse running out. Otherwise it is BubbleMergeMax - 1: the cluster stops at 5 and waits there, unripe. The gap was 4 seconds of level clock when decision 18 was built; decision 19 put it on the beat, so it is over on an off-beat, exactly when the merge service looks. Two off-beats is between one beat and two of play, by where in the beat the cluster ended.
  • A full cluster is one holding BubbleMergeMax bubbles or more, ripe or about to ripen. Counting by bubbles, not by the ripe flag, covers the tick between a merge filling a cluster and the service ripening it.
  • The count runs through a pass. A merge pass counts the board's full clusters once as it starts and adds each cluster it fills, so two merges of one pass cannot both fill a cluster when there is room for one: the second stops a bubble short, or gives nothing. A foam's hand-out counts the same way, leaving out the foaming cluster itself, which is on its way off the board.
  • It limits what a cluster is given, never what it holds. A cluster already at the cap keeps its bubbles and ripens as before, whatever the count and the cooldown say.
  • Only a cluster at the cap is ripe, so six bubbles still always means it is about to blow.
  • A foam's hand-out comes at the moment its cluster ends, so the cooldown that ending starts already holds it: with a cooldown above 0 a hand-out tops neighbouring clusters up to 5 and fills none, and the bubble it holds back pops as a leftover.
  • The off-beat count of the last ending is kept with the level it ended on. The beat runs on from one level into the next, but a level starts with no gap to wait out, whatever ended on the one before. A run reset clears it too, and a count that has fallen below the one kept (the beat started over before the reset reached the room) reads as no gap.
  • When the gap is over, nothing on the board changes. A merge pass that held a merge back marks it as something to look at, so the service scans again on each off-beat until the merge is made: a cluster waiting at 5 beside a giver fills on the off-beat the gap ends on, with no change on the board.
  • With ripeness off (FuseSeconds 0) nothing is limited.

Popped in time: the ripple

A ripe cluster is fragile: any hit bursts the whole cluster. A cluster loses one bubble a hit, so without this the first hit on a ripe 5 would make it a 4 and the choice would be between two bad rules. If that hit defused it, the ripple could only come from a one-shot pop (a pierce, a bomb, a laser) and the reward would be out of reach. If it stayed ripe, the ripple would cost five hits and five streak breaks. Fragile makes the answer one accurate shot.

The burst is a small bomb (decision 13; this section first described a ripple into the six neighbours, which it replaced). It sends one hit to every balloon on a slot whose centre lies within RippleRadius (1.35 world units) of the burst's slot centre, through IHitDispatcher, so scoring, items and chains behave as for any other hit. Slots are 0.75 apart, so the radius takes the first ring at 0.75 and, of the next, the six slots at 1.30 but not the six at 1.50: 12 slots on an open board. (The bomb item's kill radius is 1.25, by collider overlap.) The reach is measured between slot centres, not by physics overlap and not from where a balloon is drawn on its way to a slot, so it is the same on every run and the shot solver repeats it exactly. One pure rule, RipeBubbleRule.RippleReaches, answers for the game and the solver.

  • The ripple lands BurstSeconds (0.22) of level clock after the hit that popped the cluster. The cluster pops and scores at the hit, and its view spends that time folding its six bubbles into one big bubble and popping it; the ripple goes out as that bubble pops.
  • The hits land column by column, then row by row: an order the solver can repeat.
  • The cluster left its slot when it popped. A balloon the board has moved into that slot before the ripple lands stands in the middle of the blast and is hit like any other.
  • Damage 1, a new flag DamageFlags.Ripple, no DirectHit. The ripple lands after the shot has flown on, so a ripple hit scores at the shot's streak as it stands and never changes it: it cannot climb it, break it, or restart it on the neighbour's colour. Without DirectHit it triggers no pop-spawn either.
  • Reward balloons and balloons that deflect an ordinary hit (the unbreakable) are skipped.
  • A ripe cluster inside the radius bursts in turn, BurstSeconds after the ripple popped it, folding first as the first one did, and ripples from its own slot, so a chain reads as a run of blasts.
  • The wait is on the level clock, so a pause holds it. A burst still waiting when the level completes, the game ends or the run resets is dropped with the fuses: the cluster has already popped and scored, and only its ripple is lost. The pop that completes a level therefore ripples nothing.
  • A ripple strips foam coats, so popping a ripe cluster cleans the foam round it.
  • A burst scores the bubbles the cluster held.

Fuse runs out: the foam

The cluster hands its bubbles out and leaves. RipeBubbleBurst.PlanFoam decides where each bubble goes (RipeBubbleRule.PlanHandout), in this order:

  1. A coat to each neighbouring plain balloon (ICoatable: the plain balloon and its silver, gold and rainbow skins, item carriers included), up to CoatLayers.
  2. Then one bubble at a time to neighbouring clusters with room below BubbleMergeMax: one per cluster a round, in HexCoordinates.HexNeighborIndices order, until the bubbles or the room run out. The bubble that would fill a cluster to the cap is given only while the board has room for another full cluster (see Room for full clusters); with the shipped cooldown it never is, so a hand-out tops clusters up to 5. A cluster a hand-out does fill (a cooldown of 0) ripens on the next tick and starts its own fuse.
  3. Any bubbles still left pop harmlessly.

RipeBubbleBurst.HandOut applies the plan. It only sets state: it dispatches no hit. The cluster then leaves the board through IBalloonPopper.TryLeave: its slot is freed at once, with no pop, no hit and no score, while its view stays for FoamLeaveSeconds to hand the bubbles out. It stays ripe to the end, so it keeps its warning colour. Bubbles are only moved or lost, never made, so the board's bubbles only fall.

  • The first hit on a coated balloon removes the coat and passes through. The balloon is untouched, and an item it carries stays with it: an item goes off only when its balloon pops.
  • That contact washes the shot's stolen colour, as a cluster does.
  • It scores nothing and does not touch the streak. The cost is the extra hit and the lost colour.
  • A piercing or beam hit pops the balloon, coat and all. That contact does not wash: the coat went with the balloon, so the shot steals the balloon's colour as from any pop.
  • One coat at most (CoatLayers, 1). Toughs, unbreakables, reward balloons and grid actors take nothing. Clusters take bubbles, never a coat.

Architecture

State sits on the models. One small controller beside BubbleMergeService makes the decisions, woken the same way. The merge service keeps the merging: it has no fuse and no teardown, and ripeness has both. Since decision 18 it asks RipeRoom what it may fill a cluster to, as the foam's hand-out does.

New messages: BubbleRipenedMessage, RipeFuseBeatMessage (each fast throb near the end of a fuse) and RipeBubbleBurstMessage, built by Ripple or Foam (decision 14). A ripple's carries its centre, its radius and how many hits landed, which is what the view and telemetry need for a blast. A foam's carries which neighbours it coated, one bit per direction in the order HexCoordinates.HexNeighborIndices lists them, and their count. A foam's message also carries its plan in one int, four bits a bubble in the order they leave (a coat or a cluster and its direction, or a leftover) and the count above them, so it allocates nothing. A coat coming off needs none: it is already an ActorHitMessage with PassThrough, which ActorHitMessageExtensions.TookCoat tells apart from a resist.

Points that are easy to get wrong:

  • The ripple is never dispatched from inside a hit handler. ProjectileHitResolver reads the shot's streak straight after its own dispatch to grant shields; a ripple nested inside that dispatch would change it underneath. The service queues the burst when it hears the hit and drains the queue in its own Tick, with an index loop, so a chain is picked up without recursion. At most 12 clusters exist (MaxBubbles) and each pops once, so a chain is bounded.
  • The service holds models, not slots, and checks each tick that the model is still the one in its slot, as NukeFuse does, so a cluster the balancer moves keeps its fuse.
  • A ripe cluster is not anchored. An anchored actor can seal a pocket off from the spawn lines.
  • The wash is read before the hit is evaluated. After it, the coat is gone. One extension, WashesProjectileColor, answers for both a cluster and a coated balloon; the resolver uses it. The solver does not: a coat can come off mid-flight, so its gather keeps the coat count (ICoatable) and the marker apart, and decides the wash at each simulated contact. BalloonModel does not take the IWashesProjectileColor marker itself: the solver reads that marker as "resists paint".
  • A ripe cluster is not tough, nor is a coated balloon. IsTough treats more than one hit remaining as plough-through and discharge at the wall; a piercing shot must burst a ripe cluster, and pop a coated balloon, on contact. So HitsToPop counts the coat (a coated one-hit balloon takes two ordinary hits, and is no clean sweep) and IsTough does not.
  • Only a pop releases an item. ItemActivator used to fire on any hit on a carrier, which was the same thing while a plain balloon could not survive a hit. A coat makes it survive one.
  • The fold is a prelude, not a cluster case in the controller. BalloonController asks its view for a pop prelude; with none, the pop is presented at once as before. With one, the balloon leaves the grid and the registry at once, and the pop is presented when the prelude calls back. The model popped at the hit, so the rules and the score never wait on the view.
  • The foam's message goes out before the models change, so the burst view can hold what each taker shows (the coat, the cluster's count) until its bubble lands; the cluster's view is still at its slot.
  • A foamed cluster leaves, it does not pop. TryLeave frees the slot with no hit, so nothing scores, nothing fires, and the merge service sees only a grid change.

The coat: why it is state on the balloon

Option Verdict
A Coat count on BalloonModel, checked in its EvaluateHit Chosen. The balloon keeps its identity: slot, registry handle, item, rainbow paint, type counts. One override, one overlay the view makes in code
A coat on every balloon model, through the base class Touches four models to coat actors that should take none
Swap the neighbour for a "coated" actor Loses the item, the paint and the pool counts
A service that intercepts hits outside the model Hits are evaluated inside the model by six or more callers; the check has to live there

Nothing is saved mid-level and models are built new for each spawn, so ripe state and coats need no reset or save work of their own.

Presentation

All of it is done from C# with the soap shader's existing properties; nothing here edits a shader.

  • Ripe (SoapBubbleClusterVariant): the cluster swells to SwellScale over SwellSeconds. The swell is a factor the variant keeps, so a merge's wobble or a throb punches from the swollen size and settles back to it. The float runs FloatSpeedup times faster, with _TimeOffset rebased (SoapBubbleShader.RebaseTimeOffset) so the bubbles do not jump. The fuse throbs once a second, then twice a second in its last ThrobFastSeconds (RipeBubbleRule.Throbs), each a punch of ThrobStrength. The cluster fills with WarnTint (RipeBubbleRule.WarnAmount): WarnStart of the way the moment it ripens, so the player sees at once that it has turned, then evenly to full as the fuse runs out. It spins with its spiral arm (decision 17): at the arm's own turn rate and the same way round (RipeBubbleRule.ClusterSpin), slow from the ripening and a little faster from the first fast throb, its angle rebased as the float's offset is. The fill is the soap shader's flush (_Flush, set per renderer), the one shader addition of this plan besides the sixth layout: a clear film can't carry a tint, so the renderer's own colour showed nothing. Binding another model or despawning puts scale, flush, spin and float speed back.
  • Spiral: a ripe cluster sheds soap bubbles from a point that turns round it (RipeBubbleBurstView), one every SpiralInterval; each flies straight out, growing, and fades at SpiralReach, so together they draw a spiral arm winding out of the cluster. The ripening and each beat of the fuse (RipeFuseBeatMessage, now sent for every throb with a Fast flag) feed the source a little longer than the wait for the next beat, so the arm is unbroken while the fuse runs and stops when the cluster bursts or leaves the board. The source follows the cluster's view, and its bubbles are placed from where the cluster is now, so the arm travels with a cluster the board moves. In the fuse's last seconds it sheds SpiralFastSpeedup times faster and turns RipeBubbleRule.FastTurnSpeedup (1.25) times faster, the cluster with it. The source turns RipeBubbleRule.SpiralTurnRate the way the cluster spins, which it reads from the cluster's variant (IRipeSpin), so the arm and the cluster go round as one. Owner, on the first version (a ring of five bubbles thrown on each throb): "the spiral doesn't read", with a picture of one arm of many bubbles growing outward; and "color shift early, so the user knows it's going to happen, the pulse a bit larger, so it reads as danger".
  • Fold (SoapBubbleClusterVariant, the cluster's pop prelude, decision 16): the hit on a ripe cluster steps its shown count down a bubble at a time, 6 to 1, over FoldShare (0.75) of BurstSeconds: 0.22 s in all, a step every 33 ms, keeping the flush. A scale makes up for each smaller layout (RipeBubbleRule.FoldScale), so the bubbles draw together into one big bubble about the cluster's outline, and each step adds an even share of FoldGrowth (0.4). Each count shown, the six at the hit included, also kicks the scale out by FoldPulse (0.08) and settles by the next step (RipeBubbleRule.FoldPulse): six pumps, each peaking higher than the last, ending on one bubble 1.4 times the cluster. It holds that size for the rest of BurstSeconds, then the pop plays as any balloon's does.
  • Ripple, a smaller bomb (decision 15): what BombItemHandler does, each piece at a smaller scale with its own setting. RipeBubbleBurst sends a shockwave nudge of the whole board from the burst before its hits (BlastNudgeOverrides: 1.2 at the centre, falling off with distance), stamps the disturbance field with a brief inward pull and then the BubbleRipple profile's outward shock (the profile raised to radius 2.4, strength 6, over 0.12 s; the pull is its radius times BlastImplosionRadiusScale and minus its strength times BlastImplosionStrengthScale, over BlastImplosionSeconds), and flashes a neutral light (SceneLightFieldService.Flash). RipeBubbleBurstView draws the explosion from its pool of loose bubbles: the ring, one soap bubble in the coat's material under the balloons, growing from about the folded bubble's size to the blast's radius and fading over RingSeconds, and BlastBubbles (14) bubbles filled with the warning colour, thrown outward over the balloons, each along its own spoke, hard at first and slowing to a stop between 0.7 and 1 of the radius, growing as it goes and fading at the end of its BlastBubbleSeconds. The foam's press on the water keeps the numbers it had, under a stamp of its own, BubbleFoam.
  • Foam: the cluster keeps its warning colour and empties a bubble at a time, every FoamStepSeconds (0.12), keeping the bubble division. Each bubble that leaves goes where the plan says: a coat hops to its balloon on the bubble merge's arc (SoapBubbleHop, timed by IBubbleMergeCueSettings) and the coat shows as it lands (BalloonView.HoldCoat until then); a cluster's bubble hops in as a merge's does, and the cluster's count, held until then, rises by one; a leftover drifts a little way out and puffs as a coat does. The cluster's view is hidden as its last bubble leaves.
  • Coat: a child renderer BalloonView makes in code the first time its balloon is coated, using the soap bubble material with one bubble, centred on the body and scaled to CoatScale times its radius, sorted over the balloon and its item. A hit that takes it off puffs it away: it swells to CoatPuffScale and fades over CoatPuffSeconds. Binding, a pop and despawning hide it at once.
  • Audio (CombatSoundRouter): every cue is the plain balloon pop, pitched and scaled, so it sits in the colour pops' key without moving the ambient one. Ripening is an octave up and quiet. Each fast throb is the disco wave's tick. The hit on a ripe cluster plays a fifth up at half volume in place of its pop, as the fold starts. The player's burst, a fourth down and loud, plays when the folded bubble pops and the ripple goes out, and the ripple's own pops play at their colours where the shot's ramp stands, without climbing it. Foam is an octave down and soft, once as the fuse runs out. A coat taken off is a light pop a fourth up, where any other pass-through or absorb plays the resist.
  • Telemetry (per level, GameplayMetricsService): clusters_ripened, ripple_bursts, ripple_neighbours_hit, fuses_run_out, coats_applied and coats_removed. A coat hit counts as a coat removed, not as an absorb. ripple_neighbours_hit counts every hit a blast lands, which can pass six in one burst; the counter keeps its name.
  • A digit counting the fuse down, and foam drawn on the water's surface, are both shader edits. Neither is in the plan.

All of it needs a look and a listen in the Editor.

Owner decisions (2026-11-18)

Eight decisions were put to the owner, each with a recommendation. The answer: "Go with your recommendations, build the ripe cluster".

  1. A hit on a ripe cluster: it bursts whole. (The other ways: it un-ripens, or it stays ripe and needs all 5 hits.)
  2. The fuse's clock: seconds of level clock, 6 to start, not spawn lines.
  3. (Replaced by decision 10.) A cluster whose fuse ran out: spent to 1 bubble and stays. Leaving the board would trade five hits of cluster for at most six one-hit coats, which is close to no danger at all; re-arming at the cap would make a foam fountain.
  4. (Replaced by decision 11.) Foam on a neighbouring cluster: nothing. One more bubble would ripen clusters with foam and could run away.
  5. A ripple reaching a ripe neighbour: it bursts too.
  6. A hit on a coat: washes the colour and nothing else: no points, no streak break.
  7. Clusters arriving from the lines: at most one below the cap, so a cluster always ripens through a merge and none arrives ripe from level 61.
  8. A ripe burst's score: the bubbles it held (6, with the cap below). Before this a piercing pop of any cluster scored 1, less than chipping it down.
  9. The cap is 6, not 5 (the same day, after seeing it staged): "Cap the bubbles to 6, it makes more sense as it basically covers all its neighbors". A full cluster is a ring of six, one bubble toward each neighbouring slot (the shader's sixth layout), so the ripple and the foam reach exactly what the cluster shows it touches. Clusters arrive from the lines with at most 5. (The ripple's half of this was replaced by decision 13: it now reaches past the six. The foam still goes to exactly those six.)

The endings were then reworked on the owner's direction (quoted at the top), with two questions put to the owner:

  1. May the cluster disappear after foaming? "Yes". It hands every bubble out and leaves the board with no hit and no score, in its warning colour to the end. This replaces decision 3.
  2. Where do its bubbles go? A suggestion was put: coats to the plain neighbours first, then one bubble at a time to neighbouring clusters up to the cap, then any left over pop harmlessly. The answer: "Go with your suggestion". This replaces decision 4. A cluster the foam fills ripens and starts its own fuse, but bubbles are only moved or lost, so the board's bubbles only fall.
  3. The ripple follows the direction as it stands: the ripe cluster folds 6 to 1 into one big bubble and pops, and the ripple goes out then, BurstSeconds after the hit, for a chained burst too.

The player's burst was then reworked on the owner's direction after playing it on a phone (quoted at the top): "so it behaves like a bomb, just a smaller scale bomb". The fuse-out ending was left as it was. These five were decided for the owner from that direction and built; their numbers are for a look in the Editor to settle.

  1. The blast is a radius, like a bomb's. "maybe we could increase its radius a bit". The ripple hits every actor it could hit before (RipeBubbleRule.RippleTakes is unchanged) whose slot centre lies within RippleRadius, 1.35 world units, of the burst's slot centre: the first ring and six slots of the second, 12 on an open board. Slot centres, not physics overlap, so it is deterministic and the shot solver mirrors it exactly: one pure rule, RipeBubbleRule.RippleReaches, is called by the game and the solver. Damage stays RippleDamage (1) with DamageFlags.Ripple, not piercing, the scoring and streak rules are unchanged, and a ripe cluster inside the radius bursts and ripples from its own centre. This replaces the six-neighbour ripple of the first plan and of decision 9.
  2. The message carries a blast, not neighbour bits: a ripple's RipeBubbleBurstMessage has the centre, the radius and how many hits landed, and allocates nothing. The foam's encoding (its coats by direction and its hand-out) is as it was. Telemetry counts what it counted, the hits that landed, and the sound router plays what it played.
  3. The blast's presentation is a smaller bomb. "the blast from the bubbles needs to be a lot stronger, even impactful with more particles maybe". The pieces of BombItemHandler, each at a smaller scale with its own setting: an outward shockwave nudge, an inward pull then a stronger outward shock on the disturbance field, a flash of light, and soap bubbles thrown outward from the view's pool, with the ring reaching the blast's radius. No new art, and nothing allocated per burst in the burst or its view (the light service's Flash makes its own light object each time, as it does for the bomb).
  4. The fold is faster, and each bubble pumps the scale out. "converting into one single bubble faster, it each bubble adding to the scale as a pulse out". BurstSeconds goes from 0.35 to 0.22, and each step of the fold adds to the cluster's scale as a quick outward pulse that settles, six pumps ending on one bubble bigger than before (1.4 times the cluster, from 1.1). The schedule is pure, in RipeBubbleRule (FoldStepSeconds, FoldCount, FoldScale, FoldPulse). FoldGrowth and FoldPulse became settings so they can be tuned without a build.
  5. The spin matches the spiral. "the spinning is too slow compared to the shape of the spiral so it doesn't match". While ripe, the cluster turns at the angular speed of the arm it sheds and the same way round, in the slow phase and the fast one: both read RipeBubbleRule.SpiralTurnRate, the cluster changes pace on the same throb the arm does, and the arm takes its direction from the cluster (IRipeSpin). SpinSpeedup, a multiplier on the idle drift, was removed.

Full clusters were then limited, on the owner's report from a later run on a phone (quoted at the top): on a fast spawn clock at high levels they "convert into a chain of constant explosions". This one was decided for the owner from that report and built; its two numbers are for play to settle.

  1. Few clusters are full at once, and they come with a gap. A merge or a foam's hand-out fills a cluster to the cap only while fewer than MaxRipeClusters (2) clusters on the board are full and RipenCooldownSeconds (4) of level clock have passed since a ripe cluster last ended, by a hit or by its fuse; otherwise it fills it to 5, where it waits (RipeRoom, RipeBubbleRule.FillLimit). The count runs through a merge pass and through a hand-out, so neither fills more clusters than there is room for. A cluster already at 6 is never reduced, and every cluster at 6 still ripens. When a cooldown ends, the merge service looks again if a merge was waiting on it. This changes decision 11: with the shipped cooldown a foam's hand-out no longer fills a neighbouring cluster, because the hand-out is the ending that starts the cooldown. Why this way. The spam came from how many clusters reach the cap, so the cap on filling is where to limit it. Limiting which full clusters ripen instead would leave clusters sitting at 6 without a fuse, and six bubbles would stop meaning "about to blow". Stopping at 5 keeps that reading whole and takes nothing away from the player: a waiting cluster is still worth 5 and fills as soon as there is room. The limit is on danger and noise, not on score (the standing rule). The cooldown is on the level clock for the reason the fuse is (decision 2): it holds through pause and the level-up, and unlike a count of spawn lines it does not shrink when the clock runs fast, which is exactly when the spam happened. (Amended by decision 19: the gap is counted in the bubbles' off-beats, RipenCooldownBeats, not in seconds of level clock, and nothing watches for its end, since the merge service looks on every off-beat while a merge waits.)

Merging was then put on the board's rhythm, on the owner's words after hearing decision 18 (quoted at the top): "the bubbles could merge under syncopation", as "the unbreakable cycles" and "the snake movement of toughs" do, since "everything has a rhythm". This one was decided for the owner from those words and built; its two numbers are for play to settle.

  1. Clusters merge on the bubbles' off-beat, and the gap is counted in beats. This amends decision 18.
    • The beat is the unbreakables' (BeatSeconds, 1.7 s), kept by BalloonBalancer and read through a small seam, IBoardBeat.OffBeats(share). The bubbles' off-beat is MergeOffBeat (0.25) of a beat after each beat.
    • BubbleMergeService runs one merge pass on each off-beat, and only then. A board change or a hit marks that there is something to look at, and the "look again next frame" chain is gone: a follow-up merge waits for the next off-beat, so a cluster takes part in one merge a beat. An off-beat with nothing to look at scans nothing.
    • The gap after a ripe cluster ends is RipenCooldownBeats (2, one cycle) off-beats, replacing RipenCooldownSeconds (4). RipeRoom counts off-beats since the ending; a new level and a run reset still clear it.
    • The watch the merge service kept on the gap's end is gone: a merge held back is something to look at, so it is tried on every off-beat until it is made.
    • Unchanged: the cap of MaxRipeClusters, who gives to whom, BubbleMergeSeconds, the fuse on the level clock, and a foam's hand-out topping neighbours up to 5 because the hand-out is itself the ending (decision 18). Why this way. A merge that ran the frame after any change had no rhythm and could not be read: a group of clusters gathered in a handful of frames. On the beat a merge is an event the player can expect and see, one step a beat, in time with what the snakes do. A quarter beat keeps it off both of a snake's steps, so the two never move in the same instant. Counting the gap on the same rhythm makes it end on an off-beat, which is the only moment a cluster can be filled anyway; a gap in seconds would end between two off-beats and be rounded up by an amount the settings do not show. The fuse stays in seconds: it is a threat the player times a shot against, not a step in the board's dance. What it costs. Clusters gather more slowly: a pair that used to merge at once now waits up to a beat, and a group of n takes about n beats. On a fast clock more small clusters stand on the board at once, which is the compacted board the owner called the only pressure water had.

What to watch

A ripe cluster in the middle of a crowd cannot merge or move out of the way, so the fuse partly rewards how the board happens to lie. One-hit bursts and a 6 s fuse should keep that fair; the bot runs in phase 4 are where to check it.

The blast now takes up to 12 slots where the ring took 6, and a chain reaches a ripe cluster two slots away. That makes a ripe pop a bigger reward and water levels easier wherever clusters ripen. The owner's balance target (difficulty picks up after level 10) has not been checked against it: the same bot runs, with and without ripeness, are where to see what the wider blast did.

The room for full clusters (decision 18) pulls the other way: fewer blasts, and a foam that no longer fills a neighbour. Its numbers (2 at once, 2 off-beats apart) are a first guess from one report, not measured. Watch a fast clock at high levels for whether the blasts now come one at a time with air between them, and a slow one for whether ripe clusters have become too rare to be a mechanic. A cluster waiting at 5 looks like any other 5: nothing tells the player it is being held, which is fine while the wait is short.

Merging on the beat (decision 19) slows how fast clusters gather: one step a beat, 1.7 s, where a group used to gather in a few frames. That alone means fewer clusters reach 6, on top of the room above, so the two together may be more than the report needed: if ripe clusters turn rare, raise MaxRipeClusters or drop RipenCooldownBeats before touching the beat. The beat is shared: BeatSeconds and SnakeStepsPerBeat are the unbreakables' settings, and changing them now moves the bubbles too.
